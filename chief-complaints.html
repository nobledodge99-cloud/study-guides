History-Taking Frameworks

OPQRST (characterizing any pain/symptom):

Onset — sudden vs. gradual
Provocation/Palliation — what makes it better/worse
Quality — sharp, dull, burning, crampy
Radiation — does it move anywhere
Severity — 1–10 scale
Timing — constant vs. intermittent, duration

SAMPLE (rapid history in acute/trauma settings):

Symptoms
Allergies
Medications
Past medical history
Last oral intake
Events leading up to presentation

AEIOU-TIPS (altered mental status differential): Alcohol, Epilepsy/Electrolytes, Insulin (hypo/hyperglycemia), Overdose/Oxygen (hypoxia), Uremia, Trauma/Temperature, Infection, Psychiatric/Poisoning, Stroke/Space-occupying lesion/Subarachnoid hemorrhage

Triage & Vital Sign Danger Zones

Vital SignConcerning ThresholdConsider
Heart rate<50 or >120 bpm (sustained)Arrhythmia, sepsis, shock, medication toxicity
Blood pressureSBP <90 or ≥180/120Shock/sepsis (low) vs. hypertensive emergency (high)
Respiratory rate<10 or >24/minRespiratory failure, sepsis, metabolic acidosis (compensatory tachypnea)
Oxygen saturation<94% on room airHypoxia — pneumonia, PE, CHF, COPD/asthma exacerbation
Temperature>38.3°C (101°F) or <36°C (96.8°F)Infection/sepsis (fever or hypothermia both concerning in sepsis)
Glucose<70 or >400 mg/dLHypoglycemia (check immediately in AMS) vs. DKA/HHS
qSOFA (quick sepsis screen — 2 of 3 suggests higher risk): Respiratory rate ≥22/min, Altered mentation (GCS <15), Systolic BP ≤100 mmHg

ER vs. Outpatient Disposition — General Guide

These are general patterns, not rigid rules — always individualize based on the full clinical picture, comorbidities, and social factors (ability to follow up, home support).

Send to ER / Emergent EvaluationReasonable for Outpatient / Primary Care Workup
Chest pain with cardiac risk factors or abnormal vitalsChest wall pain reproducible on palpation, normal vitals, low HEART score
Sudden severe ("thunderclap") headacheChronic recurrent headache matching a known pattern (migraine, tension)
Abdominal pain with peritoneal signs or hemodynamic instabilityMild, stable abdominal pain without alarm features
Syncope with abnormal ECG or exertional triggerClassic vasovagal syncope with clear trigger and normal exam/ECG
Focal neuro deficits, suspected stroke (within treatment window)Chronic stable paresthesias without acute change
Any red-flag back pain feature (saddle anesthesia, bowel/bladder dysfunction)Typical mechanical low back pain without neuro deficits
High suspicion for testicular torsion, ectopic pregnancy, or ovarian torsionStable vaginal discharge/dysuria without pregnancy or peritoneal signs

Classic Diagnostic Mimics & Pitfalls

PresentationCommonly Mistaken ForActually Consider
Anxiety/panic attack"Just anxiety"Always exclude cardiac (ACS, arrhythmia) and thyroid causes first, especially on a first presentation
Epigastric painGERD/gastritisInferior wall MI can present as epigastric pain/"indigestion" — get an ECG in at-risk patients
Shoulder tip painMusculoskeletal shoulder injuryReferred diaphragmatic irritation (ruptured ectopic, splenic injury, subphrenic abscess)
Back pain in a patient with fever/IV drug useSimple musculoskeletal strainSpinal epidural abscess — needs urgent MRI
Confusion in an elderly patient"Baseline dementia" or normal agingNew/acute confusion is delirium until proven otherwise — look for an underlying cause (infection, medication, metabolic)
Calf pain/swellingMuscle strainDVT — especially with risk factors (immobility, malignancy, recent surgery, OCP use)
"Worst headache" that has since resolvedMigraine, tension headacheSentinel bleed from an unruptured aneurysm — still needs SAH workup even if pain resolved
Vague weakness/fatigue in an elderly patient"Just old age"Can be the sole presenting symptom of MI, sepsis (occult UTI), or PE in the elderly — atypical presentations are the norm in this population

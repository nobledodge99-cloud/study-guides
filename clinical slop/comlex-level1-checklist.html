How coverage is decided

For each of the conditions the Blueprint lists, I searched the full text of all guides and cheat sheets on this site for the condition name plus common synonyms and abbreviations (for example, "DIC" is searched for alongside disseminated intravascular coagulopathy). Each condition lands in one of four buckets:

Dedicated section — the name appears in a heading or title of a non-pharmacology guide, or is mentioned eight or more times in one.
Covered — three or more mentions in a single guide, or the name appears in a heading of a drug guide.
Brief mention — named a few times, usually in passing (for example, as a cause or an association in a different topic). You have seen it, but you probably could not answer a vignette on it.
Not covered — no match anywhere.

Mentions inside drug guides count for less (a statin guide mentioning pancreatitis as an adverse effect does not make pancreatitis "covered").

Limits to keep in mind.

1. This is a keyword match, so it can miss a topic taught under an unusual name and can over-credit a term that is only named in passing. Every status badge links to the guides it found so you can check.

2. The Blueprint says these lists "may include, but are not limited to" the conditions shown, and it does not give depth. Level 1 leans heavily on basic science (about 60% of the exam is knowledge application, and about 75% of that is foundational biomedical science), so an item can be listed here and tested lightly.

3. Question banks and quizzes you have built are not counted, only the guides themselves. UWorld, Bootcamp, and ScholarRx are not counted either; tick the UWorld, Bootcamp, or ScholarRx box on a category and its not-covered and brief-mention conditions turn purple ("covered elsewhere") and stop counting as not covered.

4. Many gaps are simply topics your course has not reached yet (obstetrics, psychiatry, and gastrointestinal topics, for example). Look at presentations where you have already finished the matching block for the gaps that matter right now.

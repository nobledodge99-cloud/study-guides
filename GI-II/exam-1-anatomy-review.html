2. The Peritoneum and Organ Relationships

The abdominopelvic cavity extends from the thoracoabdominal diaphragm above to the pelvic diaphragm below. It contains the abdominal and pelvic viscera, the peritoneum and the peritoneal cavity.

RelationshipDefinitionExamples
Intraperitoneal (peritonealized)Completely covered with peritoneum; associated with a mesenteryThe entire abdominal gut tube except the secondarily retroperitoneal parts (stomach, jejunum, ileum, transverse and sigmoid colon)
Primary retroperitonealLies posterior to the peritoneum (usually covered only on its anterior surface). Non-gut tube organs: reproductive and urinary visceraAdrenal glands, kidney, ureter
Secondary retroperitonealPeritonealized during development, but its mesentery fused to the parietal peritoneum of the posterior body wall. Gut tube organs without a mesenteryDuodenum (most of it; the bulb and the very distal part remain peritonealized), pancreas, ascending colon, descending colon
Memory hookPrimary = never in the sac (non-gut organs). Secondary = started in the sac, then stuck to the back wall (gut organs that lost their mesentery).

Board high yield (flagged on the slides)Cystohepatic triangle (of Calot): location of the cystic artery. Splenic artery: courses through the lesser sac. Gastroduodenal artery: relationship to the duodenal bulb (it runs behind it, so a deep posterior ulcer can erode it).

Colic anastomosis and watershed

  • The marginal artery (of Drummond) is the anastomosis of all the colic arteries: ileocolic (ICA), right colic (RCA), middle colic (MCA), left colic (LCA) and sigmoid arteries (red on the slide).
  • It helps prevent necrosis of the bowel if one colic artery is occluded.
  • The watershed area is the junction of midgut (SMA) and hindgut (IMA) supply.

5. Venous Drainage: Portal Vein, Hepatic Veins and Portal-Caval Anastomoses

Hepatic portal vein

  • Forms posterior to the neck of the pancreas (superior mesenteric vein + splenic vein).
  • Travels through the hepatoduodenal ligament to reach the liver.
  • Supplies about 75% of the blood to the liver and carries absorbed nutrients and enzymes from the foregut, midgut and hindgut.
  • Tributaries: splenic vein, superior mesenteric vein (SMV; drains the midgut), inferior mesenteric vein (IMV; drains the hindgut; usually a tributary of the splenic vein), and other foregut branches that drain directly into the portal vein.
  • Hepatic veins drain the capillary beds of both the hepatic arteries and the portal veins; they are the last tributaries of the IVC before it passes through the diaphragm into the right atrium.

Splanchnic nerves

A splanchnic nerve is a preganglionic sympathetic fiber that does not synapse in the sympathetic chain but synapses distally in a prevertebral ganglion closer to the organs it innervates.

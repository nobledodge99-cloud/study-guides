GERD, Peptic Ulcer Disease & H. pylori (Dr. Waller)

When to reach for this section: the vignette is heartburn/regurgitation, an ulcer patient (NSAID, H. pylori, stress), a critically ill patient needing ulcer prophylaxis, a pregnant patient with reflux, a patient on clopidogrel needing acid suppression, or a bleeding/ulcer regimen question. Dr. Waller chose no prototype drugs: red-font drugs are the ones she spent the most time on, so know those cold and recognize the rest.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "heartburn," "regurgitation," "epigastric pain relieved by food," "NSAID use," "H. pylori positive," "30 minutes before breakfast," "C. difficile," "hypomagnesemia/B12," "clopidogrel," "gynecomastia," "black stool," "pregnancy," "ICU/stress ulcer," "bismuth," "14 days."

H. pylori Combination Regimens

Principle: no single drug works. PPI (± bismuth) plus two or more antibiotics for 10–14 days. PPIs add a minor antimicrobial effect and raise gastric pH (lowering antibiotic MICs). Recognize an appropriate regimen; you do not design one. Penicillin allergy rules out amoxicillin-containing regimens.

Treatment of Viral Hepatitis (Dr. Waller)

When to reach for this section: the vignette is chronic hepatitis B or C needing therapy, a patient asking which antiviral to use, a hepatitis C patient where regimen/genotype/duration or interactions matter, or a drug-adverse-effect question (bone loss, flu-like illness, hemolytic anemia). HBV is suppressed, not cured; HCV is cured (sustained virologic response, SVR).
High-yield stem words/phrases: "suppress HBV DNA," "lose HBeAg," "cannot be cured," "48 weeks subcutaneous weekly," "bone mineral density," "NS3/4A / NS5A / NS5B," "-previr / -asvir / -buvir," "8 or 12 weeks," "pangenotypic," "SVR12," "hemolytic anemia," "teratogenic," "HBV reactivation."

GERD and PUD: Types and Causes

Aggressive vs defensive factors: erosions and ulcers arise when aggressive factors (acid, pepsin, bile) overwhelm defensive factors (mucus and bicarbonate secretion, prostaglandins, mucosal blood flow).

ComplicationsChronic reflux injurySevere erosive esophagitis, stricture, Barrett’s metaplasiaBarrett’s carries a small but significant risk of adenocarcinoma. Barrett’s, strictures and anatomic abnormalities: individualized management.
Causes of refluxTransient lower esophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation; reduced LES tone; delayed gastric emptying; hormonal changes of pregnancy. Smoking lowers LES tone.—Lifestyle: weight loss if overweight/recent gain; no meals 2–3 h before bed; sleep on the left side; elevate head of bed; stop smoking; limit caffeine, coffee, chocolate, spicy, acidic and high-fat foods if they trigger symptoms.
Atypical, extraesophageal and alarm features—Atypical: dyspepsia, epigastric pain, nausea, bloating, belching. Extraesophageal: asthma, chronic cough, laryngitis. Alarm: bleeding, anemia, early satiety, unexplained weight loss, progressive dysphagia, recurrent vomiting, family history of GI cancer, prior esophagogastric malignancy.Alarm features take a patient out of the “test-and-treat” approach.
PUD: mucosal damage from pepsin and acid in the stomach or proximal duodenum. Over 90% of ulcers are due to H. pylori or NSAIDs.

H. pylori Treatment Regimens

Principle: no single drug works. PPI (± bismuth subsalicylate) plus two or more antibiotics for 10–14 days. PPIs add a minor antimicrobial effect and raise gastric pH, lowering antibiotic MICs.

Antibiotic adverse effects (significant in 5–20% of patients; mild effects very common): clarithromycin: GI upset, diarrhea, altered taste • amoxicillin: GI upset, headache, diarrhea • metronidazole: metallic taste, dyspepsia, alcohol intolerance • tetracycline: GI upset, photosensitivity. Standard PPI doses: lansoprazole 30 mg BID, omeprazole 20 mg BID, rabeprazole 20 mg BID, esomeprazole 20 mg BID; pantoprazole 40 mg BID is non-preferred (weaker acid inhibition). PPIs 30–60 min before meals; antibiotics with or shortly after meals. The first course gives the best chance of eradication; prior antibiotic exposure lowers success, so stress compliance and counsel on adverse effects.

Bucket 1

Oral Cavity & Salivary Glands

MechanismMouth = the entry point: plaque and infection, immune and reactive lesions, premalignant white/red patches, then squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) (mucosa) vs salivary tumors (glands). Ask: what is the stimulus (tobacco/alcohol vs human papillomavirus (HPV) vs immune) and what does the lesion do when scraped?
High yieldDoes it scrape off? Thrush yes; leukoplakia, hairy leukoplakia, hyperplastic candidiasis no. Erythroplakia ~90% dysplasia. Non-HPV SCC = oral cavity, tobacco/alcohol, field cancerization, poor prognosis; HPV SCC = oropharynx, p16+, basaloid, better prognosis. Salivary tumors: smaller gland = more likely malignant.

Dental, Inflammatory & Infectious Lesions

Know the organism / trigger / buzzword

Dental cariesStreptococcus mutans ferments sugar → acid → demineralization (enamel → dentin → pulp → abscess). Prevent with hygiene + fluoride.
Periodontal diseasePlaque → tartar → gingivitis → periodontitis (loose teeth). Systemic spread: infective endocarditis, brain abscess.
Aphthous ulcer (canker sore)Shallow ulcer, fibrinopurulent base, red halo; non-keratinized mucosa; not viral. Celiac, IBD, Behçet. Heals 7–10 days.
Herpes simplex virus 1 (HSV-1)Gingivostomatitis (age 2–4); recurs at the same site (latent in ganglia; herpes labialis). Tzanck: multinucleated keratinocytes + intranuclear inclusions.
Oral candidiasisPseudomembranous (thrush) scrapes off; erythematous = flat red; hyperplastic = white plaque that does not scrape; angular cheilitis. HIV, antibiotics, chemotherapy, diabetes.
Lichen planusWickham striae (white lines, inner cheek); age 50–60; associated with hepatitis C.
Pemphigus vulgarisIgG vs desmoglein 1 and 3 → acantholysis; oral erosions (more DSG3) vs skin blisters.
Reactive lesionsIrritation fibroma (bite line); pyogenic granuloma (vascular, pregnancy); peripheral ossifying fibroma; peripheral giant cell granuloma (multinucleated giant cells).

Premalignant Lesions & SCC

Red vs white vs HPV vs not

LeukoplakiaWhite, does not scrape; tobacco/alcohol; 5–25% dysplasia; hyperkeratosis ± dysplasia. Biopsy.
Hairy leukoplakiaLateral tongue; Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), immunosuppressed (80% HIV); benign.
ErythroplakiaRed, velvety; ~90% dysplasia; high transformation risk. Mixed = erythroleukoplakia.
Non-HPV SCCOral cavity (tongue, floor of mouth, lip). Tobacco + alcohol (synergistic), betel quid. TP53, RB mutations; ulcerated, indurated; keratinizing, keratin pearls; field cancerization; poor prognosis.
HPV-associated SCCOropharynx (tonsil, tongue base). HPV E6/E7; E7 binds RB → constitutive E2F → p16 overexpression; no precursor lesion; nonkeratinizing, basaloid; early cervical node metastasis; better prognosis.
Odontogenic lesionsDentigerous cyst (crown of unerupted tooth); radicular cyst (apex, most common); odontoma (most common tumor, tooth material); ameloblastoma (cystic epithelial tumor).

Salivary Glands

Rule: parotid 65–80% mostly benign; sublingual/minor 70–90% malignant

XerostomiaSjögren (anti-SSA, anti-SSB; dry eyes + mouth), head/neck radiation, medications (most common). Caries, candidiasis.
SialadenitisMucocele (lower lip, blue; floor of mouth = ranula); sialolithiasis (stone, unilateral, S. aureus); mumps (paramyxovirus; orchitis, pancreatitis, meningitis).
Pleomorphic adenomaBenign; most common salivary tumor, parotid. PLAG1 (or HMGA2). Triphasic (epithelial, myoepithelial, chondromyxoid stroma); pseudopods → recurrence; rare carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma (risk rises with duration).
Warthin tumorBenign; parotid; smoking, bilateral/multifocal; oncocytes + lymphoid stroma with cystic spaces.
Mucoepidermoid carcinomaMost common malignant; mucinous + epidermoid + intermediate cells; CRTC1::MAML2; more solid = higher grade.
Adenoid cystic carcinomaMinor glands (50%); MYB::NF1B; cribriform/tubular/solid; perineural invasion; late lung metastasis.

Clinical Oral Complaints (Riesberg)

Scheme: anatomic (congenital / neoplasm / iatrogenic) vs infectious vs systemic

Toothbrush testDoesn't brush → infection/caries. Brushes and painless → anatomic or insidious (cancer, premalignant). Hurts when brushing → full history. Elevate the tongue on exam.
CongenitalCleft = failed palatal fusion (1/700). Micrognathia → large-looking tongue, airway/feeding; osteotomy. Torus palatinus midline, women ~30; torus mandibularis lingual, near bicuspids.
Benign look-alikesPapilloma = HPV; fibroma = cheek biters; hemangioma = blue, blanches (not melanoma); amalgam tattoo = silver, biopsy to exclude melanoma.
PremalignantLeukoplakia white, ~20% to SCC; hairy leukoplakia = EBV in HIV; erythroplakia red, >50% to SCC → refer immediately.
Drug effectsXerostomia = antihistamines/anticholinergics; dysgeusia = clarithromycin, metronidazole; staining = tetracycline; overgrowth = phenytoin; thrush = corticosteroids; mucositis = chemotherapy; decay = amphetamines.
Poor oral health sequencePlaque (~20 min) → tartar → gingivitis/periodontitis → caries → abscess → osteomyelitis / Ludwig angina (airway).
Thrush vs leukoplakiaThrush acute, immune compromise/antibiotics/steroids, clears with nystatin. Leukoplakia chronic, persists.
Viral / benign oralCoxsackie (hand-foot-and-mouth): posterior palate petechiae + hands/feet. HSV labialis: tingling then grouped vesicles outside the mouth. Geographic tongue: benign, migratory, no treatment.
Systemic cluesCrohn: recurrent ulcers, lip swelling, angular cheilitis. GERD: enamel erosion. Sjögren: dry mouth, parotomegaly, anti-SS-A/B. HIV: Kaposi + candidiasis. B12: atrophic glossitis. Leukemia: bleeding gingival hyperplasia (CBC). Aphthous ≠ HSV.
Bucket 2

Esophagus

MechanismDysphagia has four roots: structural (atresia, webs, rings, diverticula), motility (achalasia), inflammation (esophagitis) and neoplasia. The central story is GERD → Barrett → adenocarcinoma vs carcinogen → squamous cell carcinoma.
High yieldZenker = proximal, most common. Achalasia = lost inhibitory myenteric neurons, bird-beak, Chagas. Plummer-Vinson = iron-deficiency anemia + web. Barrett = intestinal metaplasia with goblet cells. Adenocarcinoma = distal/GE junction; SCC = middle, global leader.

Structural & Motility

Barium swallow clues

Atresia / tracheoesophageal fistulaAtresia = disconnected lumen (regurgitation, feeding intolerance); fistula = esophagus–trachea connection (aspiration, choking).
DiverticulaAll three layers outpouch. Zenker (proximal, elderly, halitosis, regurgitation); midesophageal (traction, TB); epiphrenic (distal, hiatal hernia).
Hiatal herniaSliding (95%) → GERD; paraesophageal → strangulation, surgery.
Webs vs Schatzki ringWeb: upper, semicircumferential, Plummer-Vinson (iron-deficiency anemia, middle-aged women). Ring: lower, circumferential, hiatal hernia.
AchalasiaIncomplete lower esophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation + aperistalsis; loss of inhibitory myenteric neurons. Secondary: Chagas (Trypanosoma cruzi), malignancy. Bird-beak on barium. Botulinum toxin temporary; surgery curative. ↑ SCC risk.

Injury, Varices & Esophagitis

Compare by appearance + organism

Mallory-Weiss vs BoerhaaveMallory-Weiss = mucosal longitudinal tear (retching, alcohol; hematemesis, self-limited). Boerhaave = transmural rupture → mediastinitis, emergency.
Esophageal varicesPortal hypertension (cirrhosis) → collaterals → dilated submucosal veins; painless hematemesis; band ligation/sclerotherapy.
HSV esophagitisPunched-out ulcers; multinucleated, ground-glass cells; Cowdry A inclusions at the ulcer edge.
CMV esophagitisLinear well-demarcated ulcer; enlarged cells with nuclear and cytoplasmic inclusions.
Candida esophagitisPseudomembranous plaques; GMS stain black fungi.
Eosinophilic esophagitisAtopy, Th2; superficial intraepithelial eosinophils, basal zone hyperplasia, “feline” rings; dysphagia/food impaction; no Barrett link.
Reflux esophagitis (GERD)LES relaxation; fewer, less superficial eosinophils, elongated papillae; complications: ulcer, stricture, Barrett.

Barrett Esophagus & Neoplasia

Metaplasia → dysplasia → carcinoma

Barrett esophagusIntestinal metaplasia (columnar + goblet cells) replacing squamous; salmon-colored, Z line pushed up. No dysplasia → low-grade → high-grade (architectural distortion).
AdenocarcinomaDistal/GE junction; GERD → Barrett, obesity, tobacco (not alcohol); males, white; TP53, CDKN2A; glandular. Rising US incidence.
Squamous cell carcinomaMiddle esophagus; most common worldwide; tobacco, alcohol, hot tea, caustic injury, achalasia, radiation; keratin pearls; African American males.
Clinical (both)Progressive dysphagia, odynophagia, weight loss; late diagnosis; 5-year survival <20% (75–80% if localized).

Clinical Dysphagia & Esophageal Disorders (Kinder)

Solids only = mechanical; solids + liquids = motility

Dysphagia approachOropharyngeal = striated muscle/neurologic. Solids only: ring, web, eosinophilic esophagitis, stricture, carcinoma. Solids + liquids: achalasia, scleroderma, diffuse esophageal spasm.
Achalasia work-up/treatmentBarium (bird-beak) → EGD → manometry. Botulinum toxin, pneumatic dilation (5% perforation), myotomy, esophagectomy.
PseudoachalasiaChagas (T. cruzi, triatomine bug, megaesophagus); paraneoplastic (small cell lung cancer, ANNA-1); Lambert-Eaton = presynaptic voltage-gated Ca channel antibodies.
Diffuse esophageal spasmCorkscrew; ↓ nitric oxide; manometry confirms; calcium-channel blocker, nitrate, tricyclic, botulinum toxin + PPI for all.
Systemic dysmotilityScleroderma up to 90%, distal two thirds, GERD → PPI. Dermatomyositis: oropharynx/proximal. Crohn 1–2%: fistulas, mediastinal abscess.
Eosinophilic esophagitis (clinical)Asthma/allergy; children dyspepsia, adults solid dysphagia/impaction; rings/scalloped; IgE ↑ in 2/3; eosinophils on biopsy.
Schatzki ring / web / stricture / ZenkerRing: GE junction, dilation + PPI. Web: proximal, Plummer-Vinson. Stricture: biopsy to exclude cancer, repeated dilation + PPI. Zenker: foul breath, old food, surgery.
GERD alarm featuresAge >60 new onset, anorexia, GI bleed, dysphagia, iron deficiency anemia, odynophagia, weight loss, family GI cancer. 70% of non-cardiac chest pain. Nissen fundoplication.
Pill esophagitisAcid (vitamin C), alkaline (alendronate), hyperosmolar (KCl), direct toxicity (tetracycline); aortic arch, little water. CMV = owl-eye inclusions, immunocompromised.
BoerhaaveForceful vomiting → full-thickness lower-third rupture; subcutaneous emphysema, pneumomediastinum on CT; mediastinitis; 80% mortality.
Bucket 3

Stomach

MechanismDisease = imbalance of damaging forces (acid, pepsin, NSAIDs, alcohol, tobacco) vs defensive forces (mucin, bicarbonate, prostaglandins, blood flow, somatostatin). Location of injury (antrum vs body) decides acid output and therefore ulcer vs cancer risk.
High yieldH. pylori antrum → ↑ gastrin → ↑ acid → duodenal ulcer; pangastritis → atrophy → ↓ acid → cancer/MALToma. Autoimmune type A: body/fundus, achlorhydria, hypergastrinemia, B12 deficiency, type I NET. Diffuse cancer = CDH1, signet ring, linitis plastica.

Gastritis & Stress Ulcers

Cause → pattern → complication

Acute gastritis / NSAIDsNSAIDs inhibit COX-1 (mucus, bicarbonate, blood flow). Erosion = mucosa only; ulcer = beyond mucosa.
Curling ulcerBurns: ↓ blood volume → ↓ mucosal flow; multiple shallow ulcers (stomach/proximal duodenum).
Cushing ulcerCNS injury/↑ intracranial pressure → vagal ↑ acid; single deep ulcer; high perforation risk.
Reactive gastropathyNSAIDs/bile reflux; minimal inflammation, foveolar hyperplasia, corkscrew pits.
Autoimmune atrophic gastritisBody/fundus; anti-parietal cell (H+/K+-ATPase) and intrinsic factor; CD4+ T cells. ↓ HCl, ↑↑ gastrin; pernicious anemia, type I neuroendocrine tumor (NET), adenocarcinoma; low ulcer risk.
H. pylori gastritisGram-negative spiral; urease, flagella, adhesins, CagA/VacA; Warthin-Starry. Antral-predominant: ↑ gastrin → ↑ acid → PUD. Pangastritis: atrophy → intestinal metaplasia → adenocarcinoma, MALToma.

Peptic Ulcer Disease & Other Structural Disorders

Gastric vs duodenal; Ménétrier vs Zollinger-Ellison

Gastric ulcerOlder adults; normal/low acid; pain may worsen with food; occult bleeding; whole-stomach H. pylori + NSAIDs.
Duodenal ulcerYounger; ↑ acid; relieved by food, recurs 2–3 h later; H. pylori ~100%; overt bleeding, more complications.
Ulcer complicationsHemorrhage 15–20%, perforation 5%, gastric outlet obstruction 2%.
Pyloric stenosisMale:female 4:1, 3–6 weeks; projectile nonbilious vomiting, olive mass; Turner, trisomy 18.
BezoarPhyto-, tricho-, pharmaco-; teeth loss, diabetes, gastric surgery.
Ménétrier disease↑ TGF-α/EGFR → foveolar hyperplasia; low albumin, low acid; no ulcers.
Zollinger-EllisonGastrinoma → parietal hyperplasia, ↑ acid, multiple ulcers; sporadic (pancreas, >2 cm) or MEN1 (duodenum).
Gastric polypsHyperplastic (most common, gastritis); fundic gland (PPI use, APC/MUTYH); adenoma (dysplastic, highest cancer risk).

Gastric Neoplasia

Four tissue types: gland, lymphoid, neuroendocrine, mesenchymal

Intestinal-type adenocarcinomaMales >50; H. pylori/autoimmune → metaplasia → dysplasia; nitrites, salt, smoking; CIN, MSI, EBV subtypes; exophytic, ulcer with rolled borders; tubular/papillary.
Diffuse-type adenocarcinomaCDH1 (E-cadherin) loss; <50, hereditary; signet-ring cells; linitis plastica; worse prognosis.
Metastasis signsVirchow node (left supraclavicular), Krukenberg (ovary), Sister Mary Joseph (periumbilical).
MALToma / DLBCLH. pylori → B-cell lymphoma; NF-κB; CD19/CD20; early MALToma regresses with eradication; stomach = most common extranodal site.
Neuroendocrine tumorType I autoimmune gastritis; type II Zollinger-Ellison; type III sporadic; chromogranin/synaptophysin; carcinoid syndrome ~10%.
GISTInterstitial cells of Cajal; KIT (~75%), PDGFRA (~8%); KIT + DOG1; bleeding/anemia; risk by size and mitoses.

Clinical Gastric Disorders & Upper GI Bleed (Kinder)

Stabilize first, then EGD

H. pylori (clinical)EGD biopsy, stool antigen, urea breath test (off antacids 2 weeks). Triple therapy 10–14 days: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin. Gastric cancer + MALT.
Stress ulcer riskVentilation >48 h, trauma, burns, shock, sepsis, organ failure, platelets <50,000 → prophylaxis (enteral feeding, H2 blocker, PPI).
PUD (clinical)H. pylori (duodenal); NSAIDs/alcohol (gastric); smoking worsens; biopsy non-healing ulcers; sucralfate + PPI.
Upper GI bleedAbove ligament of Treitz: hematemesis/melena (hematochezia if brisk). Stabilize (Hb >7, IV PPI, FFP/platelets), octreotide for varices, then EGD banding/clipping.
Bleeding lesionsPortal hypertensive gastropathy (snake skin); Dieulafoy (congenital, tortuous vessel); hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (autosomal dominant); varices with cirrhosis.
Gastric tumor frequenciesAdenocarcinoma 95%, MALT 5%, leiomyosarcoma 1%; carcinoid rare, almost never syndrome. Risk: H. pylori, salt/nitrites, pernicious anemia, acanthosis nigricans.
Bucket 4

Liver: Failure, Hepatitis, Metabolic & Vascular

MechanismHepatocyte injury goes one of two ways: massive necrosis (acute failure) or chronic injury → stellate-cell activation → fibrosis → cirrhosis. Etiology buckets: viral, autoimmune, toxic/metabolic, inherited, circulatory.
High yieldAcetaminophen → NAPQI → centrilobular necrosis. Cirrhosis = bridging fibrosis + regenerative nodules. HBV is the only DNA hepatitis virus; HCV 80–90% chronic; HDV needs HBV. AST:ALT ~2:1 alcohol; hemochromatosis = cirrhosis + diabetes + skin pigmentation.

Liver Failure & Cirrhosis

Acute vs chronic (her table)

Acute liver failure<26 weeks; acetaminophen (~50% of US cases; CYP2E1 → NAPQI, glutathione depleted → centrilobular necrosis). Gross: shrunken, soft, yellow, not fibrotic. Confluent necrosis. Complications: encephalopathy, coagulopathy, hepatorenal syndrome.
Chronic liver failure / cirrhosisInjury → Kupffer/stellate cells, TGF-β → myofibroblasts → collagen → bridging necrosis/fibrosis, regenerative nodules. Portal hypertension, hepatopulmonary syndrome (V/Q mismatch, hypoxemia), HCC.
Portal hypertension causesPre: portal/splenic vein thrombosis. Intra: cirrhosis, schistosomiasis. Post: Budd-Chiari, right heart failure. Results: ascites, varices, caput medusae, splenomegaly.

Viral Hepatitis (A–E)

Genome · route · chronicity · HCC · vaccine

Hepatitis A (HAV)RNA, fecal-oral; acute only; IgM early, IgG later; vaccine. Fulminant 0.1–0.3%.
Hepatitis E (HEV)RNA, fecal-oral, zoonotic; pregnancy = 20% mortality; chronic only if immunocompromised; no US vaccine.
Hepatitis B (HBV)DNA; parenteral/perinatal (~90% of high-incidence cases); chronic 5–10%, higher with perinatal; direct + immune damage; HBsAg = ground-glass hepatocytes; cirrhosis, HCC, glomerulonephritis, polyarteritis nodosa.
Hepatitis C (HCV)RNA, parenteral; silent, 80–90% chronic (error-prone polymerase, antigenic variability); ⅓ of US HCC; mixed cryoglobulinemia; no vaccine.
Hepatitis D (HDV)Needs HBV. Co-infection (simultaneous; usually recovers) vs superinfection (on chronic HBV; ~80% chronic, worse).
Acute vs chronic morphologyAcute: lobular inflammation, ballooning, Councilman bodies. Chronic: portal inflammation, interface hepatitis, portal/bridging fibrosis.
OutcomesAcute · chronic · carrier (infectious, little injury) · fulminant failure (rare).

Autoimmune, Steatotic & Inherited

Pattern + stain

Autoimmune hepatitisWomen (~78%); interface hepatitis, plasma cells, emperipolesis, rosettes; ↑ IgG; type 1: ANA, ASMA (adults); type 2: anti-LKM-1, LC-1 (children).
Alcohol-associated diseaseADH uses NAD+ → ↑ NADH → ↓ β-oxidation; ↑ triglycerides; CYP2E1 reactive oxygen species. Steatosis → steatohepatitis (ballooned cells, Mallory-Denk bodies, chicken-wire fibrosis) → micronodular cirrhosis. AST > ALT (~2:1).
MASLD / MASHInsulin resistance, obesity, diabetes; same morphology; AST < ALT early; MASLD → MASH → cirrhosis → HCC.
HemochromatosisLow hepcidin (HFE, HAMP, TfR2, ferroportin; juvenile HJV); micronodular cirrhosis, diabetes, bronze skin; Prussian blue; 200× HCC risk; males 40–50.
Wilson diseaseATP7B (↓ ceruloplasmin, ↑ free copper); steatosis → cirrhosis; basal ganglia, Kayser-Fleischer rings.
α1-antitrypsin deficiencyPiZZ; misfolded protein in ER; PAS-positive globules; liver disease + emphysema.

Circulatory Disorders

Into · within · out of the liver

IntoHepatic artery compromise → infarct; portal vein obstruction.
WithinCirrhosis, sickle cell disease, eclampsia, malignancy.
OutBudd-Chiari (hepatic vein thrombosis, ascites, hemorrhagic necrosis); sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (chemotherapy/stem cell transplant); passive congestion (right heart failure, nutmeg liver).

Lab Pattern Recognition

Clinical lectures folded in

HepatocellularAST/ALT ↑↑↑ (>5–10× upper limit of normal); ALP modest. Viral, drug, ischemia, autoimmune.
CholestaticALP ↑↑ (5–10×), GGT ↑, bilirubin ↑; AST/ALT ~2×. Obstruction, PBC, PSC.
InfiltrativeCholestatic-like pattern: tumor, amyloid, granuloma.
Disease-specific testsAFP (HCC), ceruloplasmin (Wilson), ferritin/transferrin saturation (hemochromatosis), AMA (PBC), ANA/ASMA (autoimmune hepatitis), A1AT level.
Bucket 5

Bile: Bilirubin, Cholestasis, Ducts & Gallbladder

MechanismFollow the bile: heme → unconjugated bilirubin (albumin) → UGT1A1 conjugation → bile → gut (urobilinogen/stercobilin). A block anywhere gives jaundice; location of the block points to the disease.
High yieldUnconjugated = UGT1A1 problems (Gilbert, Crigler-Najjar, newborn). Conjugated = excretion/obstruction (Dubin-Johnson MRP2, Rotor, duct obstruction). PBC = small ducts, AMA, women; PSC = large ducts, onion-skin, UC, men, cholangiocarcinoma. Charcot triad = fever + jaundice + RUQ pain.

Bilirubin & Jaundice

Where is the defect?

Physiologic neonatal jaundiceLow UGT1A1 at birth; rises by 3–4 months; blue light phototherapy.
Crigler-Najjar 1 / 2UGT1A1 complete loss (kernicterus, fatal) / partial.
Gilbert syndromeUGT1A1 mild transient impairment; unconjugated.
Dubin-Johnson / RotorConjugated: MRP2 defect with dark liver / defective hepatic storage.
Large duct obstructionAdults: choledocholithiasis, pancreatic cancer. Children: biliary atresia, CF, choledochal cyst. Pericholangitis.
Biliary atresiaNeonatal cholestasis; bile plugs, bridging fibrosis, cirrhosis by 3–6 months.
Other cholestasisSepsis (ductal cholestasis), primary hepatolithiasis (East Asia), Alagille, neonatal hepatitis (giant cells).

Bile Duct Disease

Autoimmune cholangiopathies

Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)Small–medium intrahepatic ducts; middle-aged women; AMA vs PDC-E2; florid duct lesion; ↑ ALP, IgM, cholesterol; cirrhosis, HCC.
Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)Large intra- + extrahepatic ducts; men; ulcerative colitis; onion-skin fibrosis; “beads on a string”; MPO-ANCA; cholangiocarcinoma (~20%).
IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitisMulti-organ (pancreas); ↑ IgG4; lymphoplasmacytic.
Ascending cholangitisObstructed duct + infection; Charcot triad; abscess.

Gallstones & Gallbladder

Stone type → risk → complication

Cholesterol stonesSupersaturation, stasis, nucleation, mucus; pale yellow; radiolucent; risk: female, estrogen, middle age.
Pigment stonesUnconjugated bilirubin; hemolysis (sickle cell); brown (infection, radiolucent) vs black (sterile, radiopaque).
Gallstone complicationsCystic duct → cholecystitis; ampulla → pancreatitis; CBD → cholangitis.
Acute cholecystitisCalculous 90%; enlarged tense gallbladder; gangrene, empyema, perforation.
Chronic cholecystitisRokitansky-Aschoff sinuses; porcelain gallbladder; hyalinizing = cancer risk.
Bucket 6

Liver & Biliary Tumors

MechanismSort by cell of origin and behavior: non-neoplastic nodules → benign tumors → malignant tumors → metastases. Imaging and stains are the tiebreakers.
High yieldMost common benign = hemangioma. FNH = central stellate scar. Adenoma = OCP/anabolic steroids; hemorrhage; β-catenin exon 3 = highest HCC risk. HCC: HBV, cirrhosis, venous washout, AFP. Metastases are more common than primary liver cancer; Arginase-1 negative.

Non-neoplastic & Benign

Know FNH vs adenoma cold

Focal nodular hyperplasia (FNH)Abnormal blood flow → hyperplasia; women; central stellate scar, fibrotic septa, ductal reaction; glutamine synthetase map-like; inconsequential.
Nodular regenerative hyperplasiaMany small nodules, no fibrosis; non-cirrhotic portal hypertension; reticulin stain.
Cavernous hemangiomaMost common benign liver tumor; no malignant risk.
Hepatocellular adenomaYoung women on oral contraceptives; males on anabolic steroids; rupture/hemorrhage. HNF1α (steatosis, LFABP loss); β-catenin (CTNNB1 exon 3, nuclear β-catenin, highest HCC risk); inflammatory (gp130, SAA/CRP).

Malignant Tumors

Risk factors · drivers · imaging

HepatoblastomaMost common pediatric liver cancer (<3 years); FAP, Beckwith-Wiedemann; fetal/embryonal.
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)HBV, cirrhosis (80%), aflatoxin, vinyl chloride; TP53, β-catenin, TERT; vascular invasion; arterial enhancement + venous washout; ↑ AFP; lung metastasis.
Fibrolamellar carcinomaYoung adult, no cirrhosis; DNAJB1::PRKACA; oncocytic cells in collagen bundles; central scar (not FNH).
CholangiocarcinomaPSC, choledochal cysts, liver flukes; cholestasis; desmoplastic adenocarcinoma; perineural invasion.
Gallbladder carcinomaFemales; gallstones (95% but only 1–2% of stone patients); late; <10% 5-year survival.
Liver metastasisMore common than primary; colon/pancreas (portal), lung/breast (artery); multiple nodules, non-cirrhotic liver; Arginase-1 negative.
Bucket 7

Pancreas

MechanismExocrine pancreas disease = premature enzyme activation (pancreatitis), duct/secretion defects (cystic fibrosis, divisum), cysts, cancer. Zymogens → trypsin → autodigestion is the master mechanism.
High yieldGallstones and alcohol cause acute pancreatitis; cathepsin B / PRSS1 / SPINK1; fat necrosis with saponification → hypocalcemia. Chronic = recurrent acute → fibrosis (triad). PDAC: KRAS → p16 → TP53/SMAD4, head 60%, painless jaundice, Trousseau, migratory thrombophlebitis, CA 19-9 for prognosis.

Congenital & Cystic Fibrosis

Pancreas divisumMost common anomaly; dorsal/ventral ducts fail to fuse; ~5% pancreatitis.
Annular pancreasTissue encircles duodenum → obstruction.
Heterotopic pancreasRests, most often stomach.
Cystic fibrosisCFTR: viscous secretions block ducts; insufficiency (steatorrhea); most common cause of chronic pancreatitis in children.

Pancreatitis

Acute vs chronic

Acute pancreatitisGallstones, alcohol, drugs, hypertriglyceridemia, ischemia. ↑ intracellular Ca2+ → lysosome-zymogen fusion → cathepsin B → trypsin. Elastase, chymotrypsin, lipase (fat necrosis). Hereditary: PRSS1 GOF, SPINK1 LOF, CFTR.
Acute morphology / clinicalInterstitial (edema) vs necrotizing (hemorrhage, saponification). Epigastric pain to back; lipase > amylase; hypocalcemia; pseudocyst.
Chronic pancreatitisIrreversible; alcohol (adults); recurrent acute → TGF-β/PDGF → stellate cells; ductal dilation + fibrosis + acinar atrophy; calcification; insufficiency, diabetes, adenocarcinoma; enzymes normal.
Autoimmune pancreatitisType 1 = IgG4, multi-organ; type 2 = granulocytic epithelial lesion, pancreas only; type 3 = checkpoint inhibitor.

Cysts & Neoplasms

Gene + sex + site

True cyst vs pseudocystTrue: epithelium (polycystic kidney disease, von Hippel-Lindau). Pseudocyst: no epithelium, granulation tissue; after pancreatitis/trauma.
Serous cystic neoplasm (SCN)Elderly women; VHL; honeycomb, central scar; benign.
Mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN)Middle-aged women, tail; KRAS, RNF43; ovarian-type stroma (ER/PR); premalignant.
Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN)Head, duct communication; KRAS, GNAS, RNF43; premalignant.
Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm (SPN)Young women; CTNNB1; solid + hemorrhagic cystic; discohesive; low malignant potential.
Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)Smoking, chronic pancreatitis, age >60; PanIN → KRAS (~90%) → CDKN2A → TP53 + SMAD4; head 60% (painless jaundice, double-duct sign), body/tail silent; perineural invasion; Trousseau; new diabetes; CA 19-9 prognosis; Whipple, 85% unresectable.
Neuroendocrine tumors (pancreas)Gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison) can be pancreatic; MEN1.
Bucket 8

Immune-Mediated GI Disorders

MechanismMostly type IV hypersensitivity (Th1, Th17, cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTL)) with a barrier/tolerance failure; food allergy = type I (IgE). Match the distribution and the key clue.
High yieldCeliac: HLA-DQ2/DQ8, tTG deamidation, anti-tTG IgA, villous flattening. Crohn = skip lesions, transmural, granulomas; UC = continuous, mucosal, crypt abscesses. IgG4 lacks effector functions. IgA deficiency = most common primary immunodeficiency.

Disease Comparison

Nash's main points

Celiac diseaseGluten/gliadin; tTG deamidation (QXXF, QXPF); HLA-DQ2/DQ8 (necessary, not sufficient); CD4 Th1 + CD8/NK intraepithelial lymphocytes; anti-tTG IgA; biopsy definitive; IgG positive → check IgA deficiency.
Crohn diseaseAnywhere, terminal ileum, patchy, transmural, cobblestone, deep ulcers, granulomas, strictures; Th17/Th1.
Ulcerative colitisColon only, continuous, mucosal; shallow ulcers, pseudopolyps; crypt abscesses; rectal bleeding; Paneth defensin defect.
PSC / PBC / IgG4-SCPSC: intra + extrahepatic, beads on a string. PBC: intrahepatic only. IgG4: multi-organ, pancreas.
Food allergyType I: IgE on mast cells → hives, vomiting/diarrhea, anaphylaxis. Non-IgE: Th2/eosinophilic. Allergy ≠ intolerance.
Selective IgA deficiencyMost common primary immunodeficiency; usually asymptomatic; mucosal infections; false-negative anti-tTG IgA.

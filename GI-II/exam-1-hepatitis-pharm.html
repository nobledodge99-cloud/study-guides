Acute viral hepatitis and when to treat

  • Case definition: discrete onset of nausea, anorexia, fever, malaise or abdominal pain plus jaundice or elevated aminotransferases. Clinical features are the same for all types, so confirm with serology.
  • Untreated HBV and HCV lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis and HCC (high morbidity and mortality).
  • HBV treat if risk of liver-related morbidity/mortality is within the foreseeable future and the chance of response is high; some patients are best monitored (low chance of response that does not outweigh risks/cost). Treatment hinges on cirrhosis presence.
  • Non-drug HBV care: vaccinate sexual and household contacts, avoid alcohol, vaccinate against HAV; insufficient data for herbal remedies.
  • Notes packet: HBV progression to chronic depends on age: ~90% perinatal, 20-50% at ages 1-5, <5% adult-acquired. HBV vaccine is the first vaccine against a major human cancer (HCC).

2. Peginterferon Alfa (HBV and HCV)

Red drugPeginterferon alfa is first-line for HBV (48-52 week course) and an older HCV agent.
  • MOA: cytokine with antiviral, immunomodulatory and antiproliferative actions; inhibits viral penetration, translation, transcription, protein processing, maturation and release; enhances macrophage phagocytosis and cytotoxic T-cell proliferation/survival (also NK cells, MHC I upregulation).
  • Pegylation (polyethylene glycol): slows absorption, decreases clearance, longer half-life (~40 h vs 2-5 h), steady concentration → once-weekly dosing (vs daily or thrice weekly). Always subcutaneous; ~30% renal (adjust in renal impairment).
  • Use: chronic HBV and chronic HCV.

3. HBV Nucleoside/Nucleotide Analogs and Interferon vs NAs

HBV life cycle (slide 6) (click to enlarge)
  • HBV: partially dsDNA virus; virion enters hepatocyte; DNA repaired to cccDNA (a "minichromosome" template) → RNAs → proteins; pgRNA packaged into capsid and reverse transcribed to DNA. Replication is not directly toxic; the immune response to viral antigens drives injury. cccDNA can be recycled to the nucleus, maintaining a stable pool.
  • NAs block replication of the genome by inhibiting the DNA polymerase / reverse transcription. Renal elimination (dose adjust), long half-life, once daily.
  • General class warnings (notes): lactic acidosis, hepatic steatosis (mitochondrial dysfunction); severe acute exacerbation if stopped; resistance risk in HBV/HIV coinfection if HIV untreated.

4. Hepatitis C: Management, SVR and the DAA Targets

  • Acute HCV rarely causes fulminant hepatitis, but ~85% progress to chronic HCV; insidious course; single largest risk factor is injection drug use.
  • Diagnosis: reactive HCV antibody plus positive HCV RNA (PCR). Screen those with risk factors or born 1945-1965; test those with abnormal aminotransferases or other findings.
  • Goal: viral eradication (cure) = SVR: no HCV RNA by PCR 12 weeks after completing therapy.
  • SVR significance: improved liver histology, fewer HCC, sometimes cirrhosis regression; a standard clinical trial endpoint. (Notes: SVR does not confer immunity, reinfection possible; >90% SVR with new regimens in adherent, treatment-naive, compensated patients.)
  • Who to treat: all patients with chronic HCV except those with short life expectancy; prioritize advanced/compensated fibrosis, transplant recipients, extrahepatic manifestations, symptomatic disease with transmission potential.

HCV life cycle and where DAAs act

  • +ssRNA genome is translated into a polyprotein, cleaved by host and viral proteases into structural and nonstructural (NS) proteins. The replication complex is NS3, NS4A, NS4B, NS5A, NS5B.
  • NS3/4A = viral protease (post-translational processing). NS5A = organizes the replication complex, regulates replication and assembly. NS5B = RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, essential for replication.

5. Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) and Guideline Regimens

DAAs target nonstructural proteins and disrupt replication. Combination regimens for 8-12 weeks; extremely effective, good safety, high cost. Most common adverse effects: headache, fatigue, nausea, diarrhea.

6. Ribavirin and DAA Drug Interactions (FYI)

Ribavirin

  • Guanosine analog: interferes with GTP synthesis (inhibits capping of viral mRNA) and inhibits initiation and elongation of RNA, blocking viral protein synthesis.
  • Use: chronic HCV (in certain alternative regimens, with DAAs, to raise SVR); also influenza A/B, parainfluenza, RSV (aerosolized), paramyxoviruses, HIV-1. Oral for HCV.
  • ADRs: dose-dependent hemolytic anemia (10-20%), depression, fatigue, nausea, rash, insomnia.
  • Contraindications: anemia, end-stage renal failure, ischemic vascular disease, pregnancy (teratogenic, embryotoxic; pregnant women and their partners); avoid conception during and for 6 months after therapy (narration).

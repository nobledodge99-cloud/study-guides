1. Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) Physiology and Regulation

The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) controls blood pressure (BP), extracellular fluid volume and electrolytes. Acute role: vascular tone and sodium handling. Chronic role: cardiovascular remodeling and kidney function. Over-activation drives hypertension (HTN), heart failure, diabetic nephropathy and myocardial infarction (MI). Every step of the cascade is a drug target.

StepWhat happensDrug that acts here
1. Renin releaseJuxtaglomerular (JG) cells secrete renin; it cleaves liver-made angiotensinogen into angiotensin I (Ang I, a minimally active decapeptide)Aliskiren (direct renin inhibitor); beta-blockers reduce renin release
2. Ang I → Ang IIAngiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), on vascular endothelium (lungs, kidney), makes angiotensin II (Ang II) and degrades bradykinin (a vasodilator). Chymase can also make Ang II in heart and kidneyACE inhibitors (ACEIs): lisinopril
3. Ang II acts on the AT₁ receptorAngiotensin II type 1 (AT₁) receptors on vascular smooth muscle, kidney, adrenal cortex, heart, brain: ↑ vasoconstriction, ↑ Na⁺/water reabsorption (proximal tubule), ↑ aldosterone synthesis, ↑ sympathetic neurotransmission, ↓ Na⁺ excretion → ↑ BPAngiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs): losartan; sacubitril/valsartan (valsartan part)
4. AldosteroneMade in the adrenal zona glomerulosa; acts on mineralocorticoid receptors (MRs) in the distal nephron: ↑ epithelial sodium channels (ENaC) and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → Na⁺ retention, K⁺ excretion, volume expansionMineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs): spironolactone
Slide: renin release. Stimuli (red arrows): low renal arterial pressure, low NaCl delivery to the macula densa, and renal sympathetic activity acting on beta-1 receptors of the JG cell.

What stimulates renin release

  • ↓ renal perfusion pressure; ↓ NaCl delivery to the macula densa; ↑ sympathetic activity (through beta-1 receptors).

Feedback on renin and the drugs that change it

LoopMechanismClinical link
Short loopAng II inhibits renin release directly via AT₁ receptors on JG cellsACEIs/ARBs remove this brake, so plasma renin activity rises
Long loopHigher BP lowers renal baroreceptor activation and sympathetic drive, suppressing renin
Macula densa↑ NaCl delivery inhibits renin secretion
DrugsBeta-blockers and NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs; less prostaglandin stimulation) decrease renin; loop diuretics and vasodilators increase renin (lower perfusion pressure or Na⁺ delivery)Explains why diuretic-treated and heart-failure patients have high renin and drop BP sharply with the first ACEI dose

Counter-regulatory axis

  • ACE2 converts Ang II to Ang-(1–7), which acts on the Mas receptor: vasodilation, natriuresis, antifibrotic effect. It balances the ACE–Ang II–AT₁ axis. No widely used drug targets it yet.

3. Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEIs): Lisinopril

CORE Lisinopril is the representative ACEI. Others: enalapril, captopril, benazepril, ramipril, fosinopril, perindopril, trandolapril, enalaprilat class-level.

Slide: ACE inhibition lowers Ang II and prevents bradykinin breakdown (bradykinin rises: vasodilation, but also cough and angioedema).
Lisinopril (ACEI)
MechanismInhibits ACE → ↓ Ang II (and ↑ bradykinin)
EffectsVasodilation; ↓ aldosterone, Na⁺/water retention; efferent arteriole dilation → ↓ intraglomerular pressure → ↓ proteinuria; natriuresis; ↓ preload and afterload (↑ cardiac output in HFrEF); no reflex tachycardia (baroreflex resetting); renin rises (loss of Ang II feedback)
UsesHTN first line (especially diabetes, chronic kidney disease (CKD), high-renin states). Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF): fewer symptoms and hospitalizations, longer survival (with beta-blocker and MRA). Post-MI: lower mortality, limit remodeling, prevent heart failure. CKD/diabetic nephropathy: ↓ proteinuria, slows progression (benefit even in normotensive diabetics)
PharmacokineticsMost ACEIs are prodrugs (hepatic activation) but lisinopril and captopril are active as given. Renal elimination, so adjust dose in CKD (fosinopril and trandolapril have mixed hepatic/renal clearance). Oral, except enalaprilat (intravenous (IV), the active form of enalapril) for hypertensive emergency or acute decompensated heart failure. High renin (heart failure, on diuretics): start low, titrate slowly. Largely interchangeable
Adverse effectsHypotension (first dose, if volume depleted or high renin); dry cough (up to 20%, bradykinin; starts weeks in, resolves within days of stopping; switch to an ARB); angioedema (rare, bradykinin, more common in people of African descent); hyperkalemia; acute kidney injury (AKI); transient creatinine rise
ContraindicationsPregnancy (teratogenic, 2nd/3rd trimester); bilateral renal artery stenosis (or severe stenosis in a solitary kidney); prior ACEI angioedema (absolute)
InteractionsK⁺-sparing diuretics and K⁺ supplements → hyperkalemia; NSAIDs blunt BP effect and raise AKI risk (block prostaglandin afferent dilation); no dual RAAS blockade (ACEI + ARB or aliskiren)
Creatinine ruleA rise in serum creatinine of up to ~30% after starting is expected (lower intraglomerular pressure) and acceptable. A larger or progressive rise suggests a problem such as renal artery stenosis; reassess and adjust.
Why AKI happensAng II normally constricts the efferent arteriole to hold filtration pressure. With ACEI, efferent tone drops; if the afferent supply is already low (bilateral renal artery stenosis, volume depletion, NSAID), filtration pressure collapses.

4. Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARBs): Losartan

CORE Losartan is the representative ARB. Others: valsartan, candesartan, irbesartan, olmesartan, telmisartan, azilsartan class-level.

Slide: ARBs block the AT₁ receptor; bradykinin is untouched, so no cough; AT₂ stays unblocked.
Losartan (ARB)
MechanismSelective, competitive AT₁ receptor antagonist (>10,000-fold selective over AT₂; functionally insurmountable blockade). Does not affect bradykinin. May suppress Ang II action more completely because non-ACE pathways make Ang II too; unopposed AT₂ may add vasodilation and antifibrotic effects
EffectsBlocks vasoconstriction, aldosterone release, Na⁺ retention, sympathetic activation; natriuresis; ↓ preload and afterload; better remodeling in HFrEF; efferent dilation → ↓ proteinuria
UsesHTN, HFrEF, post-MI with left ventricular dysfunction, CKD with albuminuria/diabetic nephropathy. Preferred when ACEI is not tolerated (cough, angioedema). Losartan special uses: Marfan syndrome (blocks transforming growth factor beta (TGF-beta) signaling, slows aortic root dilation) and post-transplant erythrocytosis. Valsartan is the ARB part of sacubitril/valsartan
PharmacokineticsOral, highly protein bound, hepatic metabolism, biliary excretion; modest differences, largely interchangeable
Adverse effectsHypotension, hyperkalemia, AKI. Much lower risk of cough and angioedema (rare cases still occur)
ContraindicationsPregnancy; bilateral renal artery stenosis; prior ARB angioedema
InteractionsK⁺-sparing diuretics, K⁺ supplements, NSAIDs; avoid dual RAAS blockade (with ACEI or aliskiren)
ACEI vs ARB in one lineSame BP, heart and kidney benefits; ARBs avoid bradykinin side effects. ACEI cough → switch to ARB. ARBs are usually second choice in HFrEF and post-MI (first when ACEI is not tolerated).

5. Angiotensin Receptor–Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI): Sacubitril/Valsartan

CORE Sacubitril/valsartan (Entresto) is the only ARNI in clinical use.

Slide: ARNI. Valsartan blocks AGTR1 (AT₁); sacubitril inhibits neprilysin, so vasodilator and natriuretic peptides are not degraded.
Sacubitril/valsartan (ARNI)
MechanismValsartan blocks AT₁ (vasoconstriction, Na⁺ retention, remodeling). Sacubitril is a prodrug turned into a neprilysin inhibitor. Neprilysin normally degrades atrial and B-type natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP), bradykinin, adrenomedullin, Ang I/II and endothelin-1, so inhibition ↑ vasodilatory and natriuretic peptides
Effects↓ preload, ↓ afterload, ↓ remodeling; more benefit than ACEI or ARB alone; greater BP reduction
UsesHFrEF: first-line in symptomatic patients (PARADIGM-HF: fewer cardiovascular deaths and heart-failure hospitalizations than enalapril). May reduce hospitalizations in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF); mortality benefit unclear
PharmacokineticsOral, twice daily. Sacubitril: ~60% bioavailable, ~11 h half-life. Valsartan active as given; hepatic metabolism, biliary elimination
Adverse effectsHypotension (especially first dose), hyperkalemia, renal dysfunction; cough/angioedema possible (neprilysin inhibition raises bradykinin) but less than ACEI
ContraindicationsNever with an ACEI, ARB or aliskiren (angioedema, hypotension, kidney injury). 36-hour washout after stopping an ACEI. Pregnancy; prior angioedema from RAAS inhibitors
InteractionsK⁺-sparing diuretics, K⁺ supplements, NSAIDs
RuleAn ARNI is started in place of, never in addition to, an ACEI. Why the 36 hours: neprilysin inhibition plus ACE inhibition both raise bradykinin → angioedema. beyond source

6. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRAs): Spironolactone

CORE Spironolactone (Aldactone) is the core MRA. Eplerenone (Inspra) class-level.

Spironolactone (MRA)
MechanismCompetitively blocks mineralocorticoid receptors in the distal nephron and in cardiovascular tissue (the final step of the cascade)
Effects↓ Na⁺/water reabsorption (↓ preload and afterload); ↓ K⁺ and H⁺ excretion; blocks aldosterone-driven cardiac fibrosis and vascular remodeling; better ventricular function; survival benefit in HFrEF
UsesHFrEF (guideline-directed, especially persistent symptoms despite ACEI/ARB); primary aldosteronism; add-on for resistant HTN
PharmacokineticsOral, once or twice daily; prodrug with active metabolites
Adverse effectsHyperkalemia (renal impairment, other RAAS inhibitors); antiandrogenic effects (binds androgen and progesterone receptors): gynecomastia, impotence, menstrual irregularities
ContraindicationsSevere renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <30), baseline hyperkalemia, Addison disease; no other K⁺-sparing diuretics
InteractionsACEI, ARB, ARNI, aliskiren → hyperkalemia and renal dysfunction (monitor K⁺ and kidney function); NSAIDs blunt diuretic effect and impair kidneys
SpironolactoneEplerenone (class-level)
Receptor selectivityAlso binds androgen/progesterone receptorsMore selective for MR
Endocrine adverse effectsGynecomastia, impotence, menstrual changesFewer
Half-life / metabolismProdrug, active metabolitesShorter half-life; CYP3A4 (cytochrome P450 3A4) substrate
Interaction—Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, clarithromycin) raise levels
CostCheaperGenerally more costly

7. Class-Level Agents: Aliskiren and Beta-1 Blockers

Direct renin inhibitor: aliskiren class-level

MechanismDirectly inhibits renin → blocks angiotensinogen → Ang I; lowers Ang I/II and aldosterone; suppresses plasma renin activity more completely
UseHTN only, rarely used: BP lowering equal to or better than ACEI/ARB, but no improvement in cardiovascular or renal outcomes
Adverse effectsHypotension, hyperkalemia, renal dysfunction; bradykinin effects less likely
ContraindicationsPregnancy; combination with ACEI or ARB in diabetes or renal impairment

Beta-1 blockers: renin-secretion inhibitors class-level

DrugsMetoprolol, bisoprolol (summary slide), carvedilol (listed on her slide) and others. beyond source Carvedilol is non-selective with alpha-1 blockade
MechanismBlock beta-1 receptors in heart and JG cells → ↓ renin release → ↓ Ang II and aldosterone (indirect, upstream RAAS modulation). Cardiac effect: ↓ heart rate, contractility, oxygen demand
UsesHTN (as part of combination therapy); HFrEF cornerstone (survival, fewer hospitalizations; titrate slowly); post-MI (prevent remodeling and recurrent ischemia)
Adverse effectsBradycardia, fatigue, exercise intolerance; bronchospasm in asthma (even cardioselective at high doses); masks hypoglycemia; mild hypotension
ContraindicationsSevere bradycardia, advanced heart block without pacemaker, cardiogenic shock; caution in asthma, severe peripheral vascular disease, uncontrolled diabetes
InteractionsNon-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (additive bradycardia/AV block); CYP2D6 inhibitors raise metoprolol levels; atenolol is renally cleared (adjust in kidney disease)
Distinguish these twoBeta-blockers reduce renin release (JG cell, beta-1). Aliskiren inhibits renin activity (the enzyme). Both are upstream.

8. Clinical Pharmacology: Choosing the Agent

SettingChoice (what Dr. Waterfall emphasizes)
HypertensionACEI or ARB first line (especially diabetes or CKD; her notes also name non-Black patients under 55). Spironolactone is the add-on for resistant HTN
Post-MI (left ventricular systolic dysfunction)Start a RAAS inhibitor early, ideally within 24 h after stabilization. ACEI lowers mortality and adverse remodeling; ARB if ACEI not tolerated (cough, angioedema)
HFrEFACEI/ARB reduce morbidity and mortality (ARB if ACEI-intolerant). ARNI superior to ACEI, started in place of the ACEI with a 36-h washout. Beta-1 blocker + MRA complement. Full neurohormonal blockade: ACEI or ARNI + beta-1 blocker + MRA
CKD, diabetic nephropathyACEI/ARB are cornerstone: dilate the efferent arteriole → ↓ intraglomerular pressure and hyperfiltration → ↓ proteinuria; delay renal replacement. Most beneficial when albuminuria ≥300 mg/day. Expect an initial creatinine rise
Primary aldosteronismSpironolactone (or eplerenone)
ACEI coughSwitch to ARB
Marfan syndromeLosartan (TGF-beta, aortic root)

9. Adverse Effects, Monitoring and Interactions

  • All RAAS drugs (ACEI, ARB, ARNI, aliskiren, MRA) can cause hyperkalemia, hypotension and kidney function decline. Highest risk: volume depletion, advanced CKD, bilateral renal artery stenosis (absolute contraindication).
  • Bradykinin effects (cough, angioedema): ACEI (most) and ARNI (less); ARB and aliskiren are less likely.
  • Efferent dilation (ACEI/ARB): lower intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria with a possible initial creatinine rise.
  • Monitor creatinine, eGFR and K⁺ (especially proteinuric or advanced CKD, after starting or raising a dose); BP and volume status before and after starting. Baseline liver function may matter for the ARNI.
  • Pregnancy: absolute contraindication (fetal kidney injury and death; teratogenic in the 2nd and 3rd trimester) for ACEI, ARB, ARNI, aliskiren (and MRAs per her summary table).
  • Counsel: avoid potassium-rich salt substitutes and NSAIDs.
  • Avoid dual RAAS blockade (ACEI + ARB, or inappropriate aliskiren): more hyperkalemia, hypotension and AKI, no added benefit.

Bucket 1

Nephrotic Syndrome

MechanismThe glomerular filter leaks (podocyte / slit diaphragm / charge barrier failure) but the body doesn't "sense" it — protein pours out with no inflammation. Albumin goes first (smallest, anionic) → ↓oncotic pressure → edema; liver ramps up lipoproteins → hyperlipidemia.
High yield>3.5 g/24 h proteinuria + albumin <3.5 g/dL + edema (generalized, periorbital) + hyperlipidemia + lipiduria (oval fat bodies, Maltese crosses). Usually no RBC casts, no HTN, no acute kidney injury (AKI). Complications: thrombosis (urinary antithrombin loss — renal vein, pulmonary embolism (PE)), infection (immunoglobulin G (IgG) loss), hyperlipidemia.

The Three Primary Causes — Know the light microscopy (LM) / IF / electron microscopy (EM) Fingerprint

Primary = disease starts in the kidney. All three: no inflammatory infiltrate.

Minimal Change DiseaseChildren (most common cause by far). LM normal, IF negative, EM foot-process effacement only. Selective proteinuria (albumin). Steroid-responsive → rarely biopsied in kids. T-cell factor injures podocytes; Hodgkin lymphoma, thymoma in adults.
Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)Adults; Black patients; human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), heroin, obesity, sickle cell, Apolipoprotein L1 (APOL1). LM: segmental scar in some glomeruli (focal + segmental; trichrome +). IF/EM: no immune deposits. Non-selective; hematuria 25–75%, HTN 30–50%. Steroid-resistant → the usual biopsy result in a child who fails steroids. Soluble urokinase plasminogen activator receptor (suPAR) elevated ~⅔. Collapsing variant (HIV/heroin) = worst prognosis.
Membranous NephropathyAdults. Anti-PLA2R (70–85%). LM: thick "wire loop" capillaries; silver stain spikes. IF: granular IgG + C3, capillary-loop pattern. EM: subepithelial deposits with new membrane growing between them. No inflammation (subepithelial = quiet; complement/C5b-9 injury). Highest renal vein thrombosis / PE risk.
Secondary MembranousThink of it when a nephrotic patient has a neoplasm or new drug. Hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), syphilis; melanoma, lung and colon cancer; Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) (class V); gold, mercury; penicillamine, captopril, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) (not penicillin). Order hepatitis serologies.

Secondary Nephrotic Causes

The systemic disease is the clue — find it.

Diabetic GlomerulosclerosisTop secondary cause; diabetes mellitus (DM) ≈ 44% of new end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Glycation + oxidative injury → matrix. LM: Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules (pink, acellular; trichrome blue), diffuse mesangial sclerosis, glomerular basement membrane (GBM)/tubular basement membrane (TBM) thickening, afferent + efferent arteriolar hyalinosis. Microalbuminuria first (albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) 30–300; >300 overt). Hyperfiltration (afferent ↑, efferent ↓) early → Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi) and angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB) protect.
Renal AmyloidosisMimics diabetic nodules but not trichrome-blue → Congo red, apple-green birefringence. Light-chain amyloidosis (AL) (most common in the United States; plasma-cell disease) vs Reactive amyloidosis (AA) (chronic inflammation — rheumatoid arthritis (RA), tuberculosis (TB)). Enlarged waxy kidney. Type by IF/mass spec.
Lupus Nephritis — Class VMembranous pattern: nephrotic, subepithelial deposits, silver spikes. (Full classes in Nephritic bucket.) Anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA), anti-Smith, low C3 and complement component 4 (C4).
Membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (MPGN) (Membranoproliferative)Overlap — nephrotic or nephritic. Subendothelial deposits → lots of inflammation, lobular/"tram-track" proliferation. Low complement. Secondary to HBV/HCV, cryoglobulins. Dense deposit disease = C3 only, dense ribbon in GBM, alternative-pathway dysregulation (C3 nephritic factor).

Side-by-Side Decoder

Three questions answer most stems: age, steroid response, what's on IF/EM.

Child, no blood, steroid-sensitiveMinimal change. Normal LM, neg IF, effaced foot processes.
Child, steroid-resistantFSGS (segmental sclerosis, neg IF).
Adult, "wire loops" / spikes / subepithelialMembranous — look for phospholipase A2 receptor (PLA2R) or a 2° cause.
Diabetic with nodulesTrichrome blue = diabetes. Congo red + = amyloid.
Subendothelial + low C3 + "tram-track"MPGN (can be nephritic).
Deposit location → inflammationSubendothelial = inflammatory (MPGN, lupus III/IV). Subepithelial = quiet (membranous). Post-infectious glomerulonephritis (GN) has both → inflammation.
Bucket 2

Nephritic Syndrome & Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN)

MechanismGlomerular inflammation (immune complexes, anti-GBM antibody, or anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-driven injury) → damaged capillaries leak blood, glomerular filtration rate (GFR) falls, body clamps down (HTN, oliguria) so less protein is lost. RPGN is the far end of the same spectrum with crescents.
High yieldHematuria (cola/tea urine; dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts) + HTN + oliguria/azotemia + edema + proteinuria <3.5 g/24 h. Creatinine climbing over days–weeks = RPGN. IF pattern + complement = your diagnostic decoder: linear = anti-GBM only; granular = immune complex; negative = pauci-immune (ANCA).

IF / Complement Decoder

Do this first: pattern → complement → serology.

Linear IgGAnti-GBM (Goodpasture) — the only linear disease. C3 normal.
Granular + LOW C3Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN), SLE, MPGN (and cryoglobulinemia). Positive antinuclear antibody (ANA) → SLE; recent strep → PSGN.
Granular + NORMAL C3Immunoglobulin A (IgA) nephropathy / Henoch-Schönlein purpura (HSP) (mesangial IgA).
Negative (pauci-immune)ANCA vasculitis (granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), microscopic polyangiitis (MPA), eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA)); acute interstitial nephritis (AIN) is also IF-negative but tubulointerstitial.
Normal complement listIgA nephropathy (IgAN), Goodpasture, polyarteritis nodosa (PAN), idiopathic RPGN, ANCA disease, AIN.
Low complement listSLE, post-infectious GN, MPGN.

Immune-Complex Nephritis (Type III)

Complexes (circulating or planted) lodge in glomerular capillaries → complement → neutrophils (NETosis).

Post-Streptococcal GNForeign (strep) antigen → not autoimmune. 1–3 wk after pharyngitis (~6 wk after skin infection). Cola urine, periorbital edema, HTN. ↑antistreptolysin O (ASO), ↑anti-deoxyribonuclease B (anti-DNase B), low C3 (recovers in 8–12 wk). LM: neutrophil-rich hypercellular tufts. EM: subepithelial humps. IF: granular, "starry sky." Kids recover with supportive care.
IgA Nephropathy (Berger)Most common primary GN worldwide. Gross hematuria 1–3 days after upper respiratory infection (URI)/gastrointestinal (GI) infection ("synpharyngitic"), recurrent. Galactose-deficient IgA1 → anti-IgA IgG → complexes. Normal C3. LM: mesangial proliferation (>3–4 cells/area). IF: mesangial IgA ± C3. Celiac association. Most mild; some → chronic kidney disease (CKD).
IgA Vasculitis (HSP)Systemic IgA: palpable purpura (legs/buttocks), abdominal pain, arthritis/arthralgia + nephritis. IgA in vessels/mesangium. Kids; post-infection.
Lupus Nephritis (International Society of Nephrology/Renal Pathology Society classes)ANA (sensitive), anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith (specific), low C3/C4; American College of Rheumatology criteria: 4 of 11; malar rash spares nasolabial folds. I minimal mesangial · II mesangial proliferative · III focal (<50%) · IV diffuse (>50%) — most common/severe: wire loops, subendothelial deposits, full-house IF (IgG, IgA, immunoglobulin M (IgM), C3, C1q) · V membranous (nephrotic) · VI >90% sclerosed. ≥10% reach ESRD.

RPGN — Crescentic Glomerulonephritis

Creatinine rising over weeks to months with active sediment. Crescents = proliferating parietal epithelial cells + macrophages + fibrin (Jones silver: GBM breaks).

Anti-GBM / Goodpasture (10–15%)Type II. Ab to α3 chain of type IV collagen (non-collagenous domain, NC1). Linear IgG; C3 normal. Lung + kidney: hemoptysis + RPGN (~50% alveolar hemorrhage); young men (smokers) + older adults. Plasmapheresis + steroids + cyclophosphamide. (Alport = α5, inherited.)
Immune-Complex RPGN (25–30%)Granular IF. PSGN, lupus (IV), IgAN, HSP. Treat the underlying disease.
Pauci-Immune (40–50%)Negative IF; ANCA+ in 80–90%. Capillaritis, necrotizing GN, neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) + alternative complement. Small-vessel vasculitis (below).
Prognosis>50% of glomeruli normal/focal lesion → >90% recovery at 5 yr. >50% globally sclerosed → <25%. Active crescents are treatable; scar is not.

ANCA-Associated Vasculitis

Small-vessel, pauci-immune. Know the antibody and the clinical tag.

GPA (Wegener)c-ANCA / proteinase 3 (PR3). Upper airway (sinusitis, nasal crusting/saddle nose) + lung nodules/cavities + GN (~80% renal). Necrotizing granulomas — the feature that separates it from MPA.
Microscopic Polyangiitisp-ANCA / myeloperoxidase (MPO). Renal in ~90%; no granulomas; leukocytoclastic vasculitis, purpura, mononeuritis, pulmonary capillaritis.
EGPA (Churg-Strauss)Asthma + eosinophilia + mononeuritis multiplex; ANCA+ in <50%; renal ~45%.
Bucket 3

Congenital & Pediatric Renal Disease

MechanismErrors of kidney development (bud ↔ metanephric mesenchyme induction, migration, drainage) or inherited tubule/GBM genes. Congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) = 1–3 per 500 births and 40–50% of pediatric CKD.
High yieldFetal urine → amniotic fluid → lung development: bilateral renal failure = oligohydramnios → pulmonary hypoplasia + Potter facies (needs bilateral disease). Embryology: pronephros → mesonephros → metanephros (final kidney). Know agenesis vs aplasia vs hypoplasia vs dysplasia.

Developmental Anomalies

Absent, small, abnormal, or misplaced.

Renal AgenesisUnilateral 1:1,000–3,000 (M>F, left>right) — healthy but needs lifelong blood pressure (BP), urinalysis and ultrasound monitoring (solitary kidney hyperfilters). Bilateral 1:7,000–10,000 → Potter sequence, incompatible with life. Aplasia = rudimentary remnant.
Hypoplasia vs DysplasiaHypoplasia = small but normally organized. Dysplasia = disorganized: primitive ducts with fibromuscular collars, cartilage. ~50% have other urinary anomalies; bilateral → neonatal acidosis, Na wasting, high Cr.
Multicystic Dysplastic KidneyNon-communicating cysts, no functioning parenchyma. Most common congenital solitary kidney; ~60% involute by age 10. Contralateral kidney normal.
Horseshoe & Pelvic KidneyHorseshoe (fused lower poles) — trapped under the inferior mesenteric artery; Turner syndrome, trisomies; urinary tract infection (UTI), stones, obstruction. Pelvic kidney = failed ascent.
Duplicated Collecting SystemF>M (<1%). Upper-pole ureter inserts ectopically → continuous incontinence in girls + recurrent UTI; lower pole refluxes. Trisomy 18/13 spectrum: renal anomalies common.

Cystic & Hereditary Tubular Disease

Name the gene, the cyst pattern, and the extra-renal hook.

Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD)PKD1 (chr 16, ~85%) > PKD2. Adults 30–50; ~5% of ESRD. Kidneys up to 1–3 kg, >50 cysts (cortex + medulla). Berry aneurysm → subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), HTN, hematuria, hepatic cysts, mitral valve prolapse (MVP). 50% inheritance.
Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD)PKHD1 / fibrocystin. Neonate: huge echogenic kidneys, radial fusiform collecting-duct cysts, smooth surface, congenital hepatic fibrosis, Potter-like picture, HTN, ESRD in childhood. Siblings affected, parents healthy.
NephronophthisisSmall kidneys with corticomedullary cysts; polyuria/polydipsia (concentrating defect), anemia; most common genetic cause of pediatric ESRD; retinitis pigmentosa associations. Hard to biopsy.
Autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD) (MUC1/UMOD/REN)Bland sediment, slowly progressive CKD, gout/hyperuricemia, family history; small kidneys. Medullary sponge kidney = "paintbrush" ducts, stones, normal function (innocuous).
Alport SyndromeX-linked α5 chain of type IV collagen. Hematuria → ESRD (males); sensorineural deafness, anterior lenticonus / cataracts. EM: basket-weave GBM. (Fabry = α-galactosidase A.)

Obstruction & Reflux

Prenatal hydronephrosis: 50–75% transient; 10–30% ureteropelvic junction (UPJ) obstruction or vesicoureteral reflux (VUR).

UPJ ObstructionMost common cause of pathologic neonatal hydronephrosis: dilated pelvis/calyces, normal ureter.
Posterior Urethral ValvesMales: distended bladder, bilateral hydronephrosis, oligohydramnios, weak stream; dilated posterior urethra on voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG). Prune belly = absent abdominal wall muscle + cryptorchidism + urinary dilation.
Vesicoureteral RefluxPrimary (short intramural ureter) vs secondary. First febrile UTI → renal ultrasound ± VCUG. Recurrent pyelonephritis → scarring (dimercaptosuccinic acid, DMSA, scan) → HTN, CKD. Prophylaxis vs surgery by grade.
Postnatal Work-UpRepeat ultrasound at 2–3 days (early oliguria hides dilation). Society for Fetal Urology (SFU) grade 0–4. Antenatal hydronephrosis, febrile UTI in infants → imaging.

Bucket 4

Atypical / Other

MechanismKidney disease that is not a glomerular nephrotic/nephritic picture: tubulointerstitial injury, plasma-cell disease, obstruction, vascular, chronic scarring, and the tubular/acid-base physiology that goes with them.
High yieldAIN: new drug + fever/rash/eosinophilia, sterile pyuria, white blood cell (WBC) casts, IF-negative. Myeloma kidney: dipstick-negative protein. CKD = damage or estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <60 for >3 months; DM + HTN cause ~72% of new ESRD (GN ~8%). End-stage kidney: shrunken (~40 g vs ~150 g), granular — origin often unknowable.

Tubulointerstitial Disease

Rising creatinine without RBC casts.

Acute Interstitial NephritisType IV (T-cell; Th1, CD8, interferon-γ, interleukin-2, interleukin-17 and tumor necrosis factor). Drugs: NSAIDs, β-lactams, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), sulfonamides, diuretics; also infections, sarcoid, SLE. Delayed onset after first exposure. Rash/fever/eosinophilia (minority), sterile pyuria, WBC casts, eosinophiluria. IF negative, C3 normal. Stop the drug ± steroids; chronic → fibrosis.
Light-Chain Cast NephropathyMost common renal lesion in myeloma. Light chains + Tamm-Horsfall protein plug distal tubules → AKI. Precipitants: dehydration, hypercalcemia, NSAIDs, contrast. Up to 50% impaired renal function, ~10% dialysis.
Multiple Myeloma CluesCRAB: hypercalcemia, renal failure, anemia, bone lesions (punched-out skull). serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) M-spike → immunofixation (e.g., IgG-κ). Infections from suppressed normal Ig. Dipstick misses light chains → urine protein electrophoresis / sulfosalicylic acid.

Chronic Kidney Disease & End-Stage

CKD Stages (eGFR)1 >90 · 2 60–89 · 3 30–59 · 4 15–29 · 5 <15 mL/min/1.73 m². Must persist >3 months.
Causes of New ESRDDM ~44%, HTN ~28%, GN ~8%. Chronic GN is a label for end-stage scarring: both kidneys symmetrically small, granular surface, thin cortex, ± acquired cysts.
Acquired Cystic DiseaseLong-term dialysis → small kidneys with multiple cysts → renal cell carcinoma risk. (Simple cortical cysts: benign, thin-walled, no renal cell carcinoma (RCC) risk.)
HydronephrosisDilated calyces/pelvis with thinned cortex from obstruction — not a cystic disease. Stone, UPJ/posterior urethral valves (PUV), benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), tumor.

Renal Vascular & Mass

Imaging-driven.

Fibromuscular DysplasiaYoung woman, resistant HTN, "string of beads" on computed tomography angiography (CTA).
Renal InfarctEmbolus (AF) → wedge-shaped non-enhancing defect, flank pain, hematuria, ↑lactate dehydrogenase (LDH).
Acute PyelonephritisFever + flank pain + pyuria/WBC casts; contrast computed tomography (CT) wedge-shaped hypo-enhancement (infection) — infarct has no inflammation.
Solid Renal MassEnhancing mass on contrast CT/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) → renal cell carcinoma until proven otherwise; painless hematuria in older adults → CT urography + cystoscopy.

Tubular & Acid-Base Pearls

Diuretic SitesLoop (furosemide) — Na-K-2Cl cotransporter (NKCC2), thick ascending limb (dissipates the medullary gradient). Thiazide — Na-Cl cotransporter (NCC), distal convoluted tubule (DCT) (↓Ca excretion). Amiloride — epithelial sodium channel (ENaC); spironolactone — aldosterone receptor (both K-sparing). Acetazolamide — carbonic anhydrase (proximal).
Anion GapAnion gap (AG) = Na − (Cl + HCO3); normal ≈ 8–12. High AG (MUDPILES): methanol, uremia, diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), propylene glycol, isoniazid/iron, lactate, ethylene glycol, salicylates. Normal AG: diarrhea, renal tubular acidosis (RTA), saline loading. Toxic alcohols → osmolar gap.
Renal Tubular AcidosisType 1 (distal; ↓H+ secretion): urine pH >5.5, ↓K, stones. Type 2 (proximal; HCO3 wasting, Fanconi). Type 4 (hypoaldosteronism): ↑K. All normal-gap.
Potassium ShiftsInsulin deficiency, hyperosmolarity, acidemia → K+ out of cells. β-agonists, insulin → K+ in. Acidemia ≈ ↑K; alkalemia ≈ ↓K.
Cross-bucket reminders Lupus lives in both nephrotic (class V) and nephritic (III/IV). MPGN can be either. IgAN is the common thread in kids and young adults (hematuria after infection). Diabetes = nephrotic + the top cause of ESRD. Subendothelial deposits = inflammation (nephritic); subepithelial = quiet (nephrotic). Linear IF = anti-GBM only.

Urinalysis & Sediment Quick-Reference

RBC casts / dysmorphic RBCs / acanthocytesGlomerular bleeding = nephritic (blood leaks into Bowman space and is molded in the tubule). A bladder/kidney tumor does not make RBC casts.
WBC castsPyelonephritis or AIN (tubulointerstitial). Eosinophiluria + sterile pyuria → AIN.
Fatty casts / oval fat bodies / Maltese crossesNephrotic lipiduria.
Muddy-brown granular castsAcute tubular necrosis (ATN). Waxy casts = advanced CKD. Hyaline casts = concentrated/prerenal, nonspecific.
Dipstick pitfallsDetects albumin (>30 mg/dL) — misses light chains (use urine protein electrophoresis (UPEP)/sulfosalicylic acid). Ketone pad reads acetoacetate, not β-hydroxybutyrate. Nitrite negative with Enterococcus. Hematuria dipstick + no RBCs on microscopy → myoglobin/hemoglobin.
Quantifying proteinSpot ACR (30–300 micro; >300 macro) / Protein-to-creatinine ratio (PCR); 24-h urine 3.5 g = nephrotic. Heavier dipstick protein → more likely glomerular.
Urine collectionInfants: catheter or suprapubic, never a bag/diaper sample. Adults: clean-catch midstream; heavy squamous cells = contaminated.
Concentration / pHFixed isosthenuria → tubular damage/CKD. Alkaline urine + normal-gap acidosis → distal RTA or urease (Proteus) infection.

Serology & Lab Quick-Reference

SerologiesAnti-GBM → Goodpasture. c-ANCA/PR3 → GPA. p-ANCA/MPO → MPA. ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith → SLE. Anti-PLA2R → membranous. suPAR → FSGS. ASO, anti-deoxyribonuclease B (anti-DNase B) → PSGN. SPEP/immunofixation → myeloma/AL.
ComplementLow C3: PSGN, SLE (also low C4), MPGN. Normal: IgAN, Goodpasture, ANCA, AIN, idiopathic RPGN.
Creatinine / eGFRLow muscle mass (cachexia) → "normal" Cr hides low GFR; high muscle mass → ↑Cr without ↓GFR. eGFR/CKD stage best from repeat values.
Blood urea nitrogen (BUN):Cr>20:1 prerenal, GI bleed (digested blood raises urea), high protein. Low urea with liver failure.
Nephrotic panelAlbumin <3.5 g/dL, ↑cholesterol/triglycerides, proteinuria >3.5 g. Hepatitis B/C, HIV, ANA, glucose/hemoglobin A1c when 2° causes suspected.
When to biopsyIf it will change management, disease is severe, treatment-resistant or steroid-resistant. Not in a typical steroid-responsive child.

Imaging Quick-Reference

First-line choicesStone: non-contrast CT (ultrasound in pregnancy/children). Hematuria (upper tract): CT urography ± cystoscopy. Solid mass: contrast CT/MRI. Hydronephrosis/obstruction: ultrasound. Reflux/PUV: VCUG. Scarring/split function: DMSA / renal scintigraphy.
Pattern recognitionADPKD: huge bilateral kidneys, innumerable cysts. ARPKD: large echogenic kidneys. CKD: small echogenic kidneys. Hydronephrosis: dilated calyces/pelvis. Pyelonephritis: wedge hypo-enhancement. Infarct: wedge non-enhancing. Fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD): string of beads. Horseshoe: fused lower poles.
Contrast safetyCheck renal function before iodinated contrast — contrast can worsen kidney injury. CT phases: arterial ~15–25 s, nephrographic ~70–100 s, excretory/delayed ≥ several minutes.

Physical Exam Quick-Reference

Bedside findings tied back to the diseases above.

EdemaPeriorbital/facial (worse in the morning) and pitting leg edema, ascites → nephrotic (loose eyelid tissue fills first); also seen in PSGN. Different from heart failure (legs, jugular venous distension (JVD), S3).
Blood pressureHTN → nephritic, FSGS, ADPKD, ARPKD, FMD, reflux scarring. Normal BP favors minimal change.
SkinMalar rash (spares nasolabial folds) → SLE. Palpable purpura on legs/buttocks → HSP/IgA vasculitis, MPA. Rash + fever after new drug → AIN. Atopic dermatitis / impetigo → PSGN history.
Lungs / ear, nose and throat (ENT)Hemoptysis → Goodpasture, GPA. Sinusitis, saddle nose, nasal crusting → GPA. Asthma → EGPA.
Abdomen / flanksBilateral flank masses → ADPKD, ARPKD. Distended bladder (newborn) → PUV. Absent abdominal wall muscle → prune belly. Bruit over renal artery → renovascular HTN.
Eyes / earsSensorineural deafness, lenticonus/cataract → Alport. Retinitis pigmentosa → nephronophthisis. Hypertensive retinopathy in CKD.
NewbornPotter facies (flat nose, low-set ears), limb deformities, respiratory distress → bilateral agenesis/ARPKD. Single umbilical artery → renal anomaly screen. Turner stigmata → horseshoe kidney.
Uremia / CKDPallor, uremic frost, asterixis, pericardial friction rub, Kussmaul breathing (acidosis), pruritus.
Urine appearanceCola/tea → glomerular (PSGN, IgAN, Goodpasture). Frothy → heavy proteinuria. Bone pain/back pain + AKI → myeloma.

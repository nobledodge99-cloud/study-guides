Bucket 1

Lower Urinary Tract (Ureter & Bladder)

MechanismDisease of the conduit and reservoir — obstruction, infection/inflammation, then metaplasia → neoplasia. Think: what irritates the urothelium?
High yieldUrothelial carcinoma = 95–97% of bladder cancer. Squamous cell = schistosomiasis / chronic Foley (catheter). Cystitis: no white blood cell (WBC) casts (that's pyelonephritis).

Ureteral & Obstructive Lesions

Hydronephrosis risk — look for the upstream kidney

UPJ obstructionMost common congenital cause of hydronephrosis in infants/children; ureteropelvic junction (UPJ). Imaging: dilated renal pelvis, normal ureter. Ultrasound first.
Retroperitoneal fibrosisFibrous encasement of ureters (medial deviation on imaging) → obstruction. Idiopathic (IgG4 link), drugs, malignancy.
DiverticulaOutpouching of bladder wall; urine stasis → recurrent infection, stones, carcinoma risk.

Cystitis Spectrum

Know the organism / trigger / histology hook for each

Bacterial cystitisE. coli ~90%. Dysuria, frequency, urgency. Urinalysis (UA): pyuria + bacteriuria; leukocyte esterase/nitrite. No WBC casts (casts = upper tract).
Hemorrhagic cystitisCyclophosphamide (toxic metabolite acrolein) or adenovirus; hematuria. Prevent with mesna/hydration.
Interstitial cystitisChronic suprapubic pain with filling, relieved by voiding; sterile urine; Hunner ulcers; mast cells.
MalakoplakiaChronic bacterial (E. coli) infection, defective macrophage killing; soft yellow plaques. Michaelis–Gutmann bodies (calcified, targetoid).
Polypoid cystitisEdematous polyps from irritation (catheter) — benign mimic of carcinoma.

Metaplasias

Chronic irritation → change of epithelium (precursor to neoplasia)

Cystitis cystica / glandularisNests of urothelium (cystica) or glandular/intestinal-type (glandularis) in lamina propria; intestinal type → adenocarcinoma risk.
Squamous metaplasiaChronic irritation (stones, Foley, Schistosoma haematobium) → squamous cell carcinoma.
Nephrogenic metaplasiaTubule-like proliferation after injury/surgery; benign mimic.

Bladder Carcinoma

Hematuria (painless) in older smoker = urothelial until proven otherwise

Urothelial carcinoma95–97%. Risks: smoking, aniline dyes, cyclophosphamide, phenacetin. Low-grade papillary: 9p/9q loss (FGFR3). High-grade/CIS: TP53. Painless gross hematuria. Carcinoma in situ (CIS) progresses 50–75%. Treatment: TURBT ± BCG (bacillus Calmette–Guérin) intravesical.
Squamous cell carcinomaSchistosomiasis (Egypt) or chronic Foley/stones; keratinizing; usually advanced at diagnosis.
AdenocarcinomaUrachal remnant (dome) or glandularis; rare.
Bucket 2

Renal Tumors

MechanismMostly tubular epithelium (adults) vs embryonal/blastemal (kids). Syndrome-driven genes: VHL, MET, WT1.
High yieldClassic RCC triad: flank pain + hematuria + palpable mass (only ~10%). Paraneoplastic: EPO (polycythemia), PTHrP (hypercalcemia), renin (HTN), ACTH. RCC invades the renal vein / IVC.

Benign Renal Tumors

AngiomyolipomaBlood vessels + smooth muscle + fat; tuberous sclerosis (TSC); HMB-45/desmin+. Imaging: fat density on CT. Bleeding risk if >4 cm.
OncocytomaMahogany-brown with central scar on gross/CT; eosinophilic mitochondria-rich cells; loss of chromosomes 1, 14, Y.
Papillary adenoma<5 mm, incidental, autopsy finding.
Medullary fibroma / Mesoblastic nephromaFibroma = incidental. Mesoblastic nephroma = most common renal tumor in infants <3 mo.
JG cell tumor (reninoma)Renin-secreting → hypertension + hypokalemia (secondary hyperaldosteronism).

Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC) Subtypes

Genetics + gross/histology hook

Clear cell (70–80%)VHL loss, 3p25; polygonal clear cells (lipid/glycogen), golden-yellow with hemorrhage; sporadic or von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) disease.
Papillary (10–15%)MET (7q31), trisomy 7/17; psammoma bodies, foamy macrophages; often multifocal/bilateral.
Chromophobe (5%)Chromosome losses; perinuclear halo; Hale's colloidal iron +; best prognosis. Birt–Hogg–Dubé.
Collecting duct / MedullaryMedullary: young Black pts with sickle cell trait; aggressive. Collecting duct: Bellini ducts, poor outcome.
StagingTumor (T) stage: ≤7 cm = T1, >7 cm = T2; perirenal/renal vein extension = T3; adrenal/beyond Gerota = T4.

Pediatric & Pelvic

Wilms tumor (nephroblastoma)Peak age 2–5 yr; triphasic (blastema, epithelial, stromal). WT1 (11p13). Syndromes: WAGR (Wilms, aniridia, genitourinary anomalies, retardation), Denys–Drash (WT1; gonadal dysgenesis + early nephropathy), Beckwith–Wiedemann (11p15; macroglossia, hemihypertrophy). Anaplasia (TP53) = poor prognosis.
Renal pelvis urothelial carcinomaSame biology as bladder urothelial; analgesic (phenacetin) nephropathy, smoking; hematuria + hydronephrosis.
Bucket 3

Renal Vascular Disease, TMA & Vasculitis

MechanismAnything that narrows or injures the renal vasculature → ischemia, hypertension or glomerular injury.
High yieldArteriolar nephrosclerosis = hyaline (benign) vs hyperplastic ('onion-skin') in malignant HTN. TTP = ADAMTS13; HUS = Shiga toxin/factor H; PT/PTT normal in both. c-ANCA = PR3 = GPA; p-ANCA = MPO = MPA.

Nephrosclerosis & Ischemia

Arterial nephrosclerosisAtherosclerosis/intimal thickening of larger arteries → fine granular surface, scarring.
Benign arteriolarHyaline arteriolosclerosis (HTN, diabetes) → symmetrically shrunken, finely granular kidneys.
Malignant HTNHyperplastic arteriolitis (onion-skin), fibrinoid necrosis; flea-bitten kidney; BP >180/120 + organ damage.
AtheroembolismCholesterol clefts after aortic catheterization; livedo reticularis, eosinophilia, ↑ creatinine weeks later.
Diffuse cortical necrosisSeptic shock/obstetric catastrophe (abruption); DIC; patchy → diffuse cortical infarct.

Renal Artery Stenosis & Salt-Handling Syndromes

Atherosclerotic RASOlder men; proximal; abdominal bruit; creatinine rises after ACE inhibitor/ARB (bilateral). Imaging: duplex, CT/MR angiography. Kidney on affected side shrinks.
Fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD)Young women; mid-distal artery; 'string of beads' on angiography; angioplasty works well.
BartterLoop (NKCC2) defect → hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, hypercalciuria; mimics loop diuretic.
GitelmanThiazide (NCC) defect → hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypocalciuria; mimics thiazide.
LiddleENaC gain-of-function → HTN, hypokalemia, alkalosis, low renin/aldosterone; treat with amiloride.

Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA)

Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia + low platelets + organ injury

TTPADAMTS13 deficiency/antibody; fever, neuro, renal, anemia, thrombocytopenia (FAT RN); schistocytes; PT/PTT normal.
HUSKids; Shiga toxin (E. coli O157:H7) — bloody diarrhea then AKI; atypical HUS = complement factor H.

ANCA & Other Vasculitis

GPA (Wegener)c-ANCA / PR3; upper airway + lung + rapidly progressive GN (RPGN); granulomas.
MPAp-ANCA / MPO; kidney + lung capillaritis; no granulomas.
EGPAAsthma + eosinophilia + p-ANCA; granulomas.
PANMedium-vessel; not ANCA+; hepatitis B; spares lung; renal microaneurysms.
IgA vasculitis (HSP)Kids; palpable purpura, abdominal pain, arthralgia, nephritis.
Bucket 4

Hypertension & Secondary Causes

MechanismClinical thinking (Dr. Kinder): classify the BP, confirm out of office, then ask is there a secondary cause? Clues drive the workup.
High yieldCategories: normal <120/80; elevated 120–129/<80; stage 1 130–139/80–89; stage 2 ≥140/90; crisis ≥180/≥120 — with organ damage = emergency. Goal <130/80. ABPM = gold standard.

Diagnosis & Patterns

MeasurementBoth arms (diff >20 → investigate); ≥2 readings on ≥2 occasions; orthostatic BP in elderly/diabetic.
ABPM normals24 h <130/80; day <135/85; night <120/70. Dipper ≥10% drop; non-dipper; reverse dipper. Nighttime HTN = strongest predictor.
White-coat vs maskedWhite-coat: office high, home <125/75 (no treatment). Masked: office normal, home high — same risk as sustained.
Treat thresholdStage 2 → drugs. Stage 1 → drugs if PREVENT 10-yr CVD ≥7.5%, CVD, CKD, diabetes.
uACR<30 normal; 30–300 elevated; >300 severe. Baseline labs: CBC, CMP, lipids, UA, ECG (LVH), TSH.

Organ Damage & Crisis

RetinopathyAV nicking, copper wiring, cotton-wool spots, flame hemorrhages, papilledema.
Brain / heart / kidneyLacunar stroke (thalamus, basal ganglia), ICH; LVH → diastolic dysfunction; nephrosclerosis → CKD, albuminuria.
EmergencyChest pain, confusion, dyspnea, seizure, AKI → ICU, IV agents. Think dissection, eclampsia, pheochromocytoma, stroke, pulmonary edema.
Asymptomatic severeRestart oral meds; follow up ~2 weeks; no rapid lowering.

Secondary Hypertension Clues

~5% of HTN; bimodal (children, >65)

Suspect whenAge <25 or >65; ≥3 drugs (resistant); bruit; unprovoked hypokalemia; severe initial HTN.
Renal artery stenosisBruit, flash pulmonary edema, ↑ Cr after ACE inhibitor. FMD: young woman, string of beads.
Primary aldosteronism (Conn)Adenoma 70%, hyperplasia 25%. Hypokalemia, ↑ aldosterone/renin ratio >20; adrenal CT; adrenal vein sampling.
PheochromocytomaSpells: tachycardia, sweat, headache, pallor; plasma free metanephrines; CT; α-block before β-block.
CushingMoon facies, striae, buffalo hump; late-night salivary cortisol / dexamethasone suppression.
CoarctationBrachial-femoral delay, rib notching on CXR, bicuspid aortic valve.
OSA / drugs / thyroidOSA = snoring, non-dipping. NSAIDs, OCPs, decongestants, cyclosporine, VEGF inhibitors. Hypothyroid = diastolic HTN.
CKDHigh Cr, proteinuria/hematuria, small kidneys.
Bucket 5

AKI, Obstruction & Stones

MechanismAbrupt fall in GFR: pre-renal (perfusion), intrinsic (tubule, glomerulus, interstitium, vessel), post-renal (obstruction).
High yieldKDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes): ↑Cr ≥0.3 in 48 h, ≥1.5× baseline in 7 d, or urine <0.5 mL/kg/h × 6 h. Pre-renal: BUN:Cr >20, FENa <1%, FEUrea <35%; ATN: FENa >2%, muddy brown casts.

AKI by Category

Pre-renalHypovolemia, ↓ cardiac output, NSAID/ACE inhibitor/ARB. Bland sediment/hyaline casts; urine osm >500; UNa <20.
ATNIschemic or nephrotoxic (aminoglycosides, contrast, cisplatin, ethylene glycol → calcium oxalate). Muddy brown (granular) casts, FENa >2%; isosthenuria.
Interstitial nephritisDrug (75%) — WBC casts, eosinophils, rash, fever, pyuria.
GlomerularRBC casts, dysmorphic RBCs (nephritic).
Post-renalBilateral/single functional kidney; BPH, stones, tumor. Ultrasound shows hydronephrosis (only imaging needed; kidneys <8 cm = chronic).

Workup & Management (Dr. Bell)

ApproachHistory, medication review, volume exam, UA/sediment, urine lytes, bladder scan/ultrasound. Stop nephrotoxins.
ManagementCrystalloids if hypovolemic; norepinephrine, MAP >65 in shock; avoid contrast; treat hyperkalemia.

Nephrolithiasis

Stone type by pH, crystal shape and imaging

Calcium oxalate / phosphate (80%)Radio-opaque; hypercalciuria; oxalate envelope crystals. Dumbbell/envelope; low citrate.
Struvite (~10%)Urease organisms (Proteus) → alkaline urine, staghorn, coffin-lid crystals.
Uric acid (6–7%)Radiolucent; acid urine (pH <5.5); gout, high cell turnover; rhomboid crystals; alkalinize urine.
Cystine (1%)AR COLA transporter defect; hexagonal crystals; positive cyanide-nitroprusside.
Imaging & managementNon-contrast CT = gold standard; ultrasound in pregnancy/kids. <5 mm pass; 5–10 mm tamsulosin/observe; ESWL (extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy) 10–25 mm. Admit for infection + obstruction, AKI, uncontrolled pain.
HydronephrosisDilated calyces/pelvis from obstruction (stone, BPH, tumor, UPJ); late → cortical atrophy.
Bucket 6

CKD & Its Complications

MechanismIrreversible nephron loss → everything the kidney does fails: filtration, acid/K handling, EPO, vitamin D, platelet function.
High yieldCKD = GFR <60 or markers of injury ≥3 months. eGFR stages: G1 ≥90, G2 60–89, G3a 45–59, G3b 30–44, G4 15–29, G5 <15. BUN:Cr >20 suggests prerenal contribution.

Complications

UremiaPericarditis (friction rub), encephalopathy, asterixis, pruritus, platelet dysfunction → desmopressin (DDAVP).
Anemia↓ EPO — normocytic normochromic; treat with ESA after iron repletion.
MBD (mineral–bone)↓ calcitriol, ↑ FGF23, ↑ phosphate, ↓ Ca → secondary hyperparathyroidism → osteitis fibrosa cystica. Don't give D2/D3 early; RANKL.
Metabolic acidosis↓ NH₄⁺ → low HCO₃⁻; bone buffering; treat with bicarbonate.
HyperkalemiaECG: peaked T → PR ↑ → QRS widening → sine wave. C-BIG-K (calcium, bicarbonate, insulin, glucose, kayexalate).
Dialysis indicationsAEIOU: acidosis, electrolytes, intoxication (lithium, methanol, aspirin, ethylene glycol), overload, uremia.

Cystic & Hereditary

ADPKDPKD1/PKD2; HTN, flank pain, hematuria, berry aneurysm, hepatic cysts, mitral valve prolapse (MVP); bilateral enlarged cystic kidneys on ultrasound.
ARPKDPKHD1; infants — Potter sequence, hepatic fibrosis, 'sponge' kidneys.
TSC / VHLTSC: angiomyolipoma, RCC 2–3%; VHL: RCC ~70%, hemangioblastoma.
Interstitial nephritis (chronic)Analgesic/ drug, reflux; papillary necrosis; WBC casts, sterile pyuria.

Dialysis & Renal Dosing

ModalitiesHD (hemodialysis), PD (peritoneal), CRRT (continuous) for unstable pts.
Dose adjustMetformin (lactic acidosis), gabapentin, morphine metabolites.
Bucket 7

Sodium, Water & Electrolytes

MechanismSodium problems are water problems. Step 1: serum osmolality → Step 2: volume status → Step 3: urine Na/osm.
High yieldCorrect Na no faster than 10–12 mEq/L/24 h — overshoot → osmotic demyelination (central pontine myelinolysis). Normal Na 136–144; serum osm 275–295; glucose: Na ↓ ~2.4 per 100 mg/dL.

Hypernatremia

DehydrationPure water loss; cells shrink. Fluid: D5W. ECF depletion → isotonic saline.
Diabetes insipidusCentral (ADH deficiency; responds to desmopressin) vs nephrogenic (no response). Polyuria >3 L/day.

Hyponatremia (<135)

PseudoNormal osm: myeloma, hyperlipidemia.
HypertonicOsm >295: hyperglycemia, mannitol.
Hypotonic — hypovolemicUNa <20: vomiting, diarrhea; UNa >20: diuretics (thiazide!), Addison, cerebral salt wasting.
Hypotonic — euvolemicSIADH (urine osm >200; UNa >20), hypothyroidism, psychogenic polydipsia / beer potomania (urine osm <100).
Hypotonic — hypervolemicCHF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome (UNa <10); renal failure (UNa >20).
TreatHypovolemic → saline; SIADH → fluid restriction; hypervolemic → diurese.

Potassium, Magnesium, Phosphorus

HypokalemiaGI loss or renal (diuretics, RTA I/II, Liddle/Bartter/Gitelman). 24-h urine K <15 → extrarenal. Replete Mg first.
HyperkalemiaECG first (peaked T). Drugs: ACE inhibitor/ARB, spironolactone, TMP-SMX, heparin, NSAIDs. Type 4 RTA.
MagnesiumLow Mg → refractory hypoK/hypoCa. FEMg >0.5% = renal wasting. High Mg: loss of deep tendon reflexes; treat with calcium gluconate.
PhosphorusFanconi = proximal tubule defect. Hyperphosphatemia: CKD, rhabdomyolysis; binders.
Bucket 8

Acid–Base Disorders

MechanismStepwise: pH → primary process → compensation → anion gap → Δ–Δ ratio. Compensation outside the expected range = a second disorder.
High yieldWinters: pCO₂ = 1.5(HCO₃) + 8. Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO₃), normal ~12. Δ–Δ <1 = + non-gap; 1–2 pure; >2 = + metabolic alkalosis. Urine anion gap: negative = GI loss; positive = RTA.

High Anion Gap Acidosis (MUDPILES / GOLDMARK)

DKA / alcoholic ketoacidosisβ-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate; glucose high (DKA) or low-normal (alcoholic).
MethanolFormic acid — blindness; high osmolal gap.
Ethylene glycolOxalic acid — urine oxalate crystals, AKI.
SalicylatesMixed respiratory alkalosis + AG acidosis; tinnitus.
LacticL-lactate: sepsis/shock; metformin; D-lactate: short bowel.
UremiaLow GFR can't excrete acid. Osmolal gap = measured − calculated (>10 = toxic alcohol).

Normal Anion Gap Acidosis & RTA

DiarrheaNegative UAG; low-normal K; urine pH <5.5.
Type 1 (distal)Positive UAG, hypokalemia, urine pH >5.5, stones; Sjögren, amphotericin B, lithium.
Type 2 (proximal)Negative UAG, hypokalemia; Fanconi; HCO₃ ~12–14; myeloma, acetazolamide.
Type 4Positive UAG, hyperkalemia; aldosterone deficiency/resistance (diabetes, ACE inhibitors, heparin).

Metabolic Alkalosis

Urine chloride splits it

Chloride-responsive (UCl <10–15)Vomiting, NG suction, loop diuretics — saline.
Chloride-resistant (UCl >15–20)Hyperaldosteronism, CAH (11β/17α-hydroxylase), Bartter, Gitelman, Liddle — HTN with hypoK in the aldosterone/Liddle group.

Compensation Quick Rules

RespiratoryAcute acidosis: HCO₃ ↑1 per 10 pCO₂; chronic ↑4. Acute alkalosis ↓2; chronic ↓5.
Metabolic alkalosispCO₂ = 0.7 × HCO₃ + 20.

1. Etiologies and the Henderson–Hasselbalch Equation

Acid–base disorders split into acidosis and alkalosis, each respiratory or metabolic. Metabolic acidosis splits by anion gap: high anion gap (MUDPILES) or normal anion gap (renal bicarbonate loss/RTA, or extrarenal loss such as diarrhea). Respiratory acidosis comes from decreased respiratory rate or decreased tidal volume.

Henderson–HasselbalchpH = 6.1 + log (HCO₃⁻ / (0.03 × pCO₂)). The numerator is metabolic, the denominator respiratory.
DisorderMechanismpHpCO₂HCO₃⁻Direction rule
Respiratory acidosisHypoventilation → hypercapnia↓↑(compensates ↑)pH and pCO₂ move in opposite directions
Respiratory alkalosisHyperventilation → hypocapnia↑↓(compensates ↓)Opposite directions
Metabolic acidosisOverproduction/accumulation of acid, loss of base (HCO₃⁻), or under-excretion of acid↓(compensates ↓)↓pH and HCO₃⁻ move in the same direction
Metabolic alkalosisLoss of acid or under-excretion of base↑(compensates ↑)↑Same direction

slide vs talk The narration says respiratory acidosis is caused by “hyperventilation” and respiratory alkalosis by hyperventilation leading to “hyperkalemia”; the slides state hypoventilation/hypercapnia (acidosis) and hyperventilation/hypocapnia (alkalosis).

3. Stepwise ABG Interpretation, Anion Gap and Δ–Δ Ratio

  1. pH: acidemia (<7.4) or alkalemia (>7.4).
  2. HCO₃⁻ and pCO₂: is the primary process metabolic or respiratory?
  3. Compensation: compensated (chronic), uncompensated (acute) or partially compensated.
  4. Anion gap (to classify metabolic acidosis and detect mixed disorders), then the Δ–Δ ratio if a gap exists.
  5. If metabolic, look at pCO₂ for an additional respiratory process.
  6. Does it fit the clinical picture?

4. High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (Part 1)

Three drug toxicities cause lactic acidosis: isoniazid, metformin, salicylates. Toxic alcohols (ethylene glycol, propylene glycol, methanol) raise osmolality and the osmolal gap.

Osmolal gapMeasured osmolality − (2 × Na + BUN/2.8 + glucose/18). >10 mOsm/kg is abnormal: suggests an ingested osmole (ethanol, ethylene glycol). Normal osmolality 275–295 mOsm/kg.

5. Non-Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis and RTA (Part 2)

Most often from GI bicarbonate loss (diarrhea, laxative abuse), kidney bicarbonate loss (type 2 RTA), or inability to excrete acid (type 1 and type 4 RTA; chronic kidney disease).

Urine anion gap (UAG)UAG = (urine Na⁺ + urine K⁺) − urine Cl⁻. In acidemia the kidney should make more ammonium (NH₄⁺); urine NH₄⁺ is hard to measure, so UAG is its surrogate. Chloride excretion matches NH₄⁺ to keep electrical neutrality, so high NH₄⁺ = negative UAG; low NH₄⁺ = positive UAG.

6. Metabolic Alkalosis (Part 3)

Blood pH >7.45 with elevated serum HCO₃⁻. Two categories by urine chloride.

Chloride-responsiveChloride-resistant
Urine Cl⁻<10 mEq/L (rationale slide: <15)>20 mEq/L (rationale slide: >15)
CausesGI acid loss: vomiting, NG-tube suction (lose acid, retain bicarbonate). Diuretic-induced renal acid loss (loop diuretics): volume depletion → ↑ aldosteroneEndocrine/renal tubular: hyperaldosteronism (adrenal tumor/hyperplasia), congenital adrenal hyperplasia (11β-hydroxylase, 17α-hydroxylase deficiency), Bartter, Liddle, Gitelman syndromes
TreatmentNormal saline, stop diuretics, correct low K⁺Replace K⁺; aldosterone antagonists (hyperaldosteronism); ENaC blockers (Liddle); cortisol (17α-hydroxylase deficiency)

Generation: how diuretics cause it

Volume depletion → ↑ aldosterone → α-intercalated cells secrete H⁺ into the collecting duct lumen (H⁺ loss → alkalosis). ↑ Na⁺ delivery moves Na⁺ through ENaC into principal cells, making the cell positive and forcing K⁺ into the lumen; this stimulates apical H⁺/K⁺-ATPase, and intercalated cells secrete more H⁺.

Chloride-resistant details

Hyperaldosteronism and congenital adrenal hyperplasia (11β-, 17α-hydroxylase deficiencies) give metabolic alkalosis, hypokalemia and hypertension. Bartter, Liddle, Gitelman: defective tubular Na⁺ and K⁺ handling; renal salt and water loss like diuretics; H⁺ loss in urine. Bartter mimics a loop diuretic and Gitelman a thiazide (autosomal recessive; hypokalemic alkalosis with urine Cl⁻ >15).

Maintenance

FactorEffect
Volume depletionAngiotensin II drives proximal Na⁺ retention; HCO₃⁻ retained with it; chloride depletion also helps maintain alkalosis; decreased GFR
HypokalemiaK⁺ leaves cells, H⁺ enters cells (raises blood pH); directly inhibits renal HCO₃⁻ excretion; stimulates luminal H⁺/K⁺-ATPase in the collecting duct (more H⁺ secretion)

slide vs talk The narration says hyperaldosteronism and these syndromes cause hyperkalemia and “low urine chloride <50”; the slides say hypokalemia and give the chloride cut-offs above. Saline-responsive alkalosis is tied to true hypovolemia.

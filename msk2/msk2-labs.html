Noble's Little Something Extra — Labs Info

Every lab test from the MSK II connective tissue disease lectures, condensed into a few reference tables.

Labs Overview

General inflammatory markers, the autoantibody panel, and organ-specific/muscle labs — all in one table
TestDefinitionRole in DiagnosisRole in Management
CBCMeasures red blood cells, white blood cells, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and plateletsDetects anemia (common in SLE or RA), leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, or other abnormalities suggesting autoimmune activityMonitors for disease-related complications like anemia of chronic disease or medication toxicity (e.g., from immunosuppressants)
ESRMeasures how quickly red blood cells settle in a tube, indicating inflammationElevated in active inflammation from CTDs; reflects more chronic inflammationTracks disease activity and response to treatment; levels may rise during flares
CRPA protein produced by the liver in response to inflammationIndicates acute inflammation; often elevated in RA or other CTDsMonitors flares, infection, or treatment efficacy; more specific for acute changes than ESR
CMPPanel including electrolytes, glucose, kidney function (e.g., creatinine), and liver enzymes (e.g., ALT/AST)Evaluates kidney/liver involvement (e.g., SLE nephritis) or baseline healthChecks for organ damage or drug toxicity (e.g., from methotrexate)
ANAImmunofluorescence assay detecting antibodies to nuclear components; reported as titer and pattern (e.g., speckled, homogeneous)Primary screening test for CTDs; highly sensitive (~95% of SLE) but nonspecific — follow with specific autoantibody tests if positiveLess useful for monitoring, as titers don't correlate well with activity; confirms persistence
Anti-dsDNAAntibodies targeting double-stranded DNAHighly specific for SLE; positive in 60–70% of cases, especially with active diseaseMonitors SLE activity; rising titers may predict flares, especially renal involvement
Anti-SmAntibodies to Sm nuclear proteinsHighly specific for SLE; present in 20–30% of casesNot typically used for monitoring, as levels are stable
Anti-RNPAntibodies to ribonucleoproteinsKey for MCTD diagnosis; also seen in SLE or sclerodermaHelps differentiate overlaps; monitors for complications
Anti-SSA (Ro) / Anti-SSB (La)Antibodies to SSA/Ro and SSB/La proteinsAssociated with Sjögren's syndrome and neonatal lupus; seen in SLEGuides pregnancy management in positive cases due to heart block risk
RFAntibodies (usually IgM) against the Fc portion of IgGPositive in ~70% of RA; also seen in other CTDsPredicts more aggressive RA; monitors response to therapy. Sensitive, not specific
Anti-CCPAntibodies to citrullinated proteinsHighly specific for RA; early marker, higher specificity than RFPredictive of erosive disease; levels may decrease with effective treatment
Complement (C3, C4)Measures proteins in the complement system involved in immune responseLow levels indicate consumption in active SLE or vasculitisMonitors SLE flares; normalization suggests remission
UrinalysisAnalysis of urine for protein, blood, cells, etc.Detects kidney involvement (e.g., proteinuria in lupus nephritis)Regular screening for renal damage or progression
CKEnzyme released from damaged muscleElevated in polymyositis or dermatomyositisTracks muscle inflammation and treatment response
Quick contrastsESR vs. CRP: ESR = chronic, slow to change; CRP = acute, rises/falls fast. ANA vs. anti-dsDNA/anti-Sm: ANA screens (sensitive, rule out); dsDNA/Sm confirm (specific, rule in). RF vs. anti-CCP: RF is sensitive but not specific; anti-CCP is specific for RA and predicts erosive disease.

ANA Titer Interpretation

Result type → interpretation → associations (patterns: speckled, homogeneous, mixed, nucleolar, centromere, peripheral)
Result TypeCommon InterpretationPossible Associations
NegativeNo ANAs detectedLow likelihood of autoimmune disease; investigate other causes of symptoms
Positive (Low Titer, e.g., 1:40–1:80)Mild elevation, often not significantCould be normal variation, aging, or minor infections; rarely indicates disease
Positive (Moderate Titer, e.g., 1:160–1:320)More notable, warrants further evaluationPossible autoimmune condition like SLE or Sjögren's; check patterns and symptoms
Positive (High Titer, e.g., >1:640)Strongly suggestive of autoimmunityHigh association with SLE, scleroderma, or other rheumatic diseases; urgent follow-up needed

When to Use RF and ANA

Tests help confirm or rule out a suspected condition — interpreted alongside clinical findings, other labs, and imaging
Patient PopulationClassic Picture
RASymmetrical joint pain and swelling (especially small joints like hands, wrists, feet), morning stiffness >1 hour, fatigue, fever, possible subcutaneous nodules
SLEMultisystem involvement including arthralgia/arthritis, malar (butterfly) rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, fatigue, fever, hair loss, Raynaud's phenomenon, possible renal involvement
Both tests togetherOften ordered together in initial rheumatology evaluations for nonspecific symptoms like chronic fatigue, joint issues, or cytopenias (e.g., anemia, low platelets)
CaveatPart of broader criteria (ACR/EULAR for RA, SLICC for SLE) — should not be used in isolation, as positive results occur in healthy individuals or other conditions (infections, aging). Refer to rheumatology if symptoms persist

1. Case 1: Systematic CXR & EKG Reading Approach

Case 1An 80-year-old female presents to the ED after awakening from sleep with chest pain, nausea, vomiting, and diaphoresis. Exam: diaphoretic, BP 140/80, pulse 80, RR 20, O2 sat 94% on room air, JVD present, lungs clear, irregular heart rhythm without murmurs/rubs/gallops, no clubbing/cyanosis/edema.

Systematic Chest X-Ray Reading — A Reusable Framework

Use this same sequence for every CXR you read:
StepWhat to Check
1. Technical qualityPosition/rotation, inspiration effort, over- vs. under-penetration of the film
2. Bones & soft tissueFractures, soft tissue abnormalities
3. Heart sizeNormal: <50% of total chest diameter (cardiothoracic ratio)
4. DiaphragmSharp edges expected; right hemidiaphragm normally sits higher than the left; look for free air under the diaphragm
5. PleuraCheck costophrenic angles for blunting (pleural effusion)
6. Great vesselsAorta contour; mediastinal or hilar adenopathy
7. Lung fieldsInfiltrate, nodules, masses, increased lung fluid, Kerley B lines (a sign of interstitial edema/heart failure)

Case 1 CXR Findings

Adequate position/inspiration; no fracture or soft tissue abnormality; heart size normal (<50% of chest diameter); diaphragm edges sharp, right side appropriately higher than left; no free air, no pleural effusion; great vessels normal; no infiltrate, nodules, masses, or Kerley B lines. → Normal chest X-ray.

Systematic EKG Reading — A Reusable Framework

StepWhat to Check
RhythmSinus vs. other; look for a P wave before every QRS (best seen in lead II)
RateCount over a standard interval
AxisUse an isoelectric lead as reference (e.g., if lead III is isoelectric, the axis is roughly perpendicular to lead III's orientation)
ST segmentsElevation, depression, or reciprocal changes

Case 1 EKG Findings

Normal sinus rhythm with first-degree AV block (best seen in lead II); rate ~75 bpm; axis ~30° (using lead III as the isoelectric reference; leads I and aVF upright); ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF, V5, and V6 — consistent with an inferior wall MI; reciprocal changes in V1, V2, and aVL.
Comparison EKG (Normal) A normal sinus rhythm EKG shows a P wave before every QRS (lead II), rate ~75 bpm, axis ~60° (using aVL as the isoelectric reference), and no ST segment abnormalities — contrast this directly with Case 1's inferior MI pattern above.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. On a chest X-ray, what is considered the upper limit of normal for heart size relative to total chest diameter?

Explanation: A normal heart size on chest X-ray is less than 50% of the total chest diameter (the cardiothoracic ratio); exceeding this threshold suggests cardiomegaly.

2. On a normal chest X-ray, which hemidiaphragm is expected to sit higher, and why is this relevant to systematic film reading?

Explanation: The right hemidiaphragm normally sits higher than the left, due to the liver positioned beneath it -- this is a standard checkpoint in the systematic diaphragm assessment step of CXR interpretation.

3. In Case 1, the EKG shows ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF, V5, and V6, with reciprocal changes in V1, V2, and aVL. What does this pattern indicate?

Explanation: ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF (the inferior leads), extending to V5/V6, with reciprocal ST depression in V1, V2, and aVL, is the classic EKG pattern of an inferior wall myocardial infarction.

Definitions

NSTEMINon-ST-segment elevation MI — partial loss of coronary perfusion
AnginaNo myocardial necrosis; can be stable or unstable
Acute coronary syndrome (ACS)Umbrella term encompassing unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI

Epidemiology (US)

ACS: 1.7 million hospitalizations/year — 700,000 initial acute MIs, 500,000 recurrent MIs.
Coronary artery disease causes 20% of all deaths; cardiovascular disease overall causes 40% of all deaths.

Prodromal Symptoms — Precise Breakdown

StatisticValue
Patients with acute MI who have a prodrome40–50%
Of those with a prodrome: symptoms 1–4 weeks before hospitalization1/3

Pain Characteristics

  • Variable intensity; described as squeezing, choking, vise-like, or heavy — or alternately as stabbing, knife-like, boring, or burning
  • Often radiates down the left arm; can also radiate to the shoulders, upper extremities, neck, or jaw
  • Epigastric pain can be mistaken for indigestion
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. What is the key pathophysiologic distinction between STEMI and NSTEMI?

Explanation: STEMI results from total occlusion of a coronary artery, whereas NSTEMI results from partial loss of coronary perfusion -- this distinction is reflected in the presence or absence of ST-segment elevation on EKG.

2. Among acute MI patients who experience prodromal symptoms, what fraction have symptoms for 24 hours or less before hospitalization?

Explanation: Of the 40-50% of acute MI patients who experience prodromal symptoms, one-third have symptoms for 24 hours or less before hospitalization, while two-thirds have symptoms for a week or less, and one-third have symptoms lasting 1-4 weeks.

3. A patient describes their chest discomfort as 'stabbing, knife-like, and burning' rather than the classic 'squeezing or vise-like' description. Does this description exclude acute MI as a diagnosis?

Explanation: Acute MI pain has variable character and can present as stabbing, knife-like, boring, or burning discomfort, in addition to the more classically taught squeezing, choking, vise-like, or heavy quality -- this variability should not be used to exclude MI.

3. Cardiac Troponin: Structure & the 99th Percentile URL

The Ideal Biomarker (What We're Looking For)

  • Early detection of myocardial injury; rapid, sensitive, and specific diagnosis
  • Risk stratification of ACS patients
  • Assess success of reperfusion therapy; detect re-occlusion/reinfarction
  • Determine timing and size of infarction
  • Detect procedural-related perioperative MI (cardiac or noncardiac surgery)

Cardiac Troponin — Structure

A regulatory protein complex of the myofibril, composed of three subunits, located 94–97% in the myofibril and 3–6% in the cytoplasm.
SubunitFunctionCardiac Specificity
Troponin CCalcium-binding componentPresent in both cardiac and skeletal muscle
Troponin IInhibitory componentSpecific to cardiac muscle (cTnI)
Troponin TTropomyosin-binding componentSpecific to cardiac muscle (cTnT)

The 99th Percentile Upper Reference Limit (URL) — Core Concept

The 99th percentile URL is a statistical value below which 99% of a healthy reference population's values fall — i.e., the largest value occurring 99% of the time in healthy people. It is calculated from a reference population of healthy individuals, and patients are considered to have myocardial injury when their high-sensitivity troponin exceeds this value.
Why the 99th Percentile URL Matters It is considered the gold standard for building accelerated diagnostic protocols — it provides a consistent cut point for clinical decision-making and is both analytically and clinically evidence-based (though not perfect).

Factors That Affect the URL

FactorEffect
GenderMen have higher URL levels than women
AgeURL levels are higher in elderly patients than younger patients
Assay usedURL varies by the specific assay and its reference population
Reference population representativenessThe URL may not replicate accurately if the reference population doesn't match the patient population being studied
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. Which two troponin subunits are specific to cardiac muscle, distinguishing them from troponin found in skeletal muscle?

Explanation: Troponin I (the inhibitory component) and Troponin T (the tropomyosin-binding component) are specific to cardiac muscle (cTnI and cTnT), while Troponin C (the calcium-binding component) is present in both cardiac and skeletal muscle.

2. How is the 99th percentile upper reference limit (URL) for cardiac troponin defined?

Explanation: The 99th percentile URL is a statistical concept defined as the value below which 99% of a healthy reference population's troponin values fall -- essentially the largest value occurring 99% of the time in healthy individuals, calculated from a healthy reference population.

3. Which patient factor is associated with a higher 99th percentile URL for cardiac troponin, all else being equal?

Explanation: Both male sex and older age are associated with higher 99th percentile URL values for cardiac troponin, which is why gender- and age-specific considerations can be important when interpreting results near the diagnostic cutoff.

4. Universal Diagnostic Criteria for MI Subtypes

Universal (Spontaneous) Acute MI Criteria

Detection of a rise and/or fall of cardiac biomarker values (cardiac troponin) with at least 1 value above the 99th percentile URL, PLUS at least one of:
  • Symptoms of ischemia
  • New or presumed-new significant ST-T changes, or new LBBB
  • Development of pathological Q waves on EKG
  • Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality
  • Identification of an intracoronary thrombus by angiography or autopsy

PCI-Related MI — Exact Numeric Criteria

Elevation of cTn >5× the 99th percentile URL in patients with a normal baseline (≤99th percentile URL), OR a rise in cTn >20% if baseline values are already elevated and stable/falling — PLUS one or more of:
  • Symptoms suggestive of myocardial ischemia
  • New ischemic EKG changes
  • Angiographic findings consistent with a procedural complication
  • Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality

Stent Thrombosis-Associated MI

Detected by coronary angiography or autopsy, in the setting of myocardial ischemia, with a rise and/or fall of cardiac troponin with at least 1 value above the 99th percentile URL.

CABG-Related MI — Exact Numeric Criteria

Elevation of cardiac troponin >10× the 99th percentile URL in a patient with a normal baseline (≤99th percentile URL), PLUS 1 or more of:
  • New pathological Q waves or new LBBB
  • Angiographically documented new graft or new native coronary artery occlusion
  • Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality
Board Tip — Memorize the Multipliers PCI-related MI: >5× URL (or >20% rise from an elevated baseline). CABG-related MI: >10× URL. CABG is the more invasive procedure and expected to cause more troponin leak, hence the higher threshold to call it a true infarction rather than expected procedural injury.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. A patient with a normal baseline troponin undergoes PCI. Post-procedure, troponin rises to 6 times the 99th percentile URL, and the patient has new ischemic EKG changes. Does this meet criteria for PCI-related MI?

Explanation: PCI-related MI requires troponin elevation greater than 5 times the 99th percentile URL from a normal baseline, plus at least one additional criterion such as new ischemic EKG changes, angiographic complication, symptoms, or imaging evidence -- this patient meets both components.

2. What numeric troponin threshold, relative to the 99th percentile URL, is required (from a normal baseline) to diagnose CABG-related MI?

Explanation: CABG-related MI requires troponin elevation greater than 10 times the 99th percentile URL (from a normal baseline), a higher threshold than PCI-related MI (>5x), reflecting the greater expected degree of troponin release from the more invasive CABG procedure itself.

3. Which finding, if identified by coronary angiography or autopsy in the setting of myocardial ischemia along with a rise/fall of troponin above the 99th percentile, defines stent thrombosis-associated MI?

Explanation: Stent thrombosis-associated MI is defined by detection of the stent thrombosis via coronary angiography or autopsy, occurring in the setting of myocardial ischemia, together with a rise and/or fall of cardiac troponin with at least one value above the 99th percentile URL.

5. Etiologies of Increased Cardiac Troponin

Not every troponin elevation is a "heart attack" in the classic sense — troponin rises whenever cardiac myocytes are injured, regardless of mechanism. The differential is organized into four categories:

1. Injury Related to Primary Myocardial Ischemia

  • Plaque rupture
  • Intraluminal coronary artery thrombus

2. Injury Related to Supply/Demand Imbalance

  • Tachyarrhythmia/bradyarrhythmia
  • Aortic dissection or severe aortic valve disease
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
  • Cardiogenic, hypovolemic, or septic shock
  • Severe respiratory failure; severe anemia
  • Hypertension with or without LVH
  • Coronary spasm; coronary embolism or vasculitis
  • Coronary endothelial dysfunction without significant CAD

3. Injury Not Related to Myocardial Ischemia

  • Cardiac contusion, surgery, ablation, pacing, or defibrillator shocks
  • Rhabdomyolysis with cardiac involvement
  • Myocarditis
  • Cardiotoxic agents — specifically anthracyclines and Herceptin (trastuzumab)

4. Multifactorial or Indeterminate Myocardial Injury

  • Heart failure
  • Stress (takotsubo) cardiomyopathy
  • Severe pulmonary embolism or pulmonary hypertension
  • Sepsis and critically ill patients
  • Renal failure
  • Severe acute neurological disease (stroke, subarachnoid hemorrhage)
  • Infiltrative diseases (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis)
  • Strenuous exercise
Board Tip An elevated troponin is not synonymous with "MI" — it reflects myocyte injury from any cause. Always correlate with the clinical picture and the Universal MI Criteria (Section 4) before diagnosing acute MI.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. A patient undergoing chemotherapy with an anthracycline agent develops an elevated troponin without evidence of coronary ischemia. Into which etiologic category does this fall?

Explanation: Cardiotoxic agents such as anthracyclines and Herceptin (trastuzumab) cause myocardial injury not related to ischemia, representing a distinct category of troponin elevation separate from ischemic causes.

2. A patient presenting with severe emotional stress develops acute heart failure symptoms and an elevated troponin, with a pattern of reversible apical ballooning on echocardiogram and no significant coronary disease. Which condition, listed among the multifactorial/indeterminate causes of troponin elevation, does this describe?

Explanation: Stress (takotsubo) cardiomyopathy is listed among the multifactorial/indeterminate causes of troponin elevation, classically triggered by severe emotional or physical stress and presenting with reversible wall motion abnormalities in the absence of significant coronary disease.

3. Which of the following is classified as a cause of troponin elevation from supply/demand imbalance rather than primary myocardial ischemia or a non-ischemic cause?

Explanation: Severe anemia is classified under supply/demand imbalance-related troponin elevation, since reduced oxygen-carrying capacity can create a mismatch between myocardial oxygen supply and demand, distinct from primary plaque-related ischemia or non-ischemic injury causes.

High-Sensitivity Cardiac Troponin (hs-cTn) — The Two Defining Criteria

  1. Total imprecision at the 99th percentile value should be ≤10%
  2. Measurable concentrations below the 99th percentile URL should be attainable, above the assay's limit of detection, in at least 50% of healthy individuals

hs-cTn — Practical Considerations

Case 1 ResolutionInitial cardiac troponin was normal; a repeat troponin 3 hours later was elevated. This rising pattern is consistent with an acute MI.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. A patient's second troponin value, drawn a few hours after the first, is 25% higher than the initial value and exceeds the 99th percentile. What should be suspected?

Explanation: A greater than 20% increase in the second troponin value, as long as it exceeds the 99th percentile, is the criterion used to suspect recurrent infarction in a patient being monitored for this possibility.

2. Which of the two defining criteria must a high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assay meet regarding measurable concentrations in healthy individuals?

Explanation: One of the two defining criteria for a high-sensitivity troponin assay is that measurable concentrations below the 99th percentile URL (but above the assay's limit of detection) must be attainable in at least 50% of healthy individuals -- reflecting the assay's improved lower limit of detection.

3. How does 'delta change' (the change in troponin level over serial measurements) improve the diagnostic performance of high-sensitivity troponin assays?

Explanation: Delta change -- tracking the change in troponin level over time via serial measurements -- can improve specificity to approximately 95%, helping to offset the decreased specificity that comes with the increased sensitivity of high-sensitivity troponin assays.

7. Case 2: Heart Failure & CXR Comparison (CHF vs. Pneumonia)

Case 2A 76-year-old female presents with 2 weeks of progressive shortness of breath, PND, orthopnea, and lower extremity edema. Exam: BP 135/82, pulse 80, RR 16, JVD, rales at the lung bases, S3 heart sound. Labs: Na 130 (hyponatremia), K 4, creatinine 1.

Case 2 CXR — Decompensated CHF

Cardiomegaly (heart size >50% of chest wall diameter); increased interstitial fluid markings bilaterally; increased pulmonary vasculature extending toward the apices (cephalization); Kerley B lines (horizontal lines in the lung periphery, indicating increased interstitial fluid). → Consistent with decompensated congestive heart failure.

Comparison CXR — Right Middle Lobe Pneumonia (a Different Patient)

FindingCHF (Case 2)Pneumonia (Comparison)
Heart size>50% of chest diameter<50% — normal
Pulmonary vasculatureIncreased, cephalizedNot increased
Interstitial markingsIncreasedNot increased
Kerley B linesPresentAbsent
Distinctive finding—Loss of the right heart border (silhouette sign, from adjacent consolidation) — left heart border remains clearly seen; air bronchograms visible through the area of infiltrate/consolidation
Board Tip — The Silhouette Sign Loss of a normally sharp cardiac/mediastinal border indicates that adjacent lung tissue (consolidation, in this case right middle lobe pneumonia) is in direct contact with that border, obscuring it on X-ray. Since the left heart border remains sharp, the pathology must be adjacent to the right heart border specifically.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. Which chest X-ray finding is most specific for distinguishing decompensated heart failure from pneumonia?

Explanation: Kerley B lines (indicating interstitial edema) together with cephalization of pulmonary vasculature and cardiomegaly are the findings that point toward decompensated heart failure, distinguishing it from pneumonia, which instead shows a focal infiltrate/consolidation with a normal heart size.

2. A chest X-ray shows loss of the right heart border while the left heart border remains clearly visible, along with air bronchograms in the adjacent lung. What does this pattern suggest, and what is the underlying principle?

Explanation: This describes the silhouette sign: consolidation (such as right middle lobe pneumonia) adjacent to the right heart border obscures that border on X-ray, while the unaffected left heart border remains sharp -- together with air bronchograms through the area of consolidation, this points to pneumonia rather than heart failure.

3. In Case 2, which combination of exam and lab findings supports a diagnosis of heart failure?

Explanation: The combination of JVD, rales at the lung bases, an S3 heart sound, and hyponatremia (sodium 130) together support a diagnosis of heart failure, consistent with volume overload and neurohormonal activation.

8. Natriuretic Peptides: Diagnosis of Heart Failure

Heart Failure Epidemiology

Affects 5 million Americans; incidence of approximately 400,000 new cases/year. Leading cause of hospitalization in individuals 65 years and older.

5-year mortality: ~10% in mild disease, 20–30% in moderate disease, up to 80% in end-stage disease.

The Two Natriuretic Peptide Biomarkers

BiomarkerKey Fact
BNP (B-type natriuretic peptide)Pharmacologically active
NT-proBNP (N-terminal proBNP)NOT pharmacologically active
Both are secreted from the ventricles in a pulsatile fashion, in response to increased wall stress.

Diagnostic Thresholds

ScenarioValue
Heart failure — BNPOften >1000 ng/L
Heart failure — NT-proBNPOften >1800 ng/L
LV dysfunction — both BNP and NT-proBNPOften <300 ng/L

Emergency Room Use

BNP LevelDisposition Tendency
>500 ng/LTend toward hospital admission
<300 ng/LTend toward discharge (HF unlikely)
BNP >100 ng/L as an independent predictor of heart failure: 90% sensitivity, 75% specificity, 81% accuracy. With BNP testing, diagnostic uncertainty dropped from 43% to 11%. Without BNP monitoring, diagnostic accuracy is only 74%.
Best Use: Ruling OUT Heart Failure A BNP <100 ng/L or NT-proBNP <300 ng/L rules out heart failure. Natriuretic peptides are not generally recommended for ongoing management/monitoring — their strongest use case is diagnostic rule-out at presentation.
Case 2 ResolutionThe patient's clinical presentation plus elevated BNP confirms a diagnosis of heart failure.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. Which natriuretic peptide biomarker is pharmacologically active, in contrast to its inactive counterpart?

Explanation: BNP is pharmacologically active, while NT-proBNP is not pharmacologically active -- both are secreted from the ventricles in a pulsatile fashion in response to increased wall stress, but only BNP itself exerts direct physiologic effects.

2. At what BNP level does the literature describe 90% sensitivity and 75% specificity for diagnosing heart failure, with diagnostic uncertainty dropping substantially when this cutoff is used?

Explanation: A BNP level above 100 ng/L functions as an independent predictor of heart failure with 90% sensitivity, 75% specificity, and 81% accuracy, and using this cutoff dropped diagnostic uncertainty from 43% to 11% in the emergency room setting.

3. What is considered the best clinical use for natriuretic peptide testing (BNP/NT-proBNP)?

Explanation: The best use of natriuretic peptides is ruling out heart failure: a BNP less than 100 ng/L or NT-proBNP less than 300 ng/L effectively rules out the diagnosis, whereas natriuretic peptide monitoring is not generally recommended for ongoing heart failure management.

9. Natriuretic Peptides: Non-Specific Causes & Prognosis

Natriuretic Peptides Are NOT Specific to Heart Failure

Elevated natriuretic peptide levels can also be seen in:
  • Left ventricular hypertrophy
  • Inflammatory cardiac diseases
  • Systemic arterial hypertension
  • Pulmonary hypertension
  • Acute and chronic renal failure
  • Cirrhosis
  • Hyperaldosteronism
  • Cushing syndrome
Board Tip Because of this broad differential, elevated natriuretic peptide levels must always be interpreted in the context of the full clinical picture — an elevated BNP alone does not confirm heart failure.

Hyponatremia as a Prognostic Indicator in Heart Failure

Hyponatremia is a poor prognostic indicator in heart failure.
Mechanism Low cardiac output → decreased renal blood flow → (this combination, along with resultant hypotension) → increased release of arginine vasopressin → water retention diluting serum sodium. Additionally, low cardiac output and angiotensin II mediate increased thirst, compounding the dilutional hyponatremia. Diuretic use can further contribute.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. An elevated BNP is found in a patient with known cirrhosis and no clinical signs of heart failure. What is the most appropriate interpretation?

Explanation: Natriuretic peptides can be elevated in numerous non-heart-failure conditions, including cirrhosis, renal failure, pulmonary hypertension, LVH, and hyperaldosteronism among others -- an elevated level must always be interpreted alongside the full clinical picture rather than in isolation.

2. What is the physiologic mechanism by which low cardiac output in heart failure leads to hyponatremia?

Explanation: Low cardiac output and decreased renal blood flow increase arginine vasopressin release, promoting water retention that dilutes serum sodium; additionally, low cardiac output and angiotensin II increase thirst, further compounding this dilutional hyponatremia -- explaining why hyponatremia is a poor prognostic sign in heart failure.

3. Which of the following conditions is NOT listed among the causes of natriuretic peptide elevation unrelated to heart failure?

Explanation: Acute appendicitis is not among the recognized non-heart-failure causes of natriuretic peptide elevation, unlike Cushing syndrome, hyperaldosteronism, pulmonary hypertension, LVH, inflammatory cardiac disease, systemic hypertension, cirrhosis, and renal failure.

Explanation: Using the Pooled Cohort Equation's categories (low <5%, intermediate 5-7.5%, high >7.5%), a 6% calculated risk falls into the intermediate risk category.

2. Which risk calculator directly incorporates hs-CRP as one of its input variables, and in which patient population was it originally developed?

Explanation: The Reynolds Risk Score directly incorporates hs-CRP as an input variable (along with cholesterol, HDL, systolic BP, smoking status, and parental history), and was originally developed and validated in healthy, non-diabetic men and women.

3. A patient's Pooled Cohort Equation risk is 7.4% and their Framingham Risk Score is 14.4%. What do both of these results have in common?

Explanation: Despite very different absolute percentages (7.4% vs. 14.4%), both calculators classify this patient as intermediate risk, since each tool uses its own distinct percentage thresholds for risk categories -- illustrating why it's important not to cross-apply one calculator's thresholds to another's result.

11. High-Sensitivity CRP & Atherosclerosis Risk

What Is hs-CRP?

CRP is an acute phase protein of hepatic origin, produced by the liver in response to the pro-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-1, interleukin-6, and tumor necrosis factor secreted by macrophages and T cells.

Risk Categories

Categoryhs-CRP Level
Low risk<1.0 mg/L
Average risk1.0–3.0 mg/L
High risk>3.0 mg/L
Board Tip hs-CRP needs to be repeated to demonstrate persistent low levels of inflammation before it's used to guide decisions — a single value can be a transient false elevation from an unrelated acute process.

Inflammation & Atherosclerosis

  • Inflammation is an integral component of atherosclerosis
  • Median hs-CRP in the US: 2 mg/L
  • hs-CRP is elevated in metabolic syndrome, likely secondary to inflammatory cytokines released from visceral fat

hs-CRP & Statin Therapy

Best treatment outcomes are seen when both LDL-C is treated to <70 mg/dL and hs-CRP is treated to <2 mg/L.
Key Trial Data In healthy men and women with hs-CRP >2 mg/L and LDL-C <130 mg/dL, treatment with rosuvastatin 20 mg reduced major cardiovascular endpoints by 50% and all-cause mortality by 20% — this trial demonstrated benefit from statin therapy driven by inflammation reduction, even when LDL-C was not markedly elevated to begin with.

Clinical Utility

hs-CRP independently predicts cardiovascular disease risk, but is not clinically useful in most patients. Its main value is in intermediate-risk patients (10–20% over 10 years by Framingham) where further risk assessment would change primary prevention therapy — exactly the scenario in Case 3.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. An hs-CRP level of 4.5 mg/L is reported for a patient. How is this classified according to standard risk categories?

Explanation: An hs-CRP level above 3.0 mg/L is classified as high risk, distinct from low risk (<1.0 mg/L) and average risk (1.0-3.0 mg/L).

2. In a landmark trial of healthy individuals with hs-CRP >2 mg/L and LDL-C <130 mg/dL, treatment with rosuvastatin 20 mg produced what outcome?

Explanation: This trial demonstrated that rosuvastatin 20 mg reduced major cardiovascular endpoints by 50% and all-cause mortality by 20% in healthy individuals with elevated hs-CRP but relatively low LDL-C -- supporting the concept that statins provide benefit through anti-inflammatory effects independent of marked LDL lowering.

3. For which patient population is hs-CRP testing considered most clinically useful?

Explanation: hs-CRP testing is most clinically useful in patients at intermediate cardiovascular risk, where additional risk stratification could meaningfully change the decision about whether to initiate statin therapy for primary prevention -- it is not considered clinically useful in most other patients.

YearFindingInvestigator

1913Fed pure cholesterol to rabbits, producing hypercholesterolemia and severe aortic atherosclerosisNikolai Anitschkow (Russian pathologist)
1939Described large families with an inherited trait of high blood cholesterol and premature heart attacksCarl Müller (Norwegian clinician)

Lipoprotein Fractionation & the LDL/HDL Story

John Gofman (Berkeley, 1950s) used an ultracentrifuge to separate lipoproteins by flotation — heart attacks correlated with elevated LDL, and were less frequent with high blood levels of HDL.
Ancel Keys (University of Minnesota, 1960s) conducted the Seven Countries Study: 15,000 middle-aged men followed for 10 years — the incidence of heart attacks was found to be linearly proportional to cholesterol level. This study lent support to the Mediterranean diet. (Keys was also known for his work on starvation physiology and inventing K-rations for the US military.)

Cholesterol Synthesis & the Discovery of Statins

Konrad Bloch & Feodor Lynen (1964 Nobel Prize) completed the pathway of cholesterol synthesis in the body — the rate-limiting step is the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate.
Akira Endo (Japanese microbiologist) theorized a mold/fungus could produce a natural HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor useful as an antimicrobial. After screening 3,800 different molds/fungi, he discovered compactin in a fermentation broth of Penicillium citrinum in the 1970s. Though not useful as an antibiotic, the potential of an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor as a lipid-lowering agent was quickly realized — leading to the development of statins.
📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. Which investigator used an ultracentrifuge in the 1950s to separate lipoproteins by flotation, establishing the correlation between LDL, HDL, and heart attack risk?

Explanation: John Gofman, working at Berkeley in the 1950s, used an ultracentrifuge to separate lipoproteins by flotation, discovering that heart attacks correlated with elevated LDL cholesterol and were less frequent with high HDL levels.

2. The Seven Countries Study, which followed 15,000 middle-aged men over 10 years and found a linear relationship between cholesterol level and heart attack incidence, was conducted by which researcher?

Explanation: Ancel Keys, a physiologist at the University of Minnesota, conducted the Seven Countries Study in the 1960s, demonstrating a linear relationship between cholesterol level and heart attack incidence, and lending support to the Mediterranean diet.

3. Akira Endo's discovery of compactin, a natural HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor, came from screening thousands of molds and fungi in search of what original application?

Explanation: Akira Endo originally theorized that a mold or fungus producing a natural HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor could serve as an antimicrobial agent; after screening 3,800 molds/fungi and discovering compactin, it was not useful as an antibiotic, but its potential as a lipid-lowering agent (by inhibiting the rate-limiting step of cholesterol synthesis) was then quickly recognized.

📝 Check Your Understanding (3 questions)

1. Using the Friedewald equation, if a patient's total cholesterol is 200 mg/dL, HDL is 50 mg/dL, and triglycerides are 150 mg/dL, what is the calculated LDL?

Explanation: LDL = Total cholesterol - (HDL + Triglycerides/5) = 200 - (50 + 150/5) = 200 - (50 + 30) = 200 - 80 = 120 mg/dL.

2. At what triglyceride level does the calculated LDL become inaccurate, requiring a repeat fasting lipid panel?

Explanation: When triglycerides exceed 500 mg/dL, the Friedewald equation used to calculate LDL is no longer accurate, requiring a repeat fasting lipid panel (often with direct LDL measurement) for reliable results.

3. A patient being worked up for newly diagnosed hypercholesterolemia should have which of the following tests to help rule out a secondary cause?

Explanation: TSH (thyroid disease), glucose or HbA1c (diabetes), urinalysis (proteinuria/nephrotic syndrome), and alkaline phosphatase (obstructive biliary disease) are the recommended tests to rule out secondary causes of hypercholesterolemia -- homocysteine, by contrast, is not recommended for routine screening.

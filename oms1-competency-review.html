PCM System Exam Checklists

PCM Station #1 — (1) System Exam

Abdominal
  • Inspection: Contour, scars, rashes, lesions
  • Auscultation: All 4 quadrants, aorta
  • Palpation: Light in 4 quadrants, deep in 4 quadrants, liver, spleen, aorta
  • Percussion: Lloyd sign for CVA tenderness
Cerebellar
  • Assess for dysdiadochokinesis: rapid alternating movements
  • Assess for dysmetria: finger to nose, heel down shin
  • Romberg and pronator drift: feet together, arms out, palms up
  • Assess balance: regular gait, tandem gait, toe walk, heel walk
Cranial Nerves
CNTest
IIDirect and indirect light in each eye
III, IV, VIFollow finger in H pattern
VMotor — palpate masseter while biting down. Sensory — cotton swab V1, V2, V3
VIIRaise eyebrows, close eyes tightly, smile, puff out cheeks
VIIIFinger rub / hearing test
IX, X“Say ahh” and use tongue depressor to evaluate soft palate movement
XIShrug shoulders OR have patient turn head against resistance
XIIStick out tongue and wiggle it
Cardiovascular (CV)
  • Inspection: Chest shape, color of skin (cyanosis — perioral or distal extremities)
  • Palpation: Precordium, PMI
  • Auscultation: Aortic, pulmonic, Erb's, tricuspid, bicuspid (diaphragm then bell); carotid arteries using bell and have patient hold breath
  • Bilateral pulses: Carotid, radial, tibialis posterior, dorsalis pedis
HEENT
  • Head — Inspection (4 of): hair, scalp, skull, face, skin, nasal symmetry, pinna, lobe symmetry, lips, TMJ, trachea, thyroid. Palpation: frontal & maxillary sinuses
  • Eyes — Inspection (4 of): lashes, eyebrows, eyelids, conjunctiva, sclera, pupils, cornea. Pupillary light reflex (direct/indirect). EOMs: 6 cardinal positions (“H”) AND convergence
  • Ears — Otoscopic inspection: ear tug, brace hand, verbalize external auditory canal & tympanic membrane. Finger rub/hearing test
  • Nose — Otoscopic inspection: verbalize patency, septum, turbinates, mucosa
  • Mouth — Otoscopic inspection: “Say ah”, verbalize at least 4 of: oral mucosa, teeth, tongue, gums, uvula, soft palate, floor of mouth, posterior oropharynx, tonsils
Lung
  • Inspection (verbalize all 4): chest shape/deformities, rate & depth of breathing, effort of breathing, accessory muscle use
  • Palpation: tenderness of anterior chest, tenderness of back and ribs, rib excursion posteriorly
  • Auscultation: “Deep breath through open mouth”, compare lung fields side to side — anterior (4), midaxillary (4), posterior (6)
Lymph & Thyroid
  • Lymph nodes: pre-auricular, post-auricular, occipital, tonsillar, submandibular, submental, anterior cervical, posterior cervical, supraclavicular
  • Thyroid & trachea: evaluate tracheal cartilage, palpate both lobes of thyroid, instruct patient to swallow
MSK Back, LE Exam, & PNS LE
  • Lumbar spine: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 4 — F, E, SB, Rot)
  • Hip exam: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 6 — F, E, IR, ER, Abd, Add)
  • Knee exam: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (both — F, E)
  • Ankle exam: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 4 — dorsi, plantar, invert, evert)
  • Bilateral strength: hip flexors, hip extensors, knee flexors, knee extensors, ankle dorsiflexors, ankle plantarflexors
  • Dermatomes: L2–S1
  • Bilateral reflexes: Patellar (L4), Achilles (S1)
MSK Neck, UE Exam, & PNS UE Exam
  • Cervical spine: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 4 — F, E, SB, Rot)
  • Shoulder exam: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 6 — F, E, IR, ER, Abd, Add)
  • Elbow exam: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 4 — F, E, pronate, supinate)
  • Wrist exam: inspection, palpation, active or passive ROM (all 4 — F, E, radial dev., ulnar dev.)
  • Bilateral strength: biceps, triceps, forearm flexors, forearm extensors, hands
  • Dermatomes: C5–T1
  • Bilateral reflexes: Biceps (C5), Brachioradialis (C6), Triceps (C7)

Special Tests

PCM Station #2 — (2) Special Tests

Neuro / HEENT

TestTechniquePositive Finding
WeberTuning fork to top of the headHear on one side only → do Rinne to localize. Sensorineural: Weber localizes to the unaffected ear. Conductive: Weber localizes to the affected ear
RinneTuning fork to mastoid until patient can't hear it, then move in front of ear canalHeard in front of ear after mastoid = air > bone conduction = sensorineural loss. NOT heard = bone > air conduction = conductive loss
Peripheral FieldsSit facing patient, patient covers one eye, examiner closes the same eye, test peripheral fields (superior, inferior, medial, lateral) using your own field as a guide; repeat other eyeNote any discrepancy between your field and the patient's
LOL/AMENLOL first; if all correct, AMEN not required. LOL = Level of consciousness, Orientation (person/place/time), Language (name objects). AMEN = Attention (spell WORLD backwards), Memory (3 words, recall in 3–10 min), Executive function (compare 2 objects), Non-dominant hemisphere (draw/copy a cube)Complete if patient- or family-reported memory issues
Ankle ClonusRepetitively dorsiflex the foot several times, then hold in full dorsiflexionRhythmic oscillations = CNS disease
BabinskiStroke lateral sole from heel to ball of foot with reflex hammer handle/tongue depressorGreat toe extends, other toes splay = abnormal = UMN lesion
Two-Point DiscriminationEyes closed, palms up; test finger pad for smallest distance (mm) at which 2 points are distinguishable; compare sidesNormal for finger pad = 2–4 mm
Axial Compression TestPress down on top of head — direct vertical compression, no side bend/rotationTingling down the arm (“lightning bolt”) = pinched cervical nerve

Cardiopulmonary / Abdominal

TestTechniquePositive Finding
Rovsing's SignPress deeply and evenly in the LLQ, then quickly withdraw fingersReferred pain to RLQ with pressure or withdrawal = acute appendicitis
Murphy's SignAs patient inhales, press inward/toward the gallbladder (RUQ)Pain on inspiration causing patient to stop inhaling = inflamed gallbladder
McBurney's Point1/3 the distance between ASIS and umbilicus (RLQ) — press deepDeep tenderness = acute appendicitis
Lloyd's Sign (CVAT)Firmly tap the ulnar side of the hand over the costovertebral anglePain = renal inflammation or infection
EgophonyAuscultate posterior auscultation points, ask patient to say “E”“E” sounds like “A” = abnormal
BronchophonyAuscultate posterior points, ask patient to say “99”Louder/clearer than expected = abnormal (normal = muffled)
Whispered PectoriloquyAuscultate posterior points, ask patient to whisper “1, 2, 3”Clearly heard = abnormal (normal = faint/not heard)
Tactile FremitusUlnar aspects of both hands firmly against either side of chest, patient says “Ninety-Nine”Asymmetric decreased = effusion/pneumothorax/neoplasm; asymmetric increased = pneumonia/consolidation
Liver SpanPercuss up the midclavicular line from RLQ (below umbilicus) to find lower dull border; percuss down from the nipple line to find upper dull border; measure the spanNormal 6–12 cm; larger = hepatomegaly
Diaphragmatic ExcursionPatient exhales fully and holds; percuss down medial to the scapula to mark dullness (diaphragm). Patient inhales fully and holds; percuss again and mark. Measure the distanceNormal 3–5 cm; reduced = disease
Valsalva Squat/Stand — Erb's PointAuscultate Erb's point standing, then squatting, then standing againMurmur worse squatting, better standing = MVP/mitral stenosis. Murmur worse standing after squat = hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

Shoulder

TestTechniquePositive Finding
Empty CanAbduct arm to 90°, bring forward to 30–45°, thumb pointing down; press down while patient resists upUnable to resist = supraspinatus
Drop Arm(If empty can positive) passively abduct arm to 90°, let go, ask patient to slowly lower itCan't control the descent = supraspinatus
Resisted External RotationArms adducted, flexed 90°, thumbs up; grasp wrist, patient pushes outward into ERPain or weakness = infraspinatus
Bear HugPatient crosses hand to opposite shoulder, resists examiner lifting hand off shoulderInability to resist = subscapularis
Lift-OffInternally rotate arm, dorsum of hand on low back, push posteriorly against resistanceUnable to push outward = subscapularis
AC Joint — CrossoverAdduct the arm across the chestPain = AC joint dysfunction
O'Brien'sFlex arm 90°, adduct ~10° across chest, internally rotate thumb-down, push down against resistancePain = labral tear
Speed'sArm extended, palm up, resist patient pushing upwardPain in bicipital groove = biceps
Yergason'sElbow flexed 90°, contact biceps tendon, patient externally rotates & supinates against resistancePain/snapping in bicipital groove = biceps
Apprehension Test“Surrender” position (abducted 90°, elbow flexed 90°); push gently forward on the shoulderLook of alarm = GH (shoulder) dislocation risk
Neer'sStabilize scapula posteriorly, internally rotate/pronate forearm while passively forward flexing the armPain = subacromial impingement
HawkinsShoulder & elbow flexed 90°, palm down; internally rotate via downward force on forearmPain = supraspinatus/long head biceps impingement
Roos TestArms abducted 90°, ER, elbows bent 90°; open/close hands for 2–3 minutesWeakness/numbness/tingling = thoracic outlet syndrome
Adson'sPalpate radial pulse; extend, abduct, externally rotate arm; patient turns head toward/away from tested sideRadial pulse diminishes = subclavian artery compression (TOS)

Elbow, Wrist & Hand

TestTechniquePositive Finding
Medial EpicondylitisPatient flexes wrist against resistance while palpating medial epicondylePain = golfer's elbow
Lateral EpicondylitisPatient extends wrist against resistance while palpating lateral epicondylePain = tennis elbow
Phalen's ManeuverBacks of hands together, wrists fully flexed, hold 1 minuteNumbness in first 3 digits = median nerve entrapment (carpal tunnel)
Reverse Phalen'sWrists dorsiflexed, palms together (“praying position”)Numbness in first 3 digits = median nerve entrapment (carpal tunnel)
Tinel's SignTap over the median nerve at the carpal tunnelPain/tingling = carpal tunnel
Finkelstein TestThumb gripped in fist, passively ulnar deviatePain = De Quervain's tenosynovitis
Allen TestOcclude ulnar & radial arteries simultaneously, patient pumps fist, open hand, release one arteryPalm stays pale = occlusion in that arch; repeat other hand to compare
Thumb — Ulnar Collateral LigamentStress the MCP joint of the thumb by deviating radiallyPain/laxity vs. opposite hand = UCL sprain

Hip / SI

TestTechniquePositive Finding
FABER (Patrick) TestFlexion, Abduction, External Rotation; supine, foot on opposite knee, press down on knee while stabilizing opposite ASISPain = SI joint pathology
FADIR TestFlexion, Adduction, Internal Rotation, supinePain = femoral acetabular impingement
Thomas TestSupine, flex hips (knees to chest), extend one hip — that leg should relax and drop below the tableLeg can't fully relax = iliopsoas flexion contracture
Trendelenburg TestPatient stands on one legHip drops on the raised (unsupported) side = weak gluteus medius on stance side
Straight Leg Raise (SLR)Supine, stabilize hip, lift straight leg to ~60°Pain = sciatica (central lumbar nerve impingement)

Knee

TestTechniquePositive Finding
Anterior DrawerSupine, knee flexed 90°, pull tibia forward relative to femur>2 cm forward motion = ACL tear
Posterior DrawerSupine, knee flexed 90°, push tibia posteriorly relative to femur>2 cm posterior motion = PCL tear
Lachman'sKnee flexed 15°, externally rotated; move femur/tibia in opposite directionsForward tibial translation = ACL tear
ThessalyStanding, hold examiner for balance, lift normal leg, bend affected leg ~5°, rotate back and forthMedial pain = medial meniscus tear; lateral pain = lateral meniscus tear
McMurrayMedial: adduct thigh + externally rotate tibia (valgus), move to extension. Lateral: abduct thigh + internally rotate tibia (varus), move to extensionPain = meniscus tear (medial or lateral, per maneuver)

Ankle

TestTechniquePositive Finding
Talar TiltATFL: plantarflex + invert. CFL: invert in neutral. PTFL: dorsiflex + invertPain = tear/instability of that ligament
Ankle Anterior DrawerSeated at table edge; grip calcaneus and lower tibia; pull calcaneus forward while pushing tibia posterior>5 mm displacement or pain = ATFL instability

Cervical, Ribs, Thoracic & Lumbar

OMM Station #1 — (1) Treatment Setup

Cervical

Supine Cervical Muscle Energy
  1. Localize the segment: index fingers on the articular pillars of the diagnosis, middle fingers on the articular pillars monitoring the segment below
  2. Bring the patient into the complete opposite of the diagnosis, to the feather edge of the barrier
  3. Instruct patient to bring head back to center and resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Supine Cervical HVLA

Localize the segment: index fingers on the articular pillars of the diagnosis, middle fingers on the articular pillars monitoring the segment below.

Rotational
  1. Bring patient into the barrier in flexion or extension and rotation
  2. Bring them into the ease in sidebending
  3. Deep breath; on exhalation, follow the barrier and deliver a quick short thrust anterior and superior. Return to neutral. Reassess
Translational
  1. Bring patient into the barrier in flexion or extension and sidebending
  2. Bring them into the ease in rotation
  3. Deep breath; on exhalation, follow the barrier and deliver a quick short thrust lateral to medial. Return to neutral. Reassess

Ribs

Supine Muscle Energy — Rib
Inhaled Ribs 2–4
  1. Cephalad hand contacts anterosuperior surface of rib 4, pushing inferiorly to the feather edge
  2. Caudal hand pulls the inferior surface of rib 4 superiorly to the feather edge of the barrier
  3. Patient exhales maximally and holds; induce inferior motion of anterior rib and superior motion of posterior rib
  4. Patient inhales as you resist superior motion of anterior rib. Repeat 3–5x. Return to neutral and reassess
Inhaled Ribs 4–10
  1. Caudal hand contacts superolateral surface of rib 10, pushing inferior to the feather edge
  2. Cephalad hand flexes and sidebends the patient toward the dysfunction
  3. Patient exhales maximally and holds; induce inferior motion of lateral rib 10
  4. Patient inhales as you resist superior motion of rib 10. Repeat 3–5x. Return to neutral and reassess
Exhaled Ribs 3–5 or 6–10
  1. Cephalad hand abducts the patient's arm to the feather edge of the barrier
  2. Caudal hand monitors superior-posterior aspect of rib 3 (or 6), provides inferior traction to the feather edge
  3. Patient inhales maximally and holds; induce inferior motion of posterior rib
  4. Patient adducts, trying to bring elbow to opposite hip. Repeat 3–5x. Return to neutral and reassess
Supine HVLA Rib — Exhaled Ribs 6–10
  1. Stand on the side opposite the diagnosis
  2. Cephalad hand pulls the patient into flexion and sidebend toward you
  3. Caudal hand thenar eminence on superior-posterior aspect of key rib 6; flex and sidebend left to approximate force vector at rib 6
  4. Deep breath; on exhalation, follow the barrier, deliver a quick thrust posteriorly. Return to neutral and reassess

Thoracic

Seated Thoracic Muscle Energy (T3 and above)
  1. Cephalad hand on top of patient's head, position head into the feather edge barrier
  2. Caudal hand monitoring transverse processes
  3. Patient brings head back to center, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Supine Thoracic Muscle Energy (T4 and below — same as lumbar)
  1. Patient overlaps arms and sits on the side of the direct barrier facing opposite the physician
  2. Cephalad hand reaches around the patient, rests on the opposite shoulder to control motion
  3. Caudal hand monitors transverse processes as you move into the feather edge of the barrier
  4. Patient brings head back to center, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Supine Thoracic HVLA
  1. Patient supine, crosses arms with the arm on top on the side of diagnosis (ease); stand on the side opposite the diagnosis
  2. Cephalad hand pulls patient into flexion and sidebend into the barrier
  3. Caudal hand thenar eminence posterior to the transverse process; flex, rotate into barrier, and sidebend into barrier
  4. Deep breath; on exhalation, follow the barrier, deliver a quick short thrust posteriorly. Return to neutral

Lumbar

Seated Lumbar Muscle Energy (same as mid-lower thoracic)
  1. Patient overlaps arms and sits on the side of the direct barrier facing opposite the physician
  2. Cephalad hand reaches around the patient, rests on the opposite shoulder to control motion
  3. Caudal hand monitors transverse processes as you move into the feather edge of the barrier
  4. Patient brings head back to center, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Side Lying Lumbar Muscle Energy
  1. Patient lies on the side of the diagnosis (ease); stand facing patient
  2. Cephalad hand pulls lumbar spine toward you to induce extension, or brings knees toward you to induce flexion
  3. Do-si-do with cephalad hand to rotate the patient; use caudal hand to monitor for rotation at transverse processes
  4. Patient gently pushes right shoulder into your hand, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Lumbar HVLA
  1. Patient lies on the side of the diagnosis (ease); stand facing the patient
  2. Cephalad hand monitors transverse process at the level of diagnosis
  3. Caudal hand flexes patient's legs (if flexed dysfunction) or upper body (if extended) until monitoring hand feels motion
  4. Caudal hand now monitors rotation at the level of diagnosis while cephalad hand do-si-dos with the patient's arm to bring them into rotation
  5. Caudal hand flexes patient's top leg so the foot rests in the popliteal space; brace their leg with your leg
  6. Bring your center of gravity over your contacts; patient takes a deep breath
  7. On exhalation, follow the barrier and deliver a quick short thrust anterior and superior

Pelvis & Sacrum — Muscle Energy

OMM Station #1 — (1) Treatment Setup

Pelvis Muscle Energy

Posterior Rotated Innominate
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side facing the feet
  2. Patient moves to the edge of the table with their SI joint off the edge, dropping the leg off the table
  3. Cephalad hand contacts the contralateral ASIS to stabilize the pelvis
  4. Caudal hand extends the patient's thigh to the feather edge
  5. Patient gently brings thigh toward the ceiling into your hand, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Anterior Rotated Innominate
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Cephalad hand flexes patient's hip to the feather edge, rotating the innominate posteriorly
  3. Caudal hand contacts ischial tuberosity, pulling it inferiorly to guide posterior rotation of the innominate
  4. Patient gently pushes knee into your hand, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Superior Innominate Shear
  1. Supine, stand at the foot of the table
  2. Abduct and internally rotate the leg to localize to the SI joint
  3. Apply traction to the leg until the feather edge
  4. Patient gently pulls/hikes hip up toward shoulder, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Innominate Inflare
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Patient flexes knee, foot flat on table
  3. Cephalad hand contacts contralateral ASIS to stabilize the pelvis
  4. Caudal hand induces external rotation of the hip by pulling the knee laterally to the feather edge
  5. Patient gently pushes knee medially into your hand, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess

Pubic Muscle Energy

Superior Pubic Shear
  1. Supine; patient moves to the edge of the table with the SI joint still on the table, drops the leg off the table (foot off the floor)
  2. Cephalad hand contacts the contralateral ASIS to stabilize the pelvis
  3. Caudal hand abducts and extends the patient's thigh to the feather edge
  4. Patient gently lifts thigh anterior and medial into your hand, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess

Sacrum Muscle Energy

L on L Sacral Torsion — Up, Up, Up
  1. Side lying with L side up (axis up)
  2. Stand in front of patient; caudal hand monitors R sacral base, cephalad hand rotates trunk to the left (face up)
  3. Switch monitoring hand to cephalad on R sacral base. Caudal hand flexes patient's thighs and pelvis until posterior motion is felt at R sacral base. Lift the ankles toward the ceiling to the feather edge (physician force up)
  4. Patient gently brings ankles down toward the table, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
R on R Sacral Torsion — Up, Up, Up
  1. Side lying with R side up (axis up)
  2. Stand in front of patient; caudal hand monitors L sacral base, cephalad hand rotates trunk right until L5 begins to rotate right (face up)
  3. Switch monitoring hand to cephalad on L sacral base. Caudal hand flexes patient's thighs and pelvis until posterior motion is felt at L sacral base. Lift the ankles toward the ceiling to the feather edge (physician force up)
  4. Patient gently brings ankles down toward the table, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
R on L Sacral Torsion — Down, Up, Down
  1. Side lying with R side up (L axis down)
  2. Stand in front of patient; caudal hand monitors R sacral base, cephalad hand pulls patient's arm anterior and caudal, inducing R rotation and L sidebending of spine (patient face up)
  3. Switch monitoring hand to cephalad on R sacral base. Caudal hand extends patient's thighs and pelvis until anterior motion is felt at R sacral base. Bring the top leg off the table, bent, to the feather edge (physician force down)
  4. Patient gently brings knee/ankle up toward the ceiling, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
L on R Sacral Torsion — Down, Up, Down
  1. Side lying with L side up (R axis down)
  2. Stand in front of patient; caudal hand monitors L sacral base, cephalad hand pulls patient's arm anterior and caudal, inducing L rotation and R sidebending of spine (patient face up)
  3. Switch monitoring hand to cephalad on L sacral base. Caudal hand extends patient's thighs and pelvis until anterior motion is felt at L sacral base. Bring the top leg off the table, bent, to the feather edge (physician force down)
  4. Patient gently brings knee/ankle up toward the ceiling, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess

Upper & Lower Extremity — ME / HVLA

OMM Station #1 — (1) Treatment Setup

Upper Extremity — Muscle Energy

Clavicle — Anterior Clavicular Head
  1. Supine, stand opposite the dysfunction
  2. Caudal hand under the scapula, contacting the medial border
  3. Engage feather edge by standing more erect until the scapula is slightly off the table
  4. Cephalad hand index/middle fingers over the SC joint to monitor motion
  5. Patient gently pulls shoulder back toward the table (retract), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Clavicle — Superior Clavicular Head
  1. Supine, slightly off-center toward the dysfunctional side, arm dropped off the table
  2. Stand on the side of dysfunction; caudal hand grasps ipsilateral wrist, engage feather edge by extending and internally rotating (clavicle head should move inferiorly)
  3. Cephalad hand index/middle fingers over the SC joint to monitor
  4. Patient gently brings arm up toward the ceiling, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Ulna — Abducted Ulna
  1. Seated, arm flexed and supinated
  2. Stand in front; engage feather edge with lateral force on the olecranon (index finger) and medial force on the distal forearm (into adduction)
  3. Patient brings forearm out/away from body (abduct), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Ulna — Adducted Ulna
  1. Seated, arm flexed and supinated
  2. Stand in front; engage feather edge with medial force on the olecranon (index finger) and lateral force on the distal forearm (into abduction)
  3. Patient brings forearm in/toward body (adduct), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Radial Head — Posterior Radial Head
  1. Seated, arm flexed; hand opposite the diagnosis holds patient's hand in a handshake, index fingers on the distal radius
  2. Supinate the forearm to engage feather edge; physician thumb acts as fulcrum, applying anterior-directed force to the radial head
  3. Other hand around the forearm, thumb on the posterior aspect of the radial head
  4. Patient gently pronates (palm down), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Radial Head — Anterior Radial Head
  1. Seated, arm flexed; hand opposite the diagnosis holds patient's hand in a handshake, index fingers on the distal radius
  2. Pronate the forearm to engage feather edge; physician thumb acts as fulcrum, applying posterior-directed force to the radial head
  3. Other hand around the forearm, thumb on the anterior aspect of the radial head
  4. Patient gently supinates (palm up), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess

Lower Extremity — Muscle Energy

Hip — Extended Hip (resists flexion)
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Cephalad hand on ipsilateral hip; use body and caudal arm to flex leg to the feather edge, leg on your shoulder
  3. Patient gently pushes hip into extension (press down while you press up), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Hip — Flexed Hip (resists extension)
  1. Prone, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Cranial hand fist contacts the ischial tuberosity with your knuckles to stabilize
  3. Extend the hip with the caudal hand, cradling the knee; foot stabilized by physician's axilla
  4. Engage feather edge by further extending the hip
  5. Patient gently pushes hip into flexion, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Hip — Externally Rotated Hip (resists internal rotation)
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Flex hip and knee to 90°; cephalad hand on knee, caudal hand on ankle
  3. Brace the knee, engage feather edge by pulling the ankle laterally (induces internal rotation); fine-tune with adduction
  4. Patient gently pushes foot medially, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Hip — Internally Rotated Hip (resists external rotation)
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Flex hip and knee to 90°; cephalad hand on knee, caudal hand on ankle
  3. Brace the knee, engage feather edge by moving the foot medially (induces external rotation)
  4. Patient gently pushes foot laterally, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Hip — Adducted Hip (resists abduction)
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Cephalad hand stabilizes ASIS
  3. Caudal hand abducts the leg; stand between the patient's leg and the table
  4. Engage feather edge by further abducting the leg
  5. Patient gently adducts leg, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Hip — Abducted Hip (resists adduction)
  1. Prone, stand on the side opposite the dysfunction
  2. Caudal hand adducts the leg across midline while cephalad hand stabilizes the ischial tuberosity
  3. Engage feather edge of the barrier
  4. Patient gently abducts hip, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Fibula — Anterior Fibular Head
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Cephalad thumb on the anterior fibular head, caudal hand on the posterior aspect of the lateral malleolus
  3. Engage feather edge by drawing the lateral malleolus anteriorly (supinate ankle — plantarflex, adduct, invert) and apply a posteromedial force to the fibular head
  4. Patient pronates the foot (dorsiflex, abduct, evert) pushing up and out, resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Fibula — Posterior Fibular Head
  1. Supine, stand on the dysfunctional side
  2. Caudal thumb on the anterior lateral malleolus, engage feather edge by pushing it posteriorly while pronating the foot
  3. Cephalad index finger on the posterior fibular head, applying anterolateral force
  4. Patient supinates the foot (pull down and in), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess
Lateral Malleolus — Anterior Lateral Malleolus
  1. Standing at the foot of the table, patient supine
  2. Bilateral thumbs on the anterior lateral malleolus, fingers wrapped around the ankle/foot; engage feather edge by inducing dorsiflexion with your thigh/abdomen and apply gentle posterior force to the lateral malleolus
  3. Patient gently pushes foot down into your thigh/abdomen (plantarflex), resist 3–5 sec, relax 1–2 sec, re-engage feather edge, repeat 3–5x, return to neutral and reassess

Lower Extremity — HVLA

Talus — Anterior Talus
  1. Standing at the foot of the table, patient supine
  2. 5th digits on the anterior/superior aspect of the talus, fingers interlaced around the dorsum of the foot, thumbs on the sole providing dorsiflexion to the restrictive barrier; slight inversion/eversion can further localize; induce traction by leaning back
  3. Deep breath; on exhalation, follow the barrier and deliver a quick thrust inferior and posterior in a “J” motion. Return to neutral and reassess

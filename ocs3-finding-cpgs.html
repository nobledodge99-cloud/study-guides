00. Overview & Learning Objectives

  • Discover organizations that produce clinical practice guidelines
  • Explore available resources that contain clinical practice guidelines
  • Demonstrate the use of information technology to access clinical practice guidelines
Practice Question

01. Organizations That Produce CPGs: NIH

  • The National Institutes of Health (NIH) is composed of 27 institutes and centers, each with its own research agenda, often focused on particular diseases and body systems
  • The NIH is the primary federal agency for conducting and supporting medical research
  • To find guidelines, you typically select the specific institute first (e.g., the National Cancer Institute), then search within that institute's own site
Practice Questions

02. Organizations That Produce CPGs: ACP, HSTAT & VA/DoD

American College of Physicians (ACP)

Produces evidence-based guidelines on many topics relating to internal medicine, with the goal of informing clinicians of the best available evidence, offering advice when there is no evidence, and helping clinicians deliver the best health care possible.

Health Services/Technology Assessment Text (HSTAT)

A free, searchable collection of large, full-text clinical practice guidelines, technology assessments, quick-reference guides for clinicians, consumer health brochures, evidence reports, and health information.

VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guidelines

The Veterans Health Administration, in collaboration with the Department of Defense, has been developing clinical practice guidelines since the early 1990s. In 2010, the Institute of Medicine identified the VA and DoD as leaders in clinical practice guideline development.

03. Other Guideline-Producing Organizations

  • Specialist organizations, such as the American Psychological Association
  • International organizations, such as the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in England
Because guidelines are produced by so many different organizations, they can be scattered across numerous resources — which can sometimes make finding a specific guideline a challenge.
Practice Question

04. Access Considerations: Where & How to Find Guidelines

  • Some guidelines are freely available; others are behind paid services such as databases, journal subscriptions, or professional organization memberships
  • Guidelines may be published in professional journals, posted on health agency or professional organization websites, or embedded in point-of-care tools
  • Several factors can affect which resources you're able to access to find guidelines:
    • What point you're at in your medical education — internship, residency, clerkship, or fellowship
    • Where you practice medicine long-term — working for a health care system versus private practice
Practice Question

05. Finding CPGs at the NIH

  1. Select the specific institute you're interested in — browse the full list of NIH institutes and centers, or use the Quick Links if you know the institute's acronym
  2. Example: the National Cancer Institute
  3. Once on the institute's page, use its internal search box:
    • Search simply for "clinical practice guidelines" to see a list of all guidelines available at that site
    • Or search a specific topic plus the word "guidelines" to narrow the results
  4. Select a guideline of interest from the results list to review its full information
Practice Question

06. Finding CPGs at the American College of Physicians

  • The ACP website has a Clinical Guidelines and Recommendations page under Clinical Information
  • You can select a topic of interest directly, or use the search feature at the top of the page
  • Example: searching "appropriate antibiotic use" surfaces best practice advice as well as patient materials
  • The full text of the best practice advice contains the complete guidance on that topic
Practice Question

07. Finding CPGs at VA/DoD

  • VA/DoD has a site dedicated specifically to clinical practice guidelines, including an introductory video with additional detail
  • Guidelines are broken up into major topic areas via a dedicated tab
  • An archive page holds any CPGs that have been retired
  • A "What's New" page shows updated guidelines and the newest published guidelines
  • Links to additional CPG resources are also provided, along with a search box at the top of the page
  • Selecting a topic provides: the full report, a provider summary, a reference card, and sometimes even pocket cards
Practice Questions

08. ECRI Guidelines Trust

  • One of the best free resources for searching widely for practice guidelines — you can create an account, search for guidelines, and view original source documentation
  • ECRI outlines specific inclusion criteria required for a guideline to be added to the Guidelines Trust
  • Search by keyword, or browse by clinical area (e.g., Allergy and Immunology)
  • Each result shows: the title, a guideline ID number, publication date, the publishing group, and where to access the full information
  • Left-side limiters allow filtering by: most recently published, publishing organization, patient age (pediatric vs. adult), additional clinical areas, or specific interventions
  • Identifiers on each guideline indicate whether a systematic review was conducted, whether evidence quality was rated, and the strength of the recommendations
  • Clicking a guideline's title opens the full text with complete access to all its information

09. PubMed

PubMed was developed by the National Center for Biotechnology Information at the National Library of Medicine, providing access to bibliographic information and publisher-supplied citations.

  1. Search your topic first (e.g., "bipolar disorder")
  2. Scroll to the bottom of the left-hand filters and select Additional filters
  3. Under Article Type, scroll down to find Practice Guideline and click Show — this adds it as a filter option
  4. Apply the Practice Guideline filter to limit your results specifically to practice guidelines
  5. Results show the total number of results, title, authors, and where each was published; you can further limit by publication date to see the most recent guidelines
  6. Clicking a title opens the abstract, additional information, and a link to the full text
Practice Question

10. ClinicalKey

  • A comprehensive online clinical information resource including textbooks, journals, drug information, practice guidelines, and other resources — includes guideline updates
  • The homepage has a section dedicated specifically to clinical practice guidelines; clicking it lets you browse all listed guideline titles
  • You can also search by topic/keyword, or use the specialty limiters on the left
  • Example: searching "hypertension," then selecting "guidelines" as the result type
  • Results show the title, a link to download the full-text PDF, publication venue, authors, and publication date — with further limits available by specialty and date
  • Opening an article provides a table of contents for that guideline
Practice Question

11. DynaMed

  1. Enter keywords into the search box at the top of the page (e.g., "autism spectrum disorders")
  2. This opens the overall topic guide compiled by DynaMed on that subject
  3. In the left navigation, go to Guidelines and Resources to find the clinical practice guidelines
  4. Results can be further limited by location/region
Practice Question

12. UpToDate

UpToDate provides evidence-based, point-of-care clinical decision support, including clinical overviews, drug monographs and interactions, graphics and calculators, and clinical decision-making pathways. It can also point clinicians to numerous links for practice guidelines.

  1. Start with the search box and type in your topic of interest
  2. Select an article on that topic
  3. In the left navigation, scroll down to Society Guideline Links
  4. This shows all society guideline links related to your topic — often including international guidelines and guides from multiple countries
Example: A search on diabetes reveals multiple society guideline links relating to different diabetes-related conditions, plus links like the latest "Standards of Care in Diabetes" — complete with editorial board information and a revision history.
Practice Questions

13. MEDLINE Complete

A full-text database of biomedical and health journals, offering access to over 1,500 active, full-text journals with comprehensive cover-to-cover indexing — a valuable research tool for physicians, healthcare practitioners, and scholars.

To search: start from the Advanced Search screen and type in your subject of interest (e.g., "pain management").

Practice Question

14. TRIP Database

TRIP stands for "Turning Research Into Practice" — a clinical search engine designed to help users quickly and easily find and use high-quality research evidence to support their practice. It has both free and paid versions; you can create a free account or simply use the search feature directly.

  1. Search a topic of interest (e.g., "cardiomyopathy") to get a full results list
  2. Use the Guidelines filter in the left navigation to limit results specifically to clinical practice guidelines
  3. Results show the title, country of publication, publication date, and the society that published it
  4. Each result also indicates where the evidence falls on the evidence pyramid, along with a quality/evidence-based rating scale
  5. Clicking the title opens the full guide
Practice Questions

15. Guidelines International Network (GIN) & WHO

Guidelines International Network (GIN Library)

  • Contains links to guidelines published or endorsed by GIN member organizations across the globe, in many languages
  • Also contains a registry of planned guidelines and guidelines currently in development
  • As an international library, you can apply filters (including language) via the left navigation
  • Results show: the title, MeSH terms, the guideline contact, scope of publication, country of application, publication status, languages, and publication year

World Health Organization (WHO)

  • Developing global guidelines that ensure the appropriate use of evidence is one of the core functions of the WHO
  • Recommendations that can impact health policies or clinical interventions are considered "guidelines" for WHO's purposes
  • WHO has a dedicated guidelines page — browse the most recent guidelines, or search a topic of interest (adding the term "guideline" to your search can help narrow results)
Practice Questions

16. Trusted Professional Websites (MedlinePlus)

  • MedlinePlus maintains a list of trusted professional websites, organized by topic, linking to organizations that provide information on that topic
  • Example: searching "abdominal pain" points you toward the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)
  • Many professional organization sites have a dedicated guidelines page accessible directly from their navigation — the ACG's guidelines page, for example, includes published guidelines, guidelines in progress, and new guidelines, sometimes with an accompanying podcast alongside the downloadable guideline content

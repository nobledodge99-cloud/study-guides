1. Overview: Objectives and How This Guide Is Built

Learning objectives

  1. Understand the difference between normal and pathological cardiac ultrasound images.
  2. Apply the four transthoracic views to image the heart.
  3. Recognize the four chambers and four valves in cardiac images.
  4. Identify pericardial effusion and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) with B-mode.
  5. Assess heart function (left ventricular ejection fraction, LVEF) with M-mode.
  6. Assess mitral and aortic valve regurgitation and stenosis with B-mode, color Doppler and pulsed Doppler.
  7. Identify pneumothorax (M-mode) and pulmonary edema (B-mode).

Direction depends on the viewRed and blue only say toward or away from the transducer, not “forward” or “backward.” Blood flowing toward the apex-placed probe is red; blood flowing away is blue. So the same jet can change color between views. Always ask: where is the probe, and which way is the jet going? handout

3. The Four Transthoracic Views

Four standard windows (the “cardiac point-of-care ultrasound” set; a fifth, the inferior vena cava (IVC) view, is often added): parasternal long axis (PLAX), parasternal short axis (PSAX), apical four-chamber (A4C), and subxiphoid (subcostal). supplemented

Where the probe goes for each window
ViewProbe position and markerBest for
1. Parasternal long axis (PLAX)Left sternal border, about 3rd–4th intercostal space; perpendicular to the chest; marker toward the right shoulderLV, LA, aortic root and valve, mitral valve; wall thickness (LVH); M-mode; pericardial vs pleural effusion; aortic outflow handout (handout: use PLAX and apical for the aortic outflow tract)
2. Parasternal short axis (PSAX)Same position; rotate the probe 90° (marker toward the left shoulder)LV as a round “donut” (global function, wall thickness, effusion); at the base the aortic valve (“Mercedes-Benz” sign)
3. Apical four-chamber (A4C)Apex, about the 5th intercostal space midclavicular line; marker toward the patient’s left; left lateral decubitus and arm abducted helps; angle toward the baseAll four chambers: compare sizes; mitral and tricuspid valves; color Doppler of mitral flow handout
4. Subxiphoid (subcostal)Below the xiphoid; knees bent, deep breath held; marker toward the patient’s left; shallow angle; depth 16–24 cmPericardial effusion (especially anterior), RV free wall, a window when the chest wall is hard to image
Image displayWhich side of the screen the left ventricle appears on depends on the machine’s convention (cardiology vs emergency/point-of-care). Orient by anatomy, not left-right: the apex or probe is at the top, the atria are deepest.

4. Recognizing the Four Chambers and Four Valves

Chambers: right atrium (RA), right ventricle (RV), left atrium (LA), left ventricle (LV). Valves: mitral (LA→LV), aortic (LV→aorta), tricuspid (RA→RV), pulmonary (RV→pulmonary artery). handout

5. Pericardial Effusion and Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (B-mode)

Left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH)

ItemContent
NormalThe LV wall is thicker than the RV wall; LV wall and intraventricular septum <12 mm handout
LVHWall thickness >12 mm; best seen in parasternal long or short axis; measured with M-mode or B-mode handout
How to measureMeasure septum and posterior wall at end-diastole, perpendicular to the wall. ASE normal is stricter than the handout: 0.6–1.0 cm (men), 0.6–0.9 cm (women); ≥1.1 cm is abnormal supplemented
PatternConcentric LVH: thick walls and a small cavity (relative wall thickness = 2 × posterior wall ÷ LV diastolic diameter >0.42) supplemented
Why it mattersChronic pressure overload (hypertension, aortic stenosis) thickens the LV, which can be seen on the same view you use for the valves supplemented

6. Heart Function: LVEF and M-Mode

Ejection fraction (handout)

  • Ejection fraction (EF) = the proportion of blood ejected from the left ventricle in one cardiac cycle; normal is about 55%. handout
  • Decreased EF = the heart is no longer efficient at pumping, as in heart failure. handout
  • Increased EF: the handout links it to a hypertrophied heart with a greater ability to deliver blood, seen with hypertension and atherosclerotic disease where afterload is high. handout
Reference ranges supplementedASE: normal LVEF 52–72% (men), 54–74% (women); mildly reduced 41–51/53%; moderately 30–40%; severely <30%. At the bedside: hyperdynamic >70%, normal 55–70%, depressed <55%. A hyperdynamic EF is also seen with hypovolemia, vasodilation and tachycardia.

What M-mode does (handout)

M-mode assesses wall, septum and valve motion to diagnose problems with cardiac output and valves, displaying movement as multiple straight lines; time runs along the x-axis. handout

Measuring EF with M-mode supplemented

MethodHowNormal / abnormal
Fractional shortening (FS)In PLAX or mid-PSAX place the M-mode line perpendicular to the LV at the mitral leaflet tips. Measure the LV internal diameter in diastole (LVIDd) and systole (LVIDs). FS = (LVIDd − LVIDs) ÷ LVIDdNormal about 25–45%; EF ≈ FS × 2
TeichholzConvert the diameters to volumes (V = 7 ÷ (2.4 + D) × D³); EF = (EDV − ESV) ÷ EDVThe machine calculates it
E-point septal separation (EPSS)M-mode through the anterior mitral leaflet in PLAX: the shortest distance between the leaflet (E-point) and the septum in early diastole<7 mm normal; >7 mm = reduced EF; >18 mm roughly EF <30%. Unreliable with mitral stenosis, aortic regurgitation
Pitfalls supplementedM-mode samples one line, so a regional wall motion abnormality (for example, after a myocardial infarction) or a line that is not perpendicular to the LV will mislead you; use B-mode views too.

7. Mitral Valve: Regurgitation and Stenosis

The mitral valve separates the left atrium from the left ventricle. In diastole the LA pressure opens it and blood fills the LV; in systole it stays closed so flow goes out the aorta (a competent valve). handout

Mitral regurgitation (MR)

ItemContent
What happensFilling is normal in diastole, but in systole the leaflets cannot stay closed against LV pressure, so blood flows backward from the LV into the LA handout
MurmurHolosystolic (pansystolic) murmur at the apex, radiating to the axilla supplemented
B-mode / M-modeLeaflets fail to coapt; a flail or prolapsing leaflet; M-mode can assess valve motion handout
Color DopplerReversal of flow during systole from LV to LA: a blue (away from an apical probe), usually mosaic jet in the LA handout. Image it in the apical four-chamber view, where normal flow is unidirectional handout
SeverityVena contracta (narrowest jet neck) <0.3 cm mild; 0.3–0.7 moderate; ≥0.7 cm severe; central jet >50% of LA area; pulmonary vein systolic flow reversal (PW Doppler); flail leaflet supplemented

Mitral stenosis (MS) supplemented

ItemContent
What happensThe valve opens poorly, so LA → LV flow in diastole is obstructed; most often rheumatic
MurmurOpening snap followed by a low-pitched mid-diastolic rumble at the apex
B-modeHockey-stick (doming) of the anterior mitral leaflet in diastole, thickened restricted leaflets, “fish-mouth” orifice in PSAX, enlarged LA
DopplerContinuous-wave through the mitral valve in A4C: mean gradient and pressure half-time (PHT); mitral valve area ≈ 220 ÷ PHT. Planimetry (tracing the orifice) is the reference method
SeverityValve area >1.5 mild, 1.0–1.5 moderate, <1.0 cm² severe; mean gradient >10 mmHg severe (current US guidelines call ≤1.5 cm² severe)

8. Aortic Valve: Regurgitation (Insufficiency) and Stenosis

The aortic valve stays closed in diastole and opens in systole when LV pressure forces it open, sending blood into the aortic root (the LV outflow tract). handout

Aortic regurgitation / insufficiency (AR)

ItemContent
What happensThe valve opens normally in systole but cannot close in diastole, so ejected blood flows back into the LV handout
MurmurEarly diastolic decrescendo murmur at the left sternal border (wide pulse pressure) supplemented
Color DopplerBidirectional flow across the aortic valve: forward in systole, backward in diastole. Use the apical and parasternal long views to see the outflow tract handout
SeveritySevere: vena contracta >0.6 cm, jet width ≥65% of the LV outflow tract, pressure half-time <200 ms, holodiastolic flow reversal in the descending aorta (PW), LV dilation when chronic supplemented
M-mode cautionCan cause premature mitral valve closure (acute AR) and makes EPSS unreliable supplemented

Aortic stenosis (AS) supplemented

ItemContent
What happensThe valve opens poorly in systole: obstruction between the LV and aorta; calcified, thickened leaflets with reduced opening; leads to LVH
MurmurCrescendo–decrescendo systolic ejection murmur at the right upper sternal border, radiating to the carotids
B-modeThick bright (calcified) leaflets that open poorly in PLAX and PSAX; bicuspid valve seen in PSAX (two cusps)
DopplerContinuous-wave across the valve (apical views): peak velocity and mean gradient; pressure gradient = 4 × velocity²
SeveritySevere: peak velocity >4 m/s, mean gradient >40 mmHg, valve area <1.0 cm²; moderate 3.0–3.9 m/s and 20–39 mmHg; mild 2.0–2.9 m/s and <20 mmHg
Stenosis vs regurgitation in one lineStenosis = obstruction (high velocity jet, measured with CW Doppler); regurgitation = leak (backward jet in the wrong phase, seen with color Doppler): MR leaks in systole, AR leaks in diastole.

9. Lung Ultrasound: Normal Lung and Pneumothorax (M-mode)

The lung is full of air, which cannot be imaged, so you see only the pleura and its artifacts. About 99% of the beam is reflected at the tissue–air interface. handout lung article

Pneumothorax

ItemContent
What it isAir in the pleural space that cannot escape (often trauma), which can compress and collapse the lung handout
M-mode findingLoss of pleural movement shows as horizontal lines all the way down: the “bar-code” (stratosphere) sign. Even a small amount of air obscures the beam so sliding is not seen handout lung article

Sea-shore vs bar-codeSea-shore = sliding present = lung against the chest wall. Bar-code = no sliding = pneumothorax is likely (also adhesions or no breathing).

ItemContent
What B-lines areVertical comet-tail reverberation artifacts that start at the pleural line, reach the bottom of the image without fading, move with lung sliding and erase the A-lines lung article

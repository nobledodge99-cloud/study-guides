2. Case 1: Complete Blood Count (CBC)

Patient42-year-old woman with progressive fatigue, exertional dyspnea, craving ice (pagophagia), heavy menses 7–8 days with clots; no GI bleeding or weight loss; pale conjunctivae, mild tachycardia, no hepatosplenomegaly or jaundice.
ParameterPatientReferenceInterpretation
Hemoglobin9.2 g/dL12.0–16.0Low
Hematocrit28%36–46Low
MCV68 fL80–100Low (microcytic)
MCH22 pg27–33Low (hypochromic)
RDW18.5%11.5–14.5High (anisocytosis)
Reticulocytes0.4%0.5–2.0Low/normal
Platelets450 ×10³/µL150–400High (reactive)
WBC6.04.0–11.0Normal
InterpretationMicrocytic, hypochromic anemia with a high RDW = iron deficiency. Always think chronic blood loss (most commonly gastrointestinal or gynecologic). Platelets may be reactively high. In contrast, anemia of chronic disease tends to have a normal RDW.

Indications and how to read a CBC

  • Indications: evaluate anemia or fatigue; assess for infection or inflammation; monitor bone marrow function (for example chemotherapy).
  • Step 1: hemoglobin and hematocrit for anemia. Step 2: the indices (MCV, MCH) to classify microcytic, normocytic or macrocytic. Step 3: the RDW for variability in cell size.
  • Leukocytosis does not always equal infection: consider stress or steroid use.

Microcytic anemia differential: iron deficiency (high RDW), thalassemia (normal RDW, normal to high red cell count), anemia of chronic disease (normal RDW) beyond source.

3. Case 2: Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)

Patient68-year-old woman with hypertension and heart failure on lisinopril and furosemide; a week of poor appetite and polyuria; BP 102/66, pulse 92, dry mucous membranes, orthostatic symptoms.
ParameterPatientReferenceInterpretation
Sodium130 mEq/L135–145Low (mild hyponatremia)
Potassium3.2 mEq/L3.5–5.0Low (mild hypokalemia)
Chloride97 mEq/L98–106Slightly low
Bicarbonate25 mEq/L22–28Normal
BUN14 mg/dL7–20Normal
Creatinine0.9 mg/dL0.6–1.3Normal
Glucose92 mg/dL70–100Normal
Calcium9.1 mg/dL8.5–10.5Normal
InterpretationMild hyponatremia and hypokalemia (the slide says hypokalemia: K 3.2 is low) from diuretic use, GI losses or excess free water. Renal function and acid–base status are normal (normal BUN, creatinine, bicarbonate). Treatment: correct the volume loss and adjust the loop diuretic.
  • Indications: evaluate renal function and electrolytes; monitor IV fluids or diuretics; evaluate altered mental status or dehydration.
  • Na = water balance/hydration; K = cardiac stability; BUN/Cr = kidney; bicarbonate = acid–base.
  • BUN:creatinine >20:1 suggests prerenal azotemia; low CO₂ (bicarbonate) = metabolic acidosis.

4. Case 3: Liver Function Tests (LFTs)

Patient58-year-old woman with 2 weeks of progressive jaundice, pruritus and dark urine; no pain or fever; history of cholelithiasis; scleral icterus, scratch marks, mild RUQ tenderness, no hepatomegaly.
ParameterPatientReferenceInterpretation
ALP480 U/L44–147Markedly elevated
GGT220 U/L9–48Elevated (supports hepatic/biliary origin)
ALT78 U/L7–56Mildly elevated
AST65 U/L10–40Mildly elevated
Total bilirubin4.2 mg/dL0.1–1.2Elevated
Direct (conjugated) bilirubin3.5 mg/dL<0.3Predominantly conjugated
Albumin3.8 g/dL3.5–5.0Normal
PT12.5 s11–13.5Normal
InterpretationHigh ALP and GGT with conjugated hyperbilirubinemia = cholestatic pattern, suggesting bile duct obstruction (gallstones or tumor). Normal albumin and PT show normal hepatic synthetic function, since the liver makes both.
  • Indications: jaundice and RUQ pain; monitor hepatotoxic medications; assess alcohol injury.
  • AST and ALT = hepatocellular injury. ALP and GGT = cholestasis. Bilirubin fractions (direct vs indirect) tell you whether the cause is hepatocellular, cholestatic or hemolytic.
  • AST:ALT >2:1 suggests alcohol injury.

5. Case 4: Urinalysis (UA)

Patient32-year-old woman, 3 days of dysuria, frequency and suprapubic discomfort; no flank pain, fever or discharge; vitals normal; mild suprapubic tenderness, no costovertebral angle tenderness.
ParameterResultNormalInterpretation
ColorCloudy, yellowClear, yellowCloudy suggests WBCs/bacteria
Specific gravity1.0201.005–1.030Normal (hydration)
pH6.04.5–8.0Normal
ProteinTraceNegativeMild; inflammation
Glucose / ketonesNegativeNegativeNormal
BloodSmall (+)NegativeInfection or irritation
Leukocyte esterasePositive (+++)NegativeWBCs, infection
NitritePositiveNegativeGram-negative bacteria (E. coli)

InterpretationCloudy urine + positive leukocyte esterase + positive nitrite + pyuria = urinary tract infection (most likely E. coli): “highly predictive of infection.”
  • Indications: dysuria or hematuria; evaluate renal disease; monitor diabetes.
  • Read three elements: dipstick (leukocyte esterase, nitrites), microscopy (WBCs, bacteria), and color/clarity.
  • Other dipstick clues: specific gravity = hydration; pH can change with certain bacteria; protein = kidney function; glucose and ketones = diabetes; blood = infection, stones or tumor.
  • Casts localize disease: for example red cell casts = glomerular disease.

6. Case 5: Coagulation Panel (PT/INR, aPTT)

Patient56-year-old man with chronic alcohol use, fatigue, easy bruising, abdominal swelling; scleral icterus, palmar erythema, spider angiomas, ascites (shifting dullness); no hematemesis or melena.
ParameterPatientReferenceInterpretation
PT19.8 s11–13.5Prolonged
INR1.80.8–1.2Elevated: decreased synthesis of clotting factors
aPTT42 s25–35Prolonged: reduced intrinsic factors
Platelets95 ×10³/µL150–400Low: splenic sequestration / hypersplenism
Fibrinogen130 mg/dL200–400Low: decreased hepatic production
D-dimerSlightly elevated<0.5 µg/mLCan be elevated in chronic liver disease
Factor V35%70–150Low: marker of hepatic synthetic dysfunction
InterpretationThis pattern reflects decreased hepatic synthesis of clotting factors, characteristic of advanced liver disease. Prolonged PT/INR = reduced synthesis of vitamin K–dependent factors; thrombocytopenia often from splenic sequestration. “This combination of an abnormal PT and low platelets should always prompt you to think about hepatic failure.”
  • Indications: pre-op or bleeding evaluation; monitor anticoagulation; assess liver function.
  • PT/INR = extrinsic pathway, monitors warfarin. aPTT = intrinsic pathway, monitors heparin. Both prolonged = DIC or liver disease.

7. Case 6: Lipid Panel

Patient47-year-old man at a routine physical, no CVD; father had an MI at 58; sedentary, fast food; non-smoker; BMI 29, BP 132/84.
ParameterPatientReferenceInterpretation
Total cholesterol225<200Mildly elevated
LDL145<130 (optimal <100)Borderline high
HDL42≥40 men, ≥50 womenLow-normal
Triglycerides180<150Mildly elevated
Non-HDL183<160Mildly elevated
Total cholesterol / HDL5.4<5.0Slightly elevated risk
InterpretationMild mixed hyperlipidemia with a family history of early MI but no prior events. At this stage, lifestyle modification (weight loss, diet, activity) is the cornerstone; treatment decisions depend on ASCVD risk and current guidelines.
  • Indications: assess cardiovascular risk; monitor statins; evaluate pancreatitis risk.
  • LDL drives atherosclerosis; HDL is protective; triglycerides >500 raise pancreatitis risk. Fasting improves accuracy.

InterpretationHigh TSH + low free T4 = primary hypothyroidism; anti-TPO antibodies confirm Hashimoto thyroiditis. The mirror image, low TSH + high T4, is hyperthyroidism (the recording slips and says “hypothyroidism” twice).

9. Case 8: Cardiac Enzymes (Troponin, CK-MB)

Patient59-year-old man with substernal chest pain for 1 hour while shoveling snow, radiating to the left arm and jaw, with dyspnea and diaphoresis; hypertension, hyperlipidemia, type 2 diabetes, smoker; BP 148/92, HR 104, SpO₂ 95%; ECG: ST elevation in II, III, aVF (inferior MI).
TimeTroponin I (ng/mL)CK-MB (ng/mL)Total CK (U/L)Meaning
0 h0.021.5110Normal baseline
3 h0.458.2260Initial rise
6 h3.825.0680Significant: diagnostic of MI
12 h7.230.5820Peak
24 h6.820.0600Troponin stays high; CK-MB declining
48 h4.18.0300Declining, resolving infarct
Interpretation and tipsThis pattern confirms acute myocardial infarction. Troponin is the most specific and stays elevated for 7–10 days. CK-MB rises and falls faster, so it is useful for detecting reinfarction. Always interpret alongside the symptoms and the ECG (a normal initial troponin does not exclude MI: repeat serially) "+BS+".

Indications: evaluate chest pain; diagnose MI; assess cardiac injury.

InterpretationLow pH with high PaCO₂ and normal bicarbonate = acute respiratory acidosis. CO₂ retention drives the acidosis; increasing ventilation corrects it: raise tidal volume and/or respiratory rate (both were raised here).

  • Step 1: pH (acidosis or alkalosis). Step 2: CO₂ and bicarbonate (respiratory vs metabolic). Step 3: compensation or a mixed disorder.

11. Case 10: CRP and ESR

Patient71-year-old woman, 3 months of diffuse aching and morning stiffness in the shoulders, neck and hips, trouble combing hair and rising from a chair, no true weakness; 5 lb unintentional weight loss; no headache, jaw claudication or visual symptoms; shoulder tenderness and reduced active range of motion with preserved strength; no synovitis.
ParameterPatientReferenceInterpretation
CRP48 mg/L<10Significantly elevated
ESR72 mm/hr<30Markedly elevated
Hemoglobin11.8 g/dL12–16Mild normocytic anemia
WBC8.24.0–11.0Normal
Platelets410150–400Mild reactive thrombocytosis
InterpretationWith the history, this is highly suggestive of polymyalgia rheumatica, which responds nicely to low-dose steroids. (The absence of headache, jaw claudication and visual symptoms makes giant cell arteritis less likely.) "+BS+"
  • Indications: assess inflammation or infection; monitor autoimmune disease and treatment; evaluate post-operative recovery.
  • Both are non-specific but sensitive markers of systemic inflammation. CRP rises and falls faster; ESR stays elevated longer. ESR also rises with anemia, pregnancy and age. Always interpret in clinical context.

12. Common Pitfalls and Summary

Common student pitfalls (slide 34)

  1. Ordering labs without an indication.
  2. Ignoring trends over time.
  3. Misinterpreting “normal” without context.
His exampleA patient with a baseline hematocrit of 52 whose hematocrit is now 45: within normal limits, but a significant change for this patient that may indicate GI blood loss and needs workup.

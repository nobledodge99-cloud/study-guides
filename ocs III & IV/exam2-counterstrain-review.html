1. What a Viscerosomatic Reflex Is and the Ground Rules

How this guide was builtSource is a single handout (a chart, no transcript), so red marks the handout’s own “key principles” and “clinical pearls”, not lecturer emphasis. Where the handout and standard osteopathic texts differ, I flag it and tag the extra content beyond source. Check the levels against your lecturer’s slides before relying on a single number.

Definition

A viscerosomatic reflex occurs when visceral afferent input facilitates specific spinal segments (primarily sympathetic T1–L2), producing palpable somatic dysfunction in the paraspinal tissues, often with referred pain or autonomic effects in the matching dermatome or myotome. Palpate for TART: Tissue texture change, Asymmetry, Restricted motion, Tenderness.

Key principles (handout)

PrincipleTake-home
Where to palpateSympathetic levels T1–L2 are the main sites for diagnosis and treatment with osteopathic manipulative treatment (OMT): rib raising, paraspinal inhibition, counterstrain
SidednessLeft: heart, stomach, spleen. Right: liver, gallbladder, appendix, duodenum (often)
±1 segmentLiterature varies by about one segment; treat the most facilitated or most tender segment
ParasympatheticVagus (via occiput-atlas-axis, OA/AA/C2) gives upper cervical findings; pelvic splanchnics S2–S4 give sacral or pelvic findings
Embryologic memory aidT1–T4 head, heart, lungs; T5–T9 foregut; T10–T11 midgut; T12–L2 hindgut plus genitourinary and pelvis
Counterstrain vocabulary used throughoutF = flexion, E = extension, SARA = sidebend away, rotate away, SART = sidebend away, rotate toward, STAR = sidebend toward, rotate away. Point families: PC posterior cervical, AC anterior cervical, AT anterior thoracic, PT posterior thoracic, AL anterior lumbar, PL posterior lumbar. TP = transverse process.
Two different toolsThe reflex chart says which segments a sick organ facilitates. The counterstrain points are tender points you can treat. The handout links them, but the “organ” column for counterstrain points is a teaching correlation, not proof that the organ caused the point beyond source.

EsophagusT2–T8VariableVagus (OA/AA/C2)AT2–AT7 or matching posterior TPs

Clinical read

  • Left T2–T4 tenderness in a chest-pain patient is a heart-reflex pattern; it does not replace the cardiac work-up (the handout itself says to correlate with history, exam and testing).
  • Upper cervical findings (OA/AA/C2) point to vagal input, which is why vagal-mediated organs share that tag.
Exam trapHeart is a left-sided reflex, lungs are usually ipsilateral. Do not carry “left” to the lungs.

Rule of thumbPosterior points: extension. Anterior points: flexion. Fine-tune with sidebend and rotation until tenderness drops about 70% (standard counterstrain technique beyond source).

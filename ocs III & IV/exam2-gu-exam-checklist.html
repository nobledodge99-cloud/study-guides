1. Overview: How to Use This Guide

What this coversFour Clinical Skills USA documents: the Order of the Male Urogenital Exam and its checklist (March 2020), and the Order of the Female Gynecological Exam and the Breast and Pelvic Exam checklist (2016/2019). For a competency exam the order, the patient-centered wording and the safety cues matter as much as the findings, so each step below shows what you do, what you note, and what you say.

The two orders at a glance

Male urogenital exam
1. Pubic and groin regions
2. Hernias
3. Scrotum (sac, testes, spermatic cord, vas deferens)
4. Penis
5. Rectum and prostate
6. Breast (as needed, based on history)
Female gynecological exam
I. Lymph nodes (sitting)
II. Breasts: inspect sitting; palpate axillae and breasts supine
III. Pelvic, external: groin and suprapubic, vulva, perineum and anus
IV. Pelvic, internal: speculum inspection; Pap test
V. Pelvic, bimanual: cervix, uterus, ovaries; recto-vaginal as needed
Rules that apply to every step
  • Explain before and during each part; check in with the patient (“how are you doing?”) and watch for nonverbal signs of pain or anxiety.
  • Reassure that there should be no pain, but they must report any pain or tenderness. Say “pressure,” not “discomfort.”
  • Do not hand the patient the tissue box (contaminated); give tissues.
  • Record the findings for every segment.

3. Male Exam: Scrotum and Penis (steps 3–4)

Step 3: Scrotum

Patient educationBegin by asking if the patient regularly does a testicular self-awareness assessment. Regardless of the answer, explain why and how to do one, and explain each step as you perform it.
ActionNote
Inspect the scrotum, stretching and opening the folds of skinAbnormal size or symmetry; skin irritation or discoloration; sebaceous cysts; tenderness; inflammation; ulcers; edema; other lesions
Palpate each testis and epididymis individually: capture the testis with the fingers and thumb spread around it; feel all sides. Tell the patient that one testicle is normally larger and hangs lower (usually the right). Use only enough pressure to move the testicle between the fingers and thumbSize, shape, consistency, nodules, tenderness
Palpate each spermatic cord / vas deferens: pinch the loose skin above each testicle and pull it laterally to identify the cordDilation (possible varicocele, “bag of worms”); edema; tenderness
If there is an enlargement or mass: trans-illumination (explain first). Auscultation may identify bowel sounds from a hernia
Record findings for the scrotal sac, testes, epididymis, cord and vas

Step 4: Penis

ActionNote
Inspect all surfaces of the shaft and glans, rotating the penis and stretching the folds of prepuce and shaft skinSkin irritation, ulcers, scars, nodules, chancres, warts, other lesions
Inspect the glans: ask the uncircumcised patient to pull back the prepuce himselfExcess smegma; irritation; ulcers; nodules; chancres; warts; lesions
Palpate the glans and shaft proximal to distal, twice (ventral/dorsal, then lateral), trying to move any discharge toward the meatusMasses (possible Peyronie’s disease); induration; contour (Peyronie’s); tenderness; discharge
If discharge is suspected: milk from the base toward the meatus with continuous pressure on the ventral side using the index fingerAbnormal discharge; collect a specimen if warranted
Record findings for the penis

4. Male Exam: Rectum, Prostate, Optional Breast Exam and Conclusion (steps 5–6)

Step 5: Rectum and prostate

Before the rectal exam
  • Explain the purpose and procedure; he may feel “pressure,” not pain; he may feel an urge to move his bowels or urinate (unlikely). Ask him to void first.
  • Educate: prostate cancer risks and symptoms; regular prostate exams for most men over 50, or over 40 in high-risk categories; the checklist teaches “1 in 5 men in the US will be diagnosed.”
  • Do the digital rectal exam (DRE) together with the prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood test; ideally draw the PSA before the DRE, because prostate stimulation can raise the PSA.
  • Change to a fresh set of gloves.
ActionNote
Position: standing-leaning or lateral, for full finger insertion
Forewarn that you will place your hands on his hips, then slide them to separate the buttocks
Inspect the sacrococcygeal area, perineum and posterior scrotumPilonidal cysts, masses, inflammation, eruption, excoriations
Palpate the perineum (external pressure on the prostate) as requiredFirmness, tenderness
Inspect the anus and perianal areaBleeding, warts, fissures or fistulas, external hemorrhoids, rectal prolapse, other lesions
DRE with the 3-step insertion: forewarn before contact (the lubricated finger is “cold”). (1) index finger at a 45-degree angle against the anus; (2) wait three seconds for the sphincter to relax; ask for a deep breath; (3) on exhalation roll the finger in slowly and follow the anterior wall toward the umbilicus
Locate the prostate: ask him to “bear down” (the prostate lifts); sweep lightly over the topSize, shape/symmetry, external nodularity
Palpate the prostate firmlyFirmness, internal nodularity, tenderness
Palpate the rectum: 360-degree sweep; ask him to tighten around the fingerMasses (internal hemorrhoids, polyps); sphincter tone; stool character, fecal impaction; tenderness
After removal: keep the finger out of sight; give tissues, NOT the tissue box
Before removing the glove: describe adherent fecal material for the note; apply to a guaiac card if warranted
Record findings for rectum and prostate

Step 6 (optional): Breasts

Perform a standard breast exam if he is high risk for breast cancer (family history and other risk factors). Educate: male breast cancer is “not just a woman’s disease; men die at a higher rate than women when it is diagnosed.” Record findings.

Conclude the exam

Thank the patient; discuss the results of every part of the exam, pertinent health issues, diagnostic impressions and health maintenance; commend participation; encourage a healthy lifestyle; invite questions; set a future exam schedule.

5. Female Exam: Lymph Nodes and Breast Exam

IntroductionDiscuss the importance of regular physical exams, mammograms and becoming familiar with one’s own body. Reassure repeatedly that there should be NO pain, and ask her to report discomfort at any time.
StepTechniqueNote
I. Inspection (sitting)Inspect both breasts in four positions: 1) arms at sides; 2) hands on hips with shoulders flexed; 3) arms raised straight above the head; 4) leaning forward with breasts hanging pendulous (view straight on and from both sides)Symmetry; lesions; masses; dimpling; “orange peel”; discharge; inverted nipple. One breast is typically larger than the other
II. Palpation, sittingPalpate under the jaw, sides of the neck, supra- and infra-clavicular areas, and the armpit. For large breasts, “sandwich” them between both hands, the upper hand sweeping downward from the upper chest toward the areolaTenderness, masses, lymphadenopathy
III. Palpation, lyingOne breast at a time, the other covered; her corresponding hand behind her head. Palpate the breast and axillary nodes with the pads (not tips) of the middle three fingers, pressing down in a continuous circular motion at 3 depths: superficial, medium, deep. Do not lift the fingers. Use the circular or vertical strips (“lawnmower”) overlapping patternTenderness, masses, lymphadenopathy. Express the nipple only if she has complained of or presents with discharge (note any discharge)
Method to avoidThe “spokes” method is not recommended: it is hard to palpate in a continuous flow without lifting the fingers off the breast.
Order of the sheet vs the checklistThe order-of-exam sheet lists the lymph node survey (cervical, tonsillar, submandibular, submental, supra- and infra-clavicular, axillary) and breast inspection sitting, then axillae and breasts supine; it lists 3 inspection positions, while the breast checklist lists 4 (adding the hands-on-hips position). Know both, and perform the four-position version.

6. Female Pelvic Exam: Introduction, Positioning and External Exam (steps I–III)

Step I: Introduction

  • Discuss the importance of regular physical exams, Pap tests, and knowing one’s own body.
  • Tell her there should be no pain, but to report discomfort. Say she may feel “pressure,” NOT “discomfort.”
  • Avoid threatening or suggestive language and medical jargon (for example, not “palpation”).

Step II: Positioning

DetailWording
Lithotomy: supine, legs bent, feet in “footrests”“Footrests,” not “stirrups”; help her place her heels in them (preferably padded)
Table tilted up or flat per her preference; pillow(s) available
Ask her to slide her bottom to the end of the tableLeave the table extender extended until she is positioned
Instruct: relax the abdomen and legs, “roll your knees out to the side”

Step III: External inspection and palpation

ActionNote
Inspect the vulva and surrounding areas: mons pubis, perineum, inguinal and perianal areas; sift through pubic hair if neededAmount and distribution of pubic hair; excoriations, skin lesions, parasites
Palpate the groin and mons pubisLymphadenopathy, masses, tenderness
Inspect the external vulva and perineumDischarge, bleeding, color, lesions, prolapse, sexually transmitted diseases (STDs), trauma
Inspect the anusDischarge, bleeding, hemorrhoids, fissures, fistulas, lesions, prolapse, STDs, trauma
Separate the labia and inspect the labia minora, clitoris, urethral meatus, vaginal opening (introitus)Discharge, bleeding, color, lesions, prolapse, STDs, trauma

7. Female Pelvic Exam: Speculum, Bimanual and Recto-vaginal Exams (steps IV–VII)

Step IV: Speculum

Demonstrate the speculum to the patient first; use the correct size.

#Action
1Hold the speculum at an oblique 45-degree angle. Separate the labia with the index and middle finger of the opposite hand, pressing downward on the posterior introitus against the perineal body. Introduce the speculum away from the urethral meatus, tip pointing toward the posterior fornix
2Insert the blades gently and slowly along the posterior vaginal wall (pelvic floor), pointing downward while depressing the perineal body. Rotate the speculum at full insertion until you meet resistance and the handle is vertical
3aOpen slowly, exposing the cervix
3bLock it open (tighten the set screw or hold the thumb tight against the lock). Inspect the vagina: color, discharge, bleeding, masses, lesions, atrophy, cystocele, rectocele. Inspect the cervix: color, position, shape, discharge, bleeding, lesions
3cPerform the Pap smear as needed
3dPull the speculum back slightly away from the cervix before closing; unlock and let it close. Practice the “3 Rs”: Retract, Release, Remove

Step V: Bimanual exam

ActionNote
Gently insert the index and middle fingers (gloved, lubricated) into the vagina
Palpate the cervixLocation, consistency, mobility, tenderness (cervical motion)
Palpate the uterine body between the vaginal hand and the abdominal handLocation/contour, size, mobility, tenderness
Palpate the adnexa: try to feel the ovaries with the pads (not tips) of the external fingers, “scooping” downward toward the pubic tubercle. Frequently non-palpablePalpability, enlargement, masses/nodularity, tenderness

Step VI: Recto-vaginal exam (if required)

  • Alert her: “pressure” during a brief (about 30 seconds) insertion, no pain; she may feel an urge to move her bowels (unlikely).
  • Lubricate the middle finger; tell her she will feel something cold on her “bottom.”
3-step insertionDetail
1Middle finger against the anus at a 45-degree angle (NOT straight in)
2Allow the sphincter to relax at least 3 seconds (“one one-thousand, two one-thousand, three one-thousand”); never force
3Insert the middle finger slowly into the rectum while simultaneously inserting the index finger of the same hand into the vagina
  • Palpate the septum between the two fingers: masses, nodules, fibroids, tenderness.
  • Push the uterine body upward with both fingers toward the lower abdomen and feel the uterine body and ovaries externally with the pads, scooping toward the pubic tubercle.
  • Remove slowly, examine the adherent material on the glove, do a Hemoccult test if needed, report findings in the note.

Step VII: Concluding

Give tissues (NOT the tissue box) so she can privately wipe away lubricant and fluids; assist as needed; report findings and follow-up; thank her; encourage regular comprehensive exams, breast and pelvic exams, Pap smears and mammograms.

1. Overview: Recognize the Common Abnormal Findings

What the lecture sets out to doThree stated objectives: describe the normal anatomy of the female genitourinary (GU) system and breast, recognize common abnormal findings, and summarize diagnostic techniques. The closing slide adds that several of these abnormalities look alike but have vastly different outcomes if not completely evaluated, so the comparisons below matter.

5. Vaginal Discharge (slides 22–27)

Physiologic

White, yellow or clear; quality and quantity vary through the cycle and over time; normal pH 3.8–4.4.

Pathologic causes (slide list)

Candida (yeast), Trichomonas, Chlamydia, Gonorrhea (GC), Bacterial vaginosis, Foreign body.

The three common causes compared

“Strawberry cervix” of trichomonas (slide 25)
Quick separatorsItch + cottage cheese + normal pH = candida. Frothy yellow-green + strawberry cervix = trichomonas. Fishy + clue cells + positive KOH, no itch = BV. Slide 27 is the textbook comparison table (BV, trichomoniasis, vulvovaginal candidiasis) reproduced above.

6. Ulcers: Herpes and Syphilis (slides 28–32)

Herpes simplex virus (HSV)Syphilis
CauseHSV-1 = oral; HSV-2 = genitalTreponema pallidum beyond slides
LesionVesicles/ulcers; first episode usually very painfulChancre: solitary, painless, nontender ulcer with a clean, firm base
EpidemiologyHSV-1: 50–80% of American adults; HSV-2: 1 in 6 people aged 14–49Decreased 90% from 1990 to 2000, now increasing
TestsViral culture; swab for PCR; blood PCR or antibody testingSerologic: VDRL (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory), RPR (rapid plasma reagin). Treponemal-specific: FTA-ABS (fluorescent treponemal antibody absorption), TP-PA (T. pallidum particle agglutination)
Painful vs painlessHerpes ulcers hurt; the syphilitic chancre does not. The deck’s syphilis slides list the first-line tests as two pairs: the serologic screens (VDRL, RPR) and the treponemal-specific confirmations (FTA-ABS, TP-PA).

Who30–60% of women aged 30–50Peak ages 15–352–10% of all women; often bilateralWithin a milk duct; solitary or multiple

Inverted nipple (slide 53)
Cyclic pain vs painless lumpCyclic, painful, bilateral lumpiness = fibrocystic. A painless, smooth, rubbery lump in a woman 15–35 = fibroadenoma. Bloody discharge from one duct = intraductal papilloma. A nipple that suddenly inverts = think malignancy.

The look-alike lessonTender, warm, feverish, breast-feeding = mastitis (antibiotics). Hard, fixed, nontender lump with a retracted nipple or peau d’orange = cancer until proven otherwise. A red breast that does not clear with antibiotics needs further evaluation beyond slides

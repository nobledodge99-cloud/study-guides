How to use this Every disease you've covered fits in one of these four buckets. Each bucket starts with its own basic mechanism + high-yield labs/buzzwords — know that cold, and most of the specific diseases underneath become "exceptions/flavors" of that one idea rather than isolated facts.
Bucket 1

Obstructive Lung Disease

MechanismCan fill the lungs fine but can't empty them — airway narrowing / collapse / mucus / loss of recoil traps air distally.
High yieldFEV1/FVC < 0.7 (FVC preserved, FEV1 ↓↓). Buzzwords: "pink puffer" (emphysema) vs "blue bloater" (chronic bronchitis).

Chronic Bronchitis

Mucus-hypersecretion problem — clinical diagnosis, not histologic

Chronic BronchitisProductive cough ≥3mo x 2yr. Goblet cell hyperplasia/hypertrophy, ↑Reid index, ciliary damage. Blue bloater — cyanosis, common infections, common cor pulmonale.

Emphysema (+ AAT deficiency)

Tissue-destruction problem — irreversible airspace enlargement, no fibrosis, loss of elastic recoil → dynamic airway collapse on exhale. "Pink puffer," rare cor pulmonale. GOLD stage I–IV by FEV1% predicted (all stages FEV1/FVC<70%). CXR: hyperinflation, flattened diaphragms, ↑retrosternal space, bullae — but can be completely normal in mild-moderate disease.

CentriacinarSmoking. Resp. bronchioles only. Upper lobes.
PanacinarAAT deficiency. Entire acinus. Lower lobes/bases.
Paraseptal (distal)Pleural surface → bullae → young males, spontaneous pneumothorax.
IrregularAdjacent to scarring; variable distribution.
α1-Antitrypsin DeficiencyPiZZ genotype. Panacinar/lower-lobe emphysema + cirrhosis (misfolded protein trapped in hepatocytes). Only setting with pure emphysema, no bronchitis, if non-smoker.

Asthma

Episodic, reversible bronchoconstriction from hyper-reactive airways — eosinophil-rich in both forms

Atopic AsthmaIgE/TH2-mediated (IL-4/IL-13→IgE+mucin, IL-5→eosinophils). FHx of allergy common. Allergens/foods.
Non-atopic AsthmaNo IgE sensitization. Viral illness, pollutants, cold, exercise, aspirin (↓prostaglandin).
Pathology (both)Curschmann spirals (mucus plugs), Charcot-Leyden crystals (eosinophil MBP), smooth muscle hyperplasia, sub-basement-membrane fibrosis. Labs: peripheral/sputum eosinophilia + ↑IgE (vs. COPD's neutrophil-predominant pattern).
Status AsthmaticusSevere exacerbation — breathe in, can't breathe out → life-threatening hyperinflation.
Samter's Triad (AERD)Asthma + ASA/NSAID sensitivity + nasal polyps. NSAID shunts arachidonic acid → leukotriene pathway → severe bronchospasm.

Bronchiectasis & Cystic Fibrosis

Obstruction + infection cycle → permanent airway damage

BronchiectasisPermanent dilation of bronchi/bronchioles. Fetid sputum, hemoptysis. Causes: CF, Kartagener/PCD, severe infection, obstruction. Complications: cor pulmonale, amyloidosis. HRCT: no bronchial tapering, bronchi visible in peripheral 1cm, lumen diameter > adjacent artery ("signet ring"). Lobe → likely cause: upper lobe = CF, lower lobe = aspiration, RML/lingula = MAI, central = ABPA.
Cystic FibrosisCFTR loss → ↑ENaC → dehydrated viscous secretions (airway/pancreas/gut); sweat duct = opposite (↓Cl⁻/Na⁺ reabsorption) → salty sweat test. Meconium ileus, Pseudomonas pneumonia, male infertility, biliary cirrhosis. ΔF508 (~70% of alleles) = Class II mutation — misfolded CFTR never reaches the cell surface. Sweat test uses pilocarpine iontophoresis to stimulate sweat for Cl⁻ measurement.
Bucket 2

Restrictive Lung Disease

MechanismScarring (usually) impairs filling and emptying equally — fibrosis is the shared histologic theme (exception: PAP = filling, not scarring).
High yield↓FEV1 and ↓FVC → normal ratio. Key mediator TGF-β. Gross: cobblestone pleura, honeycomb change. Complication: pulm HTN → cor pulmonale.

Fibrosing / Idiopathic Interstitial Pneumonias

The single fact that explains the whole group: UIP = different-age lesions; NSIP = same-age lesions

UIP / IPFM>F, >50y, smoking-assoc. Temporal heterogeneity — fibroblastic foci (in septa) + chronic fibrosis side by side. Subpleural onset. Telomerase/MUC5B/surfactant gene mutations. Worse prognosis. DLCO low; CXR bibasilar honeycomb/reticulonodular. Clubbing — IPF is the ILD most classically tied to it.
NSIPNon-smoking females, 6th decade. Autoimmune (esp. RA). Lesions all same age. Better prognosis than UIP. Imaging: bibasilar ground-glass or reticular changes.
COP (old: BOOP)No identifiable cause. Masson bodies — plugs within airspaces (vs. UIP's septal foci). Looks like pneumonia but fails multiple antibiotic courses; patchy, migratory infiltrates — the clinical tell.

Collagen Vascular Disease-Associated

Autoimmune disease → serositis — pleuritis/effusion common to all three

Rheumatoid ArthritisPleuritis, pneumonitis/fibrosis, rheumatoid nodules. Caplan syndrome = RA nodules + coal exposure. Necrobiotic nodules (upper zones); pleural effusion with very low glucose — mimics empyema.
Systemic SclerosisPleuritis + NSIP pattern. SCL-70 antibody; CREST syndrome; arterial intimal thickening → severe pulm HTN; ↑bronchogenic CA risk.
SLEPleuritis/effusions, mild-severe pneumonitis.
Mixed Connective Tissue DiseaseAnti-RNP antibody. Lung involvement in 75% of patients.

Pneumoconioses

Inorganic/organic particle exposure — ask occupation/hobbies. Cancer risk differs: coal = none, silica & asbestos = increased

Coal Worker's Pneumoconiosis"Coal macule" (dust-laden macrophages in bronchiolar walls). Anthracite most toxic; underground > surface miners. Anthracosis (pigment only) → simple (small nodules) → complicated/PMF. No ↑cancer risk. + RA = Caplan syndrome (large cavitary nodules).
SilicosisSandblasting/mining. Upper lobe, subpleural. Whorled silicotic nodules (hyalinized collagen). Hilar "eggshell" calcification — near-pathognomonic. Chronic (≥20y)/accelerated (3-10y)/acute (6mo-2y, alveolar filling) forms. ↑cancer + ↑TB risk.
AsbestosisMining/insulation/shipyard. Ferruginous bodies. Honeycomb change; rounded atelectasis w/ "comet tail" sign. ↑cancer (bronchogenic CA + mesothelioma). Chrysotile (curved, common, less damaging) vs amphibole (straight, drives disease). ~50x risk w/ smoking (synergistic).
Pleural PlaquesMarker of asbestos exposure (not asbestosis itself) — no ferruginous bodies, don't themselves cause disease.
BerylliumNon-caseating granulomas — sarcoid mimicker. BeLPT (beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test, blood/BAL) is what distinguishes it from sarcoid.

Granulomatous Disease

Sarcoid granulomas = crisp/well-formed (TH1); HP granulomas = ill-defined/peribronchiolar

SarcoidosisUnknown etiology, TH1-driven. Non-caseating granulomas, Schaumann/asteroid bodies. Hilar lymphadenopathy. ↑ACE, hypercalcemia (macrophage vit-D activation). Erythema nodosum (no granulomas). Mikulicz syndrome (eye+parotid). Löfgren syndrome — erythema nodosum + arthritis + bilateral hilar nodes, pathognomonic, favorable prognosis. CXR stages 0–4 (0 = normal → 4 = fibrosis/honeycomb). Erythema nodosum biopsy is not useful — no granulomas in the lesion itself. ACE ↑ in 60% but nonspecific. Neuro: CN VII, II, VIII most affected. Death mostly from cardiac arrhythmia.
Hypersensitivity PneumonitisInhaled organic antigen — Farmer's lung, pigeon/bird breeder's lung, humidifier lung, air conditioner lung, isocyanate-induced. Ill-defined peribronchiolar granulomas, ↑CD4/CD8. Acute = fever + restrictive PFTs; chronic = progressive dyspnea/↓TLC.
ByssinosisCotton/flax/hemp dust. NOT immune-mediated (contrast w/ HP) — no sensitization needed. Chest tightness worst by end of Monday ("Monday fever"), improves over the weekend.

Pulmonary Vasculitis

Lung-kidney syndromes — distinguish by nasal/sinus involvement and antibody

Granulomatosis w/ Polyangiitis (Wegener's)C-ANCA+. Upper airway/sinus disease + renal involvement + hemoptysis. Biopsy lung/nasal mucosa, NOT kidney.
Eosinophilic GPA (Churg-Strauss)Asthma + eosinophilia + necrotizing vasculitis — classically unmasked when steroids are withdrawn in an asthmatic started on a leukotriene receptor antagonist.

Eosinophilic Disease

Löffler SyndromeMigrating helminth larvae (Strongyloides). Transient, self-limited.
Allergic Bronchopulmonary AspergillosisAllergic (Type I/III) reaction to Aspergillus in pre-existing bronchiectasis. Brownish sputum. CXR: "tram-track" bronchial markings. Very high IgE.
Acute Eosinophilic PneumoniaRapid fever/dyspnea/respiratory failure. BAL eosinophilia. Histology = diffuse alveolar damage; CXR mimics ARDS.
Chronic Eosinophilic PneumoniaDiagnosis of exclusion. Middle-aged women, 50% w/ asthma. CXR: "photonegative of pulmonary edema" — peripheral infiltrates, central sparing. High ESR.

Smoking-Related Spectrum

Same exposure, increasing severity

Anthracosis (pigment only)→ Resp. bronchiolitis / RB-ILD (pigment + early fibrosis; upper lobes, centrilobular ground-glass nodules, no fibrosis)→ DIP (diffuse macrophage filling; >80% smokers)

Drug/Therapy-Related & Other

AmiodaroneMost common drug-related cause at autopsy. Risk tracks with cumulative dose (>400mg/day) + duration (months).
Radiation / Bleomycin / BusulfanDirect tissue damage → scarring.
Nitrofurantoin / MethotrexateNitrofurantoin: acute/subacute (hypersensitivity-like) or chronic (lifetime cumulative dose) forms. Methotrexate: also an RA treatment that can itself cause lung disease.
Musculoskeletal (kyphoscoliosis)Chest-wall mechanics restrict breathing with structurally normal lung parenchyma.
Pulmonary Langerhans Cell HistiocytosisYoung smoking males. One of the only non-restrictive ILDs (↑lung volume). Birbeck granules ("tennis rackets") on EM. Posterior pituitary → diabetes insipidus + lytic bone lesions. 10% present with pneumothorax.
Pulmonary Alveolar ProteinosisGM-CSF defect (autoimmune most common). Surfactant fills airspaces — granular/textured (vs. smooth edema). Gelatinous sputum. CXR: "crazy-paving" pattern (not specific). 2° causes: silicosis.
Bucket 3

Neoplasm

Mechanism4 primary carcinomas (squamous, adeno, small cell, large cell) + a neuroendocrine spectrum + mediastinal/pleural tumors. All 4 carcinomas spread lymphatically (sarcomas spread hematogenously).
High yieldCentral: squamous, small cell. Peripheral: adenocarcinoma. Virchow node = L supraclavicular. Old framework: SCLC (stop surgery, always mets) vs NSCLC.

The Four Primary Carcinomas

Squamous Cell CarcinomaCentral, cavitates. Keratin pearls, intercellular bridges. PTH-rP → hypercalcemia. TP53 (most common), FGFR1. Strong smoking assoc.
AdenocarcinomaPeripheral. Glandular formation. DIC, Trousseau syndrome. EGFR, ALK (targetable), ROS1/HER2/c-MET. Not strongly smoking-linked; most common overall, esp. young non-smokers.
Small Cell CarcinomaNeuroendocrine. Often central. High N:C ratio + nuclear molding. ACTH (Cushing), SIADH. ↑RB mutations, ↓KRAS vs NSCLC. Chromogranin/synaptophysin/CD56+. Most aggressive.
Large Cell CarcinomaDiagnosis of exclusion — no squamous/glandular/neuroendocrine features.

Precursor Lesions

Lack invasion — that's what separates them from true carcinoma

Squamous Dysplasia / CISSmoke → metaplasia → dysplasia → CIS (no basement membrane invasion).
Atypical Adenomatous HyperplasiaGlandular architecture, atypical but not carcinoma.
Adenocarcinoma In Situ (AIS)Lepidic growth along normal septa, no fibrosis, no invasion. Caution: can hide an invasive focus elsewhere.
DIPNECHPossible precursor to small cell carcinoma & carcinoid.

Neuroendocrine Spectrum

Typical carcinoid (salt-&-pepper chromatin, bland, <40y, collar-button lesion, ± MEN)→ Atypical carcinoid→ Large cell neuroendocrine CA→ Small cell CA (most aggressive)

Other Lung Tumors

HamartomaBenign but clonal. Cartilage+fat+fibrous+vessels. Classic incidental imaging finding.
LAMYoung females. TSC2 loss-of-function. Smooth muscle + melanocytic markers. Emphysema-like change — honeycombing described as lungs looking like "Swiss cheese"; chylous pleural effusions; spontaneous pneumothorax.
Inflammatory Myofibroblastic TumorChildren. Can carry ALK alteration.

Mediastinal Tumors — by Location

AnteriorThymoma (± myasthenia gravis), teratoma, lymphoma, thyroid/parathyroid, metastatic.
MiddleBronchogenic cyst, pericardial cyst, lymphoma.
PosteriorNeurogenic (schwannoma — sympathetic chain), lymphoma, metastatic, gastroenteric hernia.

Pleural Neoplasm — Mesothelioma

MesotheliomaAsbestos "until proven otherwise." BAP1 mutation. Encases the lung (not a discrete mass). Epithelial / sarcomatous / biphasic. Needs special IHC stains to confirm.
Paraneoplastic pairingsSquamous → PTH-rP (hypercalcemia). Small cell → ACTH (Cushing) + ADH (SIADH), also Lambert-Eaton. Adenocarcinoma → hypercoagulable (DIC, Trousseau). Pancoast (apical) tumor → Horner syndrome and/or SVC syndrome.
Bucket 4

Atypical / Other

MechanismDoesn't fit an obstructive/restrictive PFT pattern or a tumor — developmental anomalies, acute collapse/fluid/air processes, and primary pleural-space disease.
High yieldThink mechanically: what's blocking, compressing, filling, or leaking — the mechanism basically names the disease.

Congenital Anomalies

Pulmonary HypoplasiaNever reaches normal size (≠ atrophy, which shrinks after reaching normal size).
Foregut CystsEpithelium reveals origin: respiratory (bronchogenic), squamous (esophageal), GI-type (enteric).
Pulmonary SequestrationConnected to aorta/branches, no airway connection. Extralobar = usually asymptomatic. Intralobar = pneumonia/bronchiectasis risk.

Atelectasis

Collapse from within / without / can't-fill-at-all

ResorptiveAirway blockage (tumor, mucous plug, foreign body) — trapped air resorbed, nothing refills. Mediastinum shifts toward the collapse.
CompressivePneumothorax, hemothorax, effusion — external compression. Mediastinum shifts away from the involved lung.
ContractionScarring/ILD prevents filling in the first place.

Pulmonary Edema & ARDS

Hemodynamic Edema↑Hydrostatic (L heart failure, volume overload) or ↓oncotic (hypoalbuminemia, nephrotic syndrome, liver disease). Homogeneous, pale eosinophilic fluid. CXR: Kerley A/B/C lines (interstitial edema). Chronic → "heart failure cells" (hemosiderin-laden macrophages, Prussian blue+); gross brown induration.
ARDS / Diffuse Alveolar DamageNon-cardiogenic edema. Neutrophil-mediated injury → hyaline membranes. Abrupt hypoxemia + bilateral infiltrates, poor response to ventilation. Initial CXR can be normal in the first 24h. No cause found = acute interstitial pneumonia (Hamman-Rich) — smoking is not a risk factor; ~59y avg age; resp failure after ~3wk of illness; bimodal course (60% die within 6mo vs. survivors regain full lung function).

Pleural Effusion

Transudate (hydrothorax)CHF, volume overload — easily resorbed, least likely to scar.
ExudateInfection, neoplasm, pulmonary infarction — fibrinous/purulent = most likely to organize into adhesions.
ChylothoraxThoracic duct/lymphatic obstruction.
Hemorrhagic EffusionRaises concern for malignancy.

Pneumothorax

Tension PneumothoraxBall-and-valve mechanism — air in, can't get out. Life-threatening; needs urgent decompression.
Non-Tension / SpontaneousRuptured bleb/bulla — classically young males; or 2° to trauma.

Vascular Pathology

Thrombus (stays put) vs. embolus (travels) — Virchow's triad: endothelial injury + stasis + hypercoagulability

Pulmonary ThromboembolismSource: deep leg veins >> pelvic > upper extremity. Peripheral: wedge infarct, pleuritic pain — not very lethal itself but a warning sign for a future one. Massive/saddle (≥60% obstruction): sudden death. Lines of Zahn (platelet/fibrin + RBC bands) — more arterial. Organizing/recanalized clot → chronic thromboembolic pulm HTN.
Fat / Bone Marrow EmboliFat embolism syndrome: long bone fracture, pancreatitis → mental status change + dyspnea + petechiae. Bone marrow emboli: CPR/rib fracture, or bone infarct (sickle cell) — hematopoietic cells + adipose, no fracture needed.
Amniotic Fluid EmbolismPeripartum. Anucleate squamous cells + mucin (Alcian blue+, not Congo red). High maternal mortality, DIC.
Septic / Cholesterol / Other EmboliSeptic — from endocarditis, neutrophils around bacterial colonies. Cholesterol — plaque rupture, needle-shaped clefts. Tumor — renal cell & hepatocellular CA invade vessels directly. Paradoxical — venous embolus crosses PFO/ASD to systemic side. Air — ~100mL tolerated on right; one bubble on left can infarct.
Pulmonary Hypertension5 categories: arterial (idiopathic), left heart disease (e.g., mitral stenosis), lung disease/hypoxia, chronic thromboembolic, unclear (OSA, autoimmune). 2° pulm artery atherosclerosis. Plexiform lesion (classic, often not actually seen) → RVH → cor pulmonale.
Diffuse Pulmonary HemorrhageGoodpasture syndrome — anti-GBM vs. α3 chain of type IV collagen, lung + kidney, no nasal/sinus symptoms (vs. GPA/Wegener's). GPA also causes it. Idiopathic pulmonary hemosiderosis — hemosiderin-laden macrophages, spares the kidney (vs. Goodpasture), increased DLCO (outlier — CO taken up by interstitial Hgb). Prematurity — distinct, non-immune neonatal cause.

Imaging & Lab Quick-Reference

The same buzzword/pattern, collected across all four buckets

Imaging buzzwordsHoneycombing — UIP/IPF, asbestosis, end-stage ILD. Ground-glass — NSIP, RB-ILD, HP, acute processes. Eggshell calcification — silicosis (also sarcoid). Comet-tail sign — asbestosis (rounded atelectasis). Crazy-paving — PAP. Tram-track — ABPA. Signet ring — bronchiectasis (HRCT). Kerley B lines — interstitial edema. "Photonegative of pulmonary edema" — chronic eosinophilic PNA. "Swiss cheese" lung — LAM. Collar-button lesion — carcinoid. "Cannonball" lesions — hematogenous mets.
Lab / marker glossaryACE — sarcoid (↑60%, nonspecific). IgE — asthma, markedly higher in ABPA. Eosinophilia — asthma, ABPA, Churg-Strauss, Löffler, eosinophilic pneumonias. C-ANCA — Wegener's/GPA. Anti-GBM — Goodpasture. BeLPT — berylliosis. SCL-70 — scleroderma. Anti-RNP — MCTD. Sweat chloride — CF. D-dimer — PE (good negative predictive value only). DLCO ↑ — idiopathic pulmonary hemosiderosis (outlier); DLCO ↓ — most ILDs, sarcoid, IPF.
Lobe / segment localizationApical — TB. Right lower lobe — aspiration (gravity + wide/vertical R main bronchus). Upper lobe — CF bronchiectasis, centriacinar emphysema, silicosis. Lower lobe — aspiration bronchiectasis, panacinar emphysema, asbestosis (+subpleural), UIP/IPF (+subpleural). RML/lingula — MAI infection. Central (peribronchial) — ABPA.
Mediastinal shift (atelectasis)Resorptive/obstructive → shifts toward the collapsed lung (volume loss pulls it in). Compressive (pneumothorax/effusion) → shifts away from the involved side (added pressure pushes it out).
Cross-bucket reminders Same exposure, different buckets: silica → silicosis (restrictive) and PAP and ↑TB. Smoking → chronic bronchitis + emphysema (obstructive), DIP spectrum (restrictive), squamous/small cell CA (neoplasm). Asbestos → asbestosis (restrictive) + mesothelioma + bronchogenic CA (neoplasm), synergistic with smoking (~50x). Always get an exposure/occupation history — it does real diagnostic work across all four buckets.

Antihistamines & Drugs for Rhinitis and Allergies

When to reach for this section: the vignette centers on allergic rhinitis, urticaria, motion sickness/vomiting given as a sedating antihistamine, an anaphylaxis/allergic-reaction adjunct, peptic ulcer/GERD history with an old H2 blocker, a common-cold/URI patient asking for a decongestant, or a cough suppressant question — especially any question testing first- vs. second-generation antihistamine sedation/anticholinergic burden.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "sedation," "drowsiness," "dry mouth/blurred vision/urinary retention" (anticholinergic triad), "non-sedating," "once daily," "rebound congestion" after >3 days, "motion sickness," "hives/urticaria," "H1 receptor," "H2 receptor," "antitussive," "cough suppressant," "opioid-derived cough medicine."

Overview of Antimicrobial Agents (Core Agents)

When to reach for this section: the vignette asks you to choose/identify an antibiotic by mechanism, spectrum, or a class-defining adverse effect/contraindication — independent of a specific respiratory diagnosis. Think general antibiotic pharmacology: MOA/resistance mechanism questions, matching a drug to its ADR (red-man syndrome, Achilles tendon rupture, nephro/ototoxicity, tooth discoloration), or picking empiric coverage based on spectrum (MRSA vs. Pseudomonas vs. anaerobes).
High-yield stem words/phrases: "penicillin-binding protein," "transpeptidation/cell wall cross-linking," "beta-lactamase," "time-dependent vs. concentration-dependent killing," "30S vs. 50S ribosomal subunit," "DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV," "MRSA coverage," "Pseudomonas coverage," "red-man syndrome," "Achilles tendon rupture," "ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity," "tooth discoloration in children," "disulfiram-like reaction," "serotonin syndrome" (linezolid).

Drugs for Bacterial, Viral, and Opportunistic Respiratory Infections

When to reach for this section: the vignette is a specific respiratory infection syndrome — community-acquired, hospital-acquired, or ventilator-associated pneumonia; influenza; COVID-19; or an opportunistic fungal pneumonia — and is asking you to pick the empiric regimen or the antiviral/antifungal, not just identify a drug's isolated MOA.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "outpatient, previously healthy," "risk factors for DRSP," "nonsevere vs. severe inpatient pneumonia," "MRSA/Pseudomonas risk factors," "neuraminidase inhibitor," "within 48 hours of symptom onset" (influenza antivirals), "hospitalized COVID-19 patient not requiring high-flow oxygen" (remdesivir), "mild-to-moderate COVID-19 at high risk of progression" (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir), "broadest-spectrum antifungal but very toxic" (amphotericin B).
Core antibacterial agents for pneumonia are the SAME core drugs already tabled in the Overview of Antimicrobial Agents section above (ampicillin, amoxicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, ampicillin-sulbactam, piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftriaxone, ceftazidime, cefepime, meropenem, ertapenem, vancomycin, gentamicin, doxycycline, azithromycin, clindamycin, linezolid, lefamulin, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim) — see that section for their full detail. Empiric CAP regimen skeleton: outpatient healthy = amoxicillin OR doxycycline OR a macrolide; outpatient with comorbidities/DRSP risk = respiratory FQ OR (beta-lactam + macrolide); nonsevere inpatient = respiratory FQ OR (beta-lactam IV + macrolide); severe inpatient = beta-lactam IV + azithromycin IV OR beta-lactam IV + respiratory FQ; add vancomycin/linezolid for MRSA risk and an antipseudomonal beta-lactam + FQ/aminoglycoside for Pseudomonas risk.

Omalizumab Severe asthma with allergic sensitization — most effective with a history of repeated exacerbations, high corticosteroid requirement, and poor pulmonary function; also FDA-approved for chronic recurrent urticaria and peanut allergy Subcutaneous injection every 2–4 weeks; half-life ~26 days None absolute; requires observation for anaphylaxis at every dose US Boxed Warning for anaphylaxis (bronchospasm, hypotension, syncope, urticaria, angioedema of throat/tongue) — may occur as early as the first dose or beyond one year of therapy; headache and injection-site reactions in >10% Humanized monoclonal antibody against IgE; binds IgE and prevents binding to high-affinity FcER1/FcER2 receptors on dendritic cells, basophils, mast cells — blunts early and late allergic response "Steroid-sparing effect" — decreases both exacerbations and corticosteroid usage over time; reduces hospitalization-requiring exacerbations by 88% Proteolytic degradation (monoclonal antibody, not hepatic/renal clearance)
Benralizumab Add-on maintenance therapy for severe asthma with an eosinophilic phenotype SC every 4 weeks x3 doses, then every 8 weeks None absolute; hypersensitivity reactions have occurred Headache, pyrexia, pharyngitis Targets the IL-5 receptor on eosinophils — inhibits eosinophilic inflammation and airway hyperresponsiveness Targets the IL-5 RECEPTOR (not IL-5 itself, unlike mepolizumab/reslizumab) — distinguishing mechanistic detail among the three anti-IL-5 agents Proteolytic degradation (monoclonal antibody)
Mepolizumab Add-on maintenance therapy for severe asthma with an eosinophilic phenotype SC every 4 weeks None absolute; hypersensitivity reactions have occurred Injection-site reactions, headache, back pain, fatigue Humanized IL-5 monoclonal antibody; binds and neutralizes IL-5, the major cytokine driving eosinophil growth, differentiation, recruitment, and activation Injection-site reactions/fatigue is its distinguishing ADR profile among the anti-IL-5 agents; no boxed warning (unlike reslizumab) Proteolytic degradation (monoclonal antibody)

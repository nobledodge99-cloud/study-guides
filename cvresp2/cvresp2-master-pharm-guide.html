Antihyperlipidemics

When to reach for this section: the vignette centers on an abnormal lipid panel or long-term ASCVD risk reduction rather than an acute event — elevated LDL-C/triglycerides, HDL-C, a patient being started or titrated on lipid-lowering therapy, statin intolerance, familial hypercholesterolemia, or recurrent pancreatitis from severe hypertriglyceridemia.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "LDL-C," "lipid panel," "statin," "myalgia/myopathy" or "muscle aches" after starting a new drug, "xanthomas"/"xanthelasma," "family history of premature CAD/MI," "triglycerides >500" or ">1,000 mg/dL," "ASCVD risk," "HMG-CoA reductase," "PCSK9," "NPC1L1," "statin-intolerant," "recurrent pancreatitis," "homozygous/heterozygous FH."

Antiplatelets, Anticoagulants & Fibrinolytics

When to reach for this section: the vignette centers on clot formation, prevention, or dissolution rather than lipid management — ACS/PCI and stent thrombosis prevention, AFib or mechanical valve stroke prophylaxis, VTE (DVT/PE) treatment or prophylaxis, HIT, an acute thrombotic emergency (STEMI without timely PCI, acute ischemic stroke, massive PE), or a bleeding patient who needs anticoagulant/antiplatelet reversal.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "aPTT," "INR," "platelet count drop >50%" or "day 5–10 after starting heparin," "DAPT," "post-PCI/stent," "mechanical heart valve," "atrial fibrillation," "bridging," "CrCl" (renal cutoffs), "reversal agent"/"life-threatening bleeding," "STEMI" or "PCI unavailable/delayed," "within 4.5 hours" (stroke), "tissue factor," "P2Y12," "COX-1," "thrombin," "factor Xa."

Drugs Used to Treat Angina

When to reach for this section: the vignette centers on chest pain from myocardial ischemia rather than an evolving infarct — predictable exertional chest pain relieved by rest/nitroglycerin (stable angina), rest pain with transient ST-elevation that resolves and negative troponins (variant/Prinzmetal), angina in a woman or diabetic with a normal-appearing angiogram (microvascular), or choosing/adjusting antianginal therapy or a pharmacologic stress test.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "exertional chest pain relieved by rest," "chest pain at rest with transient ST-elevation," "troponin negative," "coronary vasospasm," "nitrate-free interval," "sublingual nitroglycerin," "PDE-5 inhibitor" + chest pain, "unable to exercise adequately" (stress testing), "coronary steal," "late sodium current," "minimal effect on heart rate or blood pressure."

Drugs Used in Heart Failure

When to reach for this section: the vignette centers on impaired cardiac pumping/filling — reduced or preserved ejection fraction with fatigue, dyspnea, or edema; a patient with acute decompensation needing rapid hemodynamic stabilization; or selecting/sequencing guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) for chronic HFrEF.
High-yield stem words/phrases: "ejection fraction," "NYHA class," "ACC/AHA stage," "JVD/peripheral edema/rales," "acute decompensated heart failure," "four-pillar therapy"/"GDMT," "euvolemic and hemodynamically stable" before starting a beta-blocker, "36-hour washout" (ARNI), "resting HR ≥70 bpm despite maximal beta-blocker," "recent hospitalization despite optimal GDMT," "Black patient with HFrEF."

Class III — Potassium Channel Blockers

Shared mechanism: block cardiac K⁺ channels (mainly phase 3 repolarization) → prolonged repolarization, ↑APD, ↑ERP — suppresses reentrant arrhythmias. Class-wide risk: proarrhythmia, especially Torsades de Pointes, worsened by hypokalemia, bradycardia, or other QT-prolonging drugs.

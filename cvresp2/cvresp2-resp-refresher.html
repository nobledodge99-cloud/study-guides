1. Lung Volumes & Capacities

TermDefinitionNormal
TVAir per normal breath~500 mL
IRVMax extra inhaled past TV~3,000 mL
ERVMax extra exhaled past TV~1,200 mL
RVAir left after max exhalation~1,300 mL
ICTV + IRV (max inhale after normal exhale)~3,500 mL
FRCRV + ERV (air left after normal exhale)~2,500 mL

Memory trick — RV can't be measured by spirometry (can't exhale it), so any capacity containing RV (FRC, TLC) needs plethysmography or gas dilution, not spirometry alone.

Disease trend: FEV1/FVC ratio separates obstructive (↓ ratio) from restrictive (normal/↑ ratio, but ↓ TLC).

2. Dead Space & Alveolar Ventilation

  • Anatomic dead space — conducting airways, no gas exchange, ~150 mL
  • Alveolar dead space — ventilated but not perfused alveoli
  • Physiologic dead space = anatomic + alveolar
VD = VT × (PaCO2 − PECO2) / PaCO2 Bohr equation for physiologic dead space
V̇A = (VT − VD) × RR Alveolar ventilation rate (mL/min)

Board trap — Pneumothorax = loss of the airtight pleural space → intrapleural pressure equalizes with atmospheric → lung collapses (elastic recoil unopposed).

5. Compliance, Laplace & Surfactant

Compliance = ΔV / ΔPEase of lung stretch. Greatest when deflated, least when fully inflated.
↑ ComplianceEmphysema (thin, distensible — floppy lung, easy to inflate, hard to recoil)
↓ ComplianceFibrosis, IRDS (stiff/thick — hard to inflate)
P = 2T / r Law of Laplace — smaller alveolus (↓r) needs more pressure to stay open at equal tension → collapses into larger neighbor without surfactant

Surfactant (Type II pneumocytes) lowers surface tension more in small alveoli than large ones, equalizing pressure and preventing collapse. Deficiency → IRDS in premature infants.

6. Airflow & Airway Resistance

R = 8ηl / πr⁴ Poiseuille — radius is the dominant variable; ½ radius → 16× resistance
  • Laminar flow in small distal airways (parallel arrangement, low velocity); turbulent in upper/large airways (Reynolds > 2000)
  • Most resistance = medium-sized bronchi, NOT the smallest airways (parallel arrangement + ↓ velocity → <20% of resistance from airways <2mm)
  • ↑ lung volume → ↑ airway caliber → ↓ resistance (traction effect)
Parasympathetic (vagal)↑ airway resistance (bronchoconstriction)
Sympathetic (NE)Inhibits constriction (bronchodilation)
Histamine, ACh, TXA2, PGF2, leukotrienesDirect smooth muscle constriction

7. Flow-Volume Loop & Equal Pressure Point

  • PFTs yield: FVC, peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR), expiratory flow rates
  • Equal pressure point (EPP) — point where intra-airway pressure = surrounding pressure; downstream (toward mouth) airway compresses (dynamic compression)
  • As lung volume falls, EPP moves closer to alveoli (less elastic tethering)
  • Obstructive disease → EPP shifts into small, cartilage-free airways → premature airway closure
Loop shapes — Inspiratory-limb plateau = variable extrathoracic obstruction; expiratory-limb plateau = variable intrathoracic; both limbs flat = fixed obstruction (e.g., tracheal stenosis).

8. Work of Breathing

W = P × ΔV
Restrictive (fibrosis)↓ compliance → ↑ elastic work → shallow, rapid breathing
Obstructive (asthma/bronchitis)↑ resistance → ↑ resistive work (esp. expiratory) → slow, deep breathing

9. Pulmonary Vascular Resistance

PVR = (mean PA pressure − LA pressure) / cardiac output
  • ↑ pulmonary arterial pressure → ↓ PVR (capillary recruitment + distension)
  • PVR vs. lung volume is U-shaped: lowest at FRC, highest at TLC (alveolar vessel compression) and high at RV (extra-alveolar vessel tortuosity/collapse)
  • Gravity zones: Zone 1 (apex) highest PVR/lowest flow; Zone 3 (base) lowest PVR/highest flow

10. Gas Laws

Boyle: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ ↑ volume → ↓ pressure (drives air flow)
Ideal gas: PV = nRT
Dalton: Px = PB × F humidified: Px = (PB − 47) × F
Henry: Cx = Px × solubility CO2 is 20× more soluble than O2 — equal exchange rates despite smaller pressure gradient
Fick's diffusion: V̇x = D·A·ΔP / Δx

DLCO changes: ↓ in emphysema (↓ surface area), fibrosis/edema (↑ membrane thickness), anemia (↓ Hb); ↑ during exercise (capillary recruitment).

11. Alveolar Gas Equation & A-a Gradient

PAO2 = (Patm − PH2O)(FiO2) − PaCO2 / 0.8 Patm=760, PH2O=47, RQ≈0.8
Normal A-a gradient ≈ (Age + 10) / 4 ↑ 5–7 for every 10% ↑ FiO2

12. V/Q Ratio & Gravity Zones

ZoneVentilationPerfusionV/Q
1 (apex)LowestLowestHighest (~3.3)
3 (base)HighestHighestLowest (~0.6)

Overall lung average V/Q ≈ 0.8 (ideal = 1). Perfusion varies more than ventilation across zones (~10× base:apex vs ~3× for ventilation) → base has the lower ratio.

Dead space (V/Q = ∞)Ventilated, not perfused — e.g., PE
Shunt (V/Q = 0)Perfused, not ventilated — right-to-left
Unique lung reflex — hypoxia causes local vasoconstriction (redirects blood away from poorly-ventilated areas) — opposite of the vasodilation seen in most other tissues.

Body plethysmography = gold standard for lung volumes (esp. heterogeneous obstruction like COPD/asthma), superior to gas dilution.

14. O2 Transport & Hemoglobin Curve

  • Hb = tetramer (α2β2), 4 O2-binding sites, cooperative binding (T-state → R-state)
  • O2 carrying capacity depends on Hb concentration, not PO2, once saturated
O2 content = (O2 binding capacity × %Sat) + dissolved O2
O2 delivery = Cardiac Output × O2 content

P50 = PO2 at 50% saturation. ↑P50 = ↓affinity (right shift); ↓P50 = ↑affinity (left shift).

Shifts RIGHT (↓ affinity, favors unloading)Shifts LEFT (↑ affinity, favors loading)
↑ Temp, ↑ CO2, ↓ pH (Bohr), ↑ 2,3-BPG↓ Temp, ↓ CO2, ↑ pH, ↓ 2,3-BPG, HbF, CO
CO poisoning — CO binds Hb 250× more avidly than O2 → ↓ available sites AND left-shifts the curve for remaining sites → O2 that IS bound is harder to unload peripherally (double hit).

Anemia: ↑ 2,3-BPG compensates with a right shift (favors unloading), but max O2 carrying capacity still ↓ (low Hb/hematocrit).

15. CO2 Transport

  • 3 forms: dissolved CO2, carbaminohemoglobin, bicarbonate (>90%, dominant form)
CO2 + H2O ⇌ H2CO3 ⇌ HCO3⁻ + H⁺ catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase, reversible
V̇A = (V̇CO2 × K) / PACO2 K = 863 mmHg; VA inversely proportional to PACO2

Hyperventilation: ↓CO2 → ↓H⁺/HCO3⁻ → ↑pH (alkalosis) → left-shifts O2-Hb curve (↑ O2 binding, harder to unload).

16. Respiration Under Stress

Exercise

  • ↑ RR and TV → ↑ minute/alveolar ventilation; V̇A rises more steeply than cardiac output
  • Blood redistributed to mid/upper zones → ↓ regional V/Q mismatch, ↑ diffusion capacity
  • Anaerobic threshold (AT): lactate production begins; VA/minute ventilation rise disproportionately to CO2 production beyond this point (buffering lactic acid)

Altitude & Acclimatization

  • ↓ atm pressure → ↓ PO2 → peripheral chemoreceptors trigger hyperventilation → respiratory alkalosis
  • Slower adaptations: ↑ 2,3-BPG (partially offsets alkalosis-driven left shift), polycythemia (EPO-driven, takes days–weeks)

Decompression Sickness

  • ↑ ambient pressure (diving) → more N2 dissolves in blood/fat; rapid ascent → N2 bubbles out of solution → tissue/vascular injury ("the bends")

17. Fetal Circulation

  • Placenta does gas exchange, not lungs — lungs are hypoxic/high-resistance in utero
  • 4 key shunts: umbilical vessels, ductus venosus, foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus — all divert blood away from the lungs
  • Fetal circulation has higher right-sided pressure (opposite of adult)
  • At birth: first breaths → ↑ O2 → pulmonary vasodilation → ↓ PVR → pressure reversal closes foramen ovale + constricts ductus arteriosus (→ ligamentum arteriosum)
  • HbF (α2γ2) has higher O2 affinity than HbA — 80% at birth, ~10% by 4 months

18. Control of Breathing

Central chemoreceptors (medulla)Most important minute-to-minute; sense CSF pH (via CO2 crossing BBB → H⁺); most potent driver overall
Peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid/aortic bodies)Sense arterial PO2 (respond strongly <60 mmHg), PCO2, pH; via CN IX/X to medulla
Dorsal respiratory group (medulla)Inspiratory center — basic rhythm, output via phrenic nerve
Ventral respiratory group (medulla)Forced/active expiration, especially in exercise
Pneumotaxic center (pons)Inhibits inspiration — fine-tunes rate
Apneustic center (pons)Prolongs inspiration
Hering-Breuer reflexLung stretch → ↓ breathing rate (prevents overinflation)
Irritant receptorsChemicals/dust/cold air → cough, bronchoconstriction
J receptorsCapillary engorgement/edema → shallow rapid breathing
Joint/muscle receptorsLimb movement → ↑ breathing rate (anticipatory, exercise)
Board trap — CO2 (via CSF pH) is the dominant driver of ventilation under normal conditions; hypoxia only becomes a strong drive once PO2 drops below ~60 mmHg (relevant in COPD "hypoxic drive" patients).

DrugMechanismAdverse Effects
Cell Wall Synthesis — Penicillins
Ampicillin & AmoxicillinAminopenicillin Bind penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) and block the transpeptidase cross-linking of peptidoglycan → bactericidal. Extended gram-negative reach beyond natural penicillin (H. influenzae, E. coli, Listeria, enterococci). Hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis; classic non-allergic rash when given to a patient with mono/EBV; diarrhea, C. difficile colitis.
Piperacillin (± tazobactam)Antipseudomonal penicillin Same PBP/transpeptidase inhibition, extended to cover Pseudomonas and other resistant gram-negatives. Tazobactam = β-lactamase inhibitor, protects the ring from bacterial β-lactamases. Hypersensitivity, interstitial nephritis, hypokalemia (Na⁺ load), platelet dysfunction/bleeding at high doses.
Cell Wall Synthesis — β-Lactam/β-Lactamase Inhibitor Combinations
Ampicillin-sulbactam Ampicillin + sulbactam (β-lactamase inhibitor) — restores activity against β-lactamase-producing organisms (many Staph, H. influenzae, anaerobes). Good empiric choice for polymicrobial wounds/bites. Same as ampicillin; diarrhea is more prominent with the combination.
Amoxicillin-clavulanate Oral amoxicillin + clavulanate; same β-lactamase inhibitor protection logic — otitis media, sinusitis, animal bites. GI upset/diarrhea is the most common complaint (clavulanate-driven); rare hepatotoxicity.
Piperacillin-tazobactam Broadest of the three — antipseudomonal penicillin + β-lactamase inhibitor; standard broad empiric gram-negative/anaerobic coverage. Same as piperacillin above.
Cell Wall Synthesis — Cephalosporins
Ceftriaxone3rd generation PBP inhibition/cell-wall synthesis block; excellent CNS penetration (meningitis), broad gram-negative + some gram-positive coverage. No Pseudomonas, no enterococcus, no MRSA. Biliary sludging/pseudolithiasis; displaces bilirubin (avoid in neonates — kernicterus risk); hypersensitivity.
Ceftazidime3rd generation Same mechanism, antipseudomonal 3rd-gen cephalosporin. Nephrotoxicity, hypersensitivity, neurotoxicity/seizures (especially with renal impairment).
Cefepime4th generation Same mechanism, broadest cephalosporin — gram-negatives + Pseudomonas + good gram-positive coverage. Neurotoxicity/encephalopathy (especially with renal impairment and under-dose adjustment), hypersensitivity.
Cell Wall Synthesis — Carbapenems
Meropenem PBP inhibition; very broad spectrum (gram +/-, anaerobes); stable to most β-lactamases (not all carbapenemases). Seizures (less than imipenem), GI upset, hypersensitivity (low but real cross-reactivity with penicillin allergy).
Ertapenem Same mechanism; notably weaker against Pseudomonas/Acinetobacter than meropenem — once-daily dosing. Similar profile; lower seizure risk than imipenem.
Cell Wall Synthesis — Glycopeptide
Vancomycin Binds the D-Ala-D-Ala terminus of peptidoglycan precursors, blocking transglycosylation/cross-linking — a different site than the β-lactams. Gram-positive only (incl. MRSA; oral form for C. difficile). Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, "red man syndrome" (histamine-mediated infusion reaction, not a true allergy), thrombophlebitis.
30S Ribosomal Subunit
GentamicinAminoglycoside Binds the 30S subunit, irreversibly blocking the initiation complex and causing misreading → bactericidal. Requires O2-dependent uptake (ineffective vs. anaerobes); synergistic with cell-wall agents for enterococcal/streptococcal endocarditis. Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity (vestibular & cochlear), neuromuscular blockade (avoid in myasthenia gravis).
DoxycyclineTetracycline Binds the 30S subunit, blocking aminoacyl-tRNA binding → bacteriostatic. Broad coverage incl. atypicals (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Rickettsia) and Lyme disease. Photosensitivity, GI upset, tooth discoloration/bone growth inhibition (contraindicated in children & pregnancy), esophagitis if taken without water.
50S Ribosomal Subunit
AzithromycinMacrolide Binds the 50S subunit (23S rRNA), blocking translocation → bacteriostatic. Strong atypical coverage. GI upset, QT prolongation/torsades, reversible ototoxicity at high dose.
ClindamycinLincosamide Binds the 50S subunit, blocking peptide-bond formation/translocation. Strong anaerobic + toxin-suppression activity (necrotizing fasciitis, toxic shock syndrome). Classic high-risk drug for C. difficile colitis, GI upset, rash.
LinezolidOxazolidinone Binds the 50S subunit (23S rRNA), blocking formation of the initiation complex. Covers resistant gram-positives (MRSA, VRE). Serotonin syndrome (weak MAO inhibitor — avoid with SSRIs/SNRIs), thrombocytopenia/myelosuppression, peripheral/optic neuropathy (prolonged use).
LefamulinPleuromutilin Binds the 50S subunit's peptidyl transferase center — a newer class, approved for community-acquired pneumonia. GI upset (diarrhea, nausea), QT prolongation, transaminase elevation; avoid in pregnancy.
DNA Gyrase / Topoisomerase IV
CiprofloxacinFluoroquinolone Inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase (gram-negative) and topoisomerase IV, blocking DNA replication. Strong gram-negative + Pseudomonas coverage. Tendinopathy/Achilles rupture, QT prolongation, CNS effects (seizures, confusion), cartilage damage in children (avoid), photosensitivity, dysglycemia.
LevofloxacinFluoroquinolone Same mechanism; broader gram-positive/atypical ("respiratory quinolone") coverage than ciprofloxacin. Same class effects as ciprofloxacin.
Folate Synthesis
TMP-SMXSulfonamide/folate inhibitor Sulfamethoxazole blocks dihydropteroate synthase (PABA→dihydrofolate); trimethoprim blocks dihydrofolate reductase (dihydrofolate→tetrahydrofolate) — sequential blockade of the same pathway, synergistic. Used for UTI, PJP treatment/prophylaxis, MRSA skin infections. Hyperkalemia, hypersensitivity (Stevens-Johnson/TEN risk), myelosuppression (megaloblastic-type anemia via folate inhibition), kernicterus in neonates (avoid late pregnancy/breastfeeding), hemolysis in G6PD deficiency.

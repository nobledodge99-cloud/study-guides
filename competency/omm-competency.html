Diagnosis primer

A complete somatic dysfunction diagnosis is "region + position of ease" (and the motion restriction is the opposite).

PART: say these as you palpate

  • Asymmetry: "The left transverse process of T4 is more posterior than the right."
  • Restriction of motion: "T4 resists right rotation and left sidebending with a firm endpoint."
  • Tissue texture: "There is paraspinal hypertonicity and a bogginess/warmth."
  • Tenderness: "The patient reports tenderness over ___."
"I am palpating the transverse processes of T4. The left transverse process is more posterior and the right is more anterior. T4 has restricted motion into right rotation and left sidebending with a firm endpoint. That is T4 FRSR (flexed, rotated right, sidebent right)." (Example from the faculty email, shown in their style.)
Type II (single segment)

Named for the position of EASE. Rotation and sidebending to the SAME side.

FRS: stuck OPEN on the side of rot/SB, restricted into extension, TP asymmetry more apparent in FLEXION.

ERS: stuck CLOSED, restricted into flexion, TP asymmetry more apparent in EXTENSION.

Type I (group, neutral)

3+ segments, SB and rotation to OPPOSITE sides, named "N SL RR" (neutral, sidebent left, rotated right).

Ribs

Inhaled rib = restricted EXHALATION (key rib = lowest of the group). Exhaled rib = restricted INHALATION (key rib = highest).

Innominate / pelvis

Anterior: ASIS inferior, PSIS superior. Posterior: ASIS superior, PSIS inferior. Superior shear: whole innominate higher. Inflare/outflare: ASIS toward/away from umbilicus.

Sacrum

"L on L" = left rotation on a left oblique axis (forward torsion). First letter = rotation, second = axis. Same letters = forward ("Up, Up, Up"); different letters = backward ("Down, Up, Down").

Counterstrain: required points & regions

Two points are randomly assigned in your OMS1 guide. Your OCS III list requires the seven below.

Steps (indirect, passive)
  1. Locate the tender point; get consent
  2. Baseline 10/10 (press hard enough to blanch the nail)
  3. Ease to monitoring pressure, keep the finger on the point until the end
  4. Find the position of comfort: ≥70% reduction (ideally 0, at most 2–3/10)
  5. Patient fully relaxed; hold 90 seconds monitoring tissue change
  6. Slowly return to neutral, keeping contact; recheck tenderness

Lines to say

Indication

"The patient has ___ with asymmetry, restricted motion and tissue texture change at ___. Osteopathic manipulation is indicated to restore motion, reduce hypertonicity and improve function."

Contraindication screen (ask out loud)

"Any recent trauma, fracture, surgery or hardware in this area? History of cancer, osteoporosis, blood thinners or bleeding disorders? Any numbness, tingling, weakness, dizziness or vision changes? Are you pregnant? Any pacemaker or other implanted device? Is any area open or infected? And do I have your permission to treat?"

Justify, specific to the technique

Factors that change your approach

"Although I could use muscle energy, the patient has significant hypertonicity that may limit participation. I would consider soft tissue/myofascial treatment first to reduce tone, then muscle energy."

Reassess: improved

"On reassessment there is improved motion in all three planes, decreased tissue texture abnormality and improved symmetry of the transverse processes."

Reassess: no change

"There is no significant change in the somatic dysfunction. I would reassess my diagnosis and consider another modality: for example, add soft tissue/MFR first, or use an indirect technique if the barrier is guarded."

Post-treatment instructions

"You may feel mild soreness for 24 to 48 hours. Stay hydrated, keep moving gently, avoid heavy lifting or strenuous activity today, and use heat or ice if sore. Tell me or call if you have new numbness, weakness, dizziness, worsening pain or any unexpected symptoms. We'll reassess at your next visit."

Special Tests for Competency (by complaint)

Test (click image to enlarge)HowPositive meansIf positive
Cough / shortness of breath
Tactile fremitus
Ulnar edge of both hands on the chest; patient says "99"Asymmetric decrease = effusion / pneumothorax / mass. Increase = pneumoniaIncreased fremitus suggests consolidation: I'd recommend a chest X-ray, CBC and pulse oximetry, then treat for pneumonia (e.g., amoxicillin or doxycycline) and follow up in 48-72 h. Decreased fremitus suggests effusion/pneumothorax: chest X-ray (consider ultrasound), and refer if confirmed.
Bronchophony
Auscultate; patient says "99"Louder, clearer than normal = consolidationPositive (louder, clearer) suggests lobar consolidation: I'd recommend a chest X-ray to confirm pneumonia, CBC, pulse oximetry, severity score (CURB-65), antibiotics, and return precautions.
Earache
Weber
Tuning fork on top of the headHears on one side only. Sensorineural: louder in the GOOD ear. Conductive: louder in the BAD earLocalizes to one ear: I'd recommend doing Rinne to sort conductive vs sensorineural, then an otoscopic exam. Conductive (cerumen, effusion, otitis): treat the cause. Sensorineural: audiology referral and urgent ENT if sudden loss.
Rinne
Fork on mastoid until it fades, then in front of the ear canalAir > bone (heard in front) = normal or sensorineural. Bone > air (not heard) = conductiveBone > air (conductive): I'd recommend removing cerumen or treating otitis media/effusion and rechecking. Air > bone with Weber to the good ear (sensorineural): audiogram and ENT referral; steroids urgently if sudden.
Palpitations / chest pain
Valsalva squat / stand (Erb's point)
Listen at Erb's point standing, squatting, standingMurmur louder squatting, softer standing = MVP / mitral. Louder on standing after squat = HOCMMurmur change suggests MVP/mitral or HOCM: I'd recommend an ECG and an echocardiogram, cardiology referral, and avoiding strenuous exertion until HOCM is excluded.
Abdominal pain
McBurney's point
Press deep at 1/3 from ASIS to umbilicus (RLQ)Tender = appendicitisTenderness suggests appendicitis: I'd recommend NPO, IV fluids, CBC, CMP, urinalysis, a CT abdomen/pelvis (ultrasound if young or pregnant), and a surgical consult.
Murphy's
Press under the right costal margin while patient inhalesStops breathing in from pain = cholecystitisPositive suggests cholecystitis: I'd recommend a RUQ ultrasound, CBC, LFTs and lipase, NPO, IV fluids, and a surgery consult for cholecystectomy.
Lloyd's (CVAT)
Tap the CVA with the ulnar edge of the fistPain = renal infection / inflammationPositive suggests pyelonephritis/renal stone: I'd recommend a urinalysis with culture, CBC and BMP, a CT or renal ultrasound if a stone or obstruction is suspected, and antibiotics or urology referral.
Liver span
Percuss up the MCL from below the umbilicus and down from the nipple line; measure between the dull bordersNormal 6–12 cm; more = hepatomegalySpan over 12 cm suggests hepatomegaly: I'd recommend LFTs, a hepatitis panel, and a RUQ ultrasound to find the cause.
Low back pain / hip
Straight leg raise
Supine; lift the straight leg to ~60° (dorsiflex foot to add stretch)Pain at 40–60° down the leg = sciatic / lumbar nerve root. Pain with more hip flexion = tight hamstringsPositive suggests lumbar radiculopathy: I'd recommend NSAIDs, activity as tolerated, PT and OMT; MRI only if red flags (weakness, cauda equina signs) or no improvement in 6 weeks.
FABER (Patrick)
Foot on opposite knee, press the knee down, other hand on opposite ASISPain = SI joint (or hip) pathologyPositive suggests SI or hip joint pathology: I'd recommend NSAIDs, PT and OMT, with hip/pelvis X-rays, and an MRI or ortho referral if it persists.
FADIR
Hip and knee 90°, adduct and internally rotate the hipPain = femoroacetabular impingementPositive suggests femoroacetabular impingement: I'd recommend hip X-rays (AP pelvis and frog-leg lateral), an MR arthrogram if symptoms persist, PT, and an ortho referral.
Thomas
Supine, knees to chest, extend one legLeg won't lie flat = iliopsoas contracturePositive suggests a tight iliopsoas: I'd recommend hip flexor stretching, PT, and OMT (muscle energy or counterstrain for the psoas).
Trendelenburg
Patient stands on one legPelvis drops on the raised side = weak gluteus medius on the standing sidePositive suggests a weak gluteus medius: I'd recommend PT with hip abductor strengthening, a gait assessment, and imaging only if hip pathology is suspected.
Knee pain
Anterior drawer
Supine, knee 90°; pull the tibia forward>2 cm forward = ACLPositive suggests an ACL injury: I'd recommend knee X-rays to rule out fracture, an MRI of the knee to better visualize the ACL, an orthopedic consult, and RICE, crutches and a brace until seen.
Posterior drawer
Supine, knee 90°; push the tibia back>2 cm back = PCLPositive suggests a PCL injury: I'd recommend knee X-rays, an MRI, an orthopedic consult, and RICE with a brace.
McMurray
Medial: ER tibia + valgus, extend. Lateral: IR tibia + varus, extendPain or click = meniscusPositive suggests a meniscus tear: I'd recommend knee X-rays, an MRI if mechanical symptoms (locking, catching), RICE, NSAIDs, PT, and an orthopedic consult.
Valgus stress (MCL)
Knee slightly flexed (~20-30°); one hand on the lateral knee, other on the medial ankle; push the knee medially and the ankle laterallyMedial opening or pain = MCL sprain/tearPositive suggests an MCL sprain/tear: I'd recommend X-rays, an MRI if severe or other ligaments are involved, a hinged brace, RICE, PT, and orthopedic follow-up.
Varus stress (LCL)
Knee slightly flexed (~20-30°); one hand on the medial knee, other on the lateral ankle; push the knee laterally and the ankle mediallyLateral opening or pain = LCL sprain/tearPositive suggests an LCL sprain/tear: I'd recommend X-rays, an MRI (LCL tears often involve the posterolateral corner), bracing, RICE, and an orthopedic consult.

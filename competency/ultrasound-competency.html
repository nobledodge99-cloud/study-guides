Station rules

The five images (B-mode)
  1. Parasternal long axis (PLAX) of the heart
  2. Apical 4-chamber (A4C) of the heart
  3. Lung sliding, anterior chest
  4. Biceps tendon, transverse plane
  5. Patellar tendon, longitudinal plane
Logistics
  • One image, 5 minutes, no prompting
  • Show a frozen image OR real-time scanning
  • Butterfly (bring it fully charged) or the Mindray
  • The 2-minute silent grading period follows, with both students silent
Image is graded on 5 things
  1. Correct machine pre-setting and transducer selection (Mindray: pick the right probe and exam preset)
  2. Correct transducer placement and orientation
  3. Correct anatomy on the screen
  4. Correct gain
  5. Correct depth

Not on this list: the manual's other competency scans (knee lateral transverse, shoulder posterior, carotid IMT, LVEF, abdominal aorta, popliteal vein, gallstones, kidney, eFAST). The course overview replaced them with these five. If your faculty email or syllabus still lists others, tell me.

Pre-flight checklist (do before the clock matters)
  • Butterfly: charged, logged in, probe connected, correct preset loaded
  • Gel, towel, patient position set, sleeves up, probe cleaned
  • Tap the probe and confirm the marker dot on the screen so you know which side is which
  • Say what you are doing: "I'm selecting the cardiac preset with the phased-array probe…"

Normal vs abnormal (from your US manual)

  • PLAX: LV diameter at the mitral valve level. LVEF = (LVD diastole − LVD systole) / LVD diastole. Normal LV wall and septum thickness < 12 mm; 14 mm wall + 13 mm septum with SOB and 20 yrs of HTN = LV hypertrophy; small pericardial effusion may coexist.
  • Lung: Normal lung shows A-lines (horizontal reverberation) and lung sliding (pleural movement with breathing). B-lines are vertical comet-tail lines. On M-mode, normal = seashore sign; pneumothorax = lung sliding gone and a barcode (stratosphere) sign.
  • Tendons: Tendinopathy appears as a thickened, hypoechoic tendon (acute inflammation/edema) compared with normal. Avoid anisotropy: keep the beam perpendicular to the tendon fibers, or a normal tendon will look falsely dark.
  • Aortic valve insufficiency (cardiac color Doppler): diastolic regurgitation jet; useful context for A4C/PLAX.
